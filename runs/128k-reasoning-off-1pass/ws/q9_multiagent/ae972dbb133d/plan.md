The problem asks for the number of ways to rotate tiles in a grid such that the resulting line segments form a continuous flow on a torus with no dead ends. This condition implies that for every edge (horizontal or vertical) between adjacent cells (including wrap-around), either both cells have a segment crossing that edge or neither does. Since Type A tiles have exactly one segment (connecting adjacent edges) and Type B tiles have exactly one segment (connecting opposite edges), the constraint effectively forces a specific relationship between the orientation of a tile and its neighbors. Specifically, if a cell has a segment exiting to the right, the cell to its right must have a segment entering from the left, and similarly for other directions. We can model this by observing that the choice of orientation for a tile is constrained by the "flow" required at its boundaries. By fixing the orientation of one tile (or analyzing the degrees of freedom), we can determine the valid configurations. The solution involves counting the number of valid global orientations based on the fixed tile types (A or B) and the grid dimensions, often reducing to a product of independent choices or a simple combinatorial formula derived from the constraints on rows and columns.
