The problem asks us to maximize the total number of units purchased given a budget $M$, where buying $k$ units of product $i$ costs $k^2 P_i$. Since the cost function $k^2 P_i$ is convex, the optimal strategy involves greedily purchasing units in the order of their marginal cost. The marginal cost of the $k$-th unit of product $i$ is $(k^2 P_i) - ((k-1)^2 P_i) = (2k-1)P_i$. We should continue buying the next cheapest available unit until the budget is exhausted. Because $N$ is large ($2 \times 10^5$) and $M$ is up to $10^{18}$, we cannot simulate unit by unit. Instead, we can sort the products by their base cost $P_i$. For a fixed number of units $k$ bought from a specific product, the marginal costs form an arithmetic progression. We can use binary search on the answer (total units) or a two-pointer/sweep approach to determine how many units to take from each product such that the sum of costs does not exceed $M$, leveraging the fact that we will fully deplete products with lower marginal costs before moving to higher ones. Specifically, since we buy units sequentially by increasing marginal cost, we can iterate through sorted products and calculate how many units we can buy from the current product given the remaining budget, stopping when we can't afford even one more unit.
