The problem asks for the sum of concatenated numbers formed by all permutations of $1$ to $N$. Since the concatenation order matters, we can calculate the contribution of each number $x \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ to the total sum by considering its position in the permutation. For a fixed number $x$ with $L_x$ digits, it appears in any of the $N$ positions with equal probability ($1/N$). If $x$ is at position $i$ (1-indexed from left), it contributes $x \times 10^{N-i}$ to the value. We sum this over all permutations. The total sum is $\sum_{x=1}^N x \cdot L_x \cdot (\text{sum of powers of 10 weighted by position})$. Specifically, for a fixed $x$, it appears $N!$ times in total across all permutations. In exactly $(N-1)!$ permutations, $x$ is at position 1, contributing $x \cdot 10^{N-1}$. In $(N-1)!$ permutations, $x$ is at position 2, contributing $x \cdot 10^{N-2}$, and so on. Thus, the total contribution of $x$ is $x \cdot (N-1)! \cdot \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} 10^j$. We can precompute the sum of powers of 10 and the sum of $x \cdot L_x$ modulo 998244353, then multiply them by $(N-1)!$.
