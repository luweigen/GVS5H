For each index i as the middle of the 5-subsequence, we need to count pairs of indices (a,b) before i and (c,d) after i such that nums[i] is the unique mode. The middle value v=nums[i] appears at least once; other selected elements must appear fewer times than v's count, and if v appears only once total then no other value can repeat. We classify by how many of the four outer elements equal v (k = 0,1,2), then count combinations where the remaining distinct values don't tie or exceed v's frequency. Using frequency maps of values on the left and right of i, we can compute these counts with combinatorics over "number of indices with each value" per side. Precompute for each i the left/right counts per value, then derive aggregate sums (total pairs, pairs sharing same value, etc.) to compute valid combinations in O(1) per i after O(n) per-side aggregation, giving overall O(n^2) or better.
