Simulating insertions directly into a list costs O(N) per operation in the worst case, giving O(N²) total, which is too slow for N up to 5×10⁵. Instead, process insertions in reverse: after all operations, element i's final position is determined by P_i and how many later-inserted elements (j > i) ended up before it. Equivalently, iterate i from N down to 1 and insert i at position P_i - 1 into a data structure supporting k-th-order queries — since all elements j > i currently occupy exactly the slots they will hold relative to i, inserting i at index P_i - 1 among "remaining free slots" works. Concretely, maintain a Fenwick tree (BIT) over positions 1..N marking free slots; for i from N down to 1, find the P_i-th free slot via binary lifting on the BIT, place A[slot] = i, and mark it used. This runs in O(N log N).
