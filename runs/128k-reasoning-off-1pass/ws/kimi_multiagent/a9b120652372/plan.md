Model each piece as a point on a line; an operation with target i moves every piece one step toward i (pieces at i stay). Key observation: the relative order of pieces is preserved, and each operation moves every piece by at most 1, so after k operations a piece starting at p can only reach squares within [p-k, p+k]; moreover the set of reachable configurations after exactly k operations can be characterized greedily. Since pieces are identical but order-preserving, the k-th piece (in order) must end at the k-th target position. A known result for this AtCoder-style problem: feasibility reduces to matching the sorted initial positions P with sorted target positions Q such that a greedy "expand/contract" condition holds, and the minimum number of operations equals the minimum k such that for every prefix/suffix the intervals [P_j - k, P_j + k] can cover Q_j with order preserved — equivalently, binary search k and check whether a valid non-decreasing assignment exists where each Q_j lies within reach and the sequence of chosen targets is feasible (each Q_j reachable from P_j within k steps with the constraint that pieces can't cross). The check can be done in O(N) per k via greedy interval matching, giving O(N log N) per test case.
