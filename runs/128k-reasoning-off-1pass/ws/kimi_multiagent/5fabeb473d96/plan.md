The distance between u and v in T(P) is the sum of A_i over edges on the u–v path, so by linearity the answer is Σ_i A_i · (number of P where edge (i, P_i) lies on the u–v path). Edge i is on the path iff exactly one of u, v is in the subtree of i, so we need, for each query (u,v) and each i, the count of parent sequences P where i separates u from v. This count depends only on the relative order of i, u, v: if i > max(u,v), both u and v choose parents among smaller indices, and symmetry gives separation probability 1/2 (pairing P by swapping the roles of u,v via an involution); if i is between u and v or equal to one of them, the count is 0 or (N-1)! respectively (edge incident to u or v is always on the path only when the other endpoint is in its subtree — careful case analysis needed). The key derivation: for i > v > u, exactly half of all (N-1)! sequences put u and v in different subtrees of i, giving count (N-1)!/2; for i ≤ v, edge i cannot separate u,v (both endpoints' ancestor chains below i stay within {1..i-1}... actually if i ≤ u < v, neither u nor v can be in subtree of i since descendants of i are > i... wait descendants of i must have index > i? No — parent of j is < j, so subtree of i consists of vertices ≥ i; thus u,v < i are never in subtree of i, and if i ∈ {u,v}, the other vertex j > i may or may not be in subtree of i). So: if i ≤ u, count 0; if i = u or i = v... u < i ≤ v: only v (if v ≥ i) can be in subtree of i; u cannot. Then edge i is on path iff v is in subtree of i, which happens with probability 1/(i-1)·... — v is in subtree of i iff following parents from v reaches i; since P_v uniform over i-1 choices and independent, count = (N-1)!/(i-1) when i ≤ v. Combining: answer(u,v) = (N-1)! · [ Σ_{u < i ≤ v} A_i/(i-1) + (1/2) Σ_{i > v} A_i ] (mod p, with division = modular inverse). Precompute prefix sums of A_i/(i-1) and of A_i to answer each query in O(1).
