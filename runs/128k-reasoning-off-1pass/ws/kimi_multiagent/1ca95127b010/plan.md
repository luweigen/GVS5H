Model the binary string as a sequence of runs of 0s and 1s. Operation A takes a boundary "0^X 1^Y" (a 0-run of length at least X immediately followed by a 1-run of length at least Y) and moves the boundary right by (X−Y)... actually it swaps adjacent blocks of X zeros and Y ones, effectively moving a "01 boundary" by shifting X zeros past Y ones; Operation B does the reverse. The key insight: each operation moves a boundary between a 0-run and 1-run by ±(X−Y) positions, or more precisely transfers min(X,Y)... — the cleanest model is that an operation moves a block of X zeros and Y ones past each other, which changes run lengths by ±(X−Y) at that boundary while preserving total counts of 0s and 1s. So a necessary condition is equal count of 1s; then characterize reachability via boundaries that can slide by multiples of g = |X−Y| (with X=Y making operations pure swaps that cancel), handling edge cases X=Y and boundary merging/splitting carefully.
