The cost per product is convex (k²·Pᵢ), so the marginal cost of the j-th unit of product i is (2j−1)·Pᵢ, which is increasing in j. The optimal strategy is greedy: always buy the cheapest next unit. Since M ≤ 10¹⁸ and Pᵢ ≥ 1, the total answer is at most ~√M ≈ 10⁹ units, so a min-heap of size N popped up to answer times is too slow in the worst case (10⁹ pops). Instead, binary search on the total number of units K: for a given K, the minimum cost to buy K units is found by distributing units so that each product i gets kᵢ units where marginal costs stay below a threshold — equivalently, for a threshold T, product i can provide up to floor((T/Pᵢ + 1)/2) units with marginal cost ≤ T. Binary search the threshold T to find how many units have marginal cost ≤ T and their total cost, then adjust for the remainder. Final algorithm: binary search on marginal-cost threshold λ; count units with marginal cost ≤ λ and sum their costs; the answer is the count plus extra units purchasable with leftover budget at marginal cost λ+... handled carefully.
