Model the game as a race to be the player who first “touches” the last untouched index. Each index i must be chosen at least once to enter S; after its first selection, further selections of i are just filler moves that consume turns without changing S. The key observation is that the game ends exactly when the N-th distinct index is chosen for the first time, so only the parity of the total number of moves up to that moment matters. Reduce the game to computing, under optimal play, who makes the final first-time pick: this becomes a normal-play game where each index i is a pile of size A_i, the first move on a pile “claims” it, and the player claiming the last pile wins. Analyze this via strategy stealing / pairing: the second player can mirror on piles with equal counts, so the outcome depends on the parity structure of A_i (count of odd A_i and total sum parity). Implement an O(N) check and print the winner.
