Each operation either does nothing, sets the interval [L,R] to 1, or sets the complement of [L,R] to 1. Since setting 1s never hurts, the final state is all-1s iff for every position j, at least one chosen operation covers j (op1 with j∈[L,R], or op2 with j∉[L,R]). Minimizing cost = minimum number of chosen operations such that every position is "hit"; this is a set-cover-like problem but with special structure: each operation covers either an interval or its complement. Key insight: with one operation we succeed iff some [L,R] = [1,N]. With two operations i,j: op1+op1 fails (uncovered outside union unless union is full), actually analyze all pairs: two op1s cover [Li,Ri]∪[Lj,Rj]; op1+op2 covers everything except (complement of Ri interval intersected with [Lj,Rj])... The clean approach: check feasibility for K=0 (only if N=0, impossible), K=1 (some operation is [1,N]), K=2 (enumerate conditions), and K=3: show 3 always suffices when feasible — e.g., pick operations to cover left part, right part, and middle. Actually known result (AtCoder ABC/ARC problem "Interval and Complement"): answer is at most 3, and 3 suffices iff there exist operations allowing coverage; specifically feasibility condition reduces to existence of i,j with Li ≤ Lj and Ri... need careful derivation. Implement checks for K=1, K=2 (O(M) or O(M log M)), else answer 3 with a constructive triple, else -1.
