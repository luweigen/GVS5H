Model the process as a shortest-path problem on the hypercube of states of A, where flipping bit i moves to a neighboring state with edge weight equal to the weighted sum (by C) of the new state. Since edge weights are nonnegative, Dijkstra from A to B over 2^N states is infeasible, so exploit structure: the cost of a flip depends only on the current total S = Σ A_k C_k, and flipping bit i changes S by ±C_i. Reformulate as a graph on the value S (range up to ΣC_i ≤ 2×10^11 — too large), so instead observe that the optimal sequence flips each index at most once (flipping twice is wasteful), reducing the problem to choosing an order of the needed flips minimizing Σ of intermediate sums. This becomes a scheduling/ordering problem solvable by a greedy exchange argument (sort by C_i) or a min-cost formulation; derive and validate the correct ordering rule, then implement in O(N log N).
