This is the classic AtCoder ABC 287 F-style / typical "shortest palindromic path" problem. We BFS over pairs of vertices (u, v) representing the two ends of a partially built palindrome: dist[u][v] = shortest palindrome length taking us from u to v. Initialize dist[i][i] = 0 (empty string) and dist[i][j] = 1 when an edge i→j exists (single character is a palindrome). Transitions: from state (u, v), find edges a→u labeled c and v→b labeled c, then dist[a][b] = min(dist[a][b], dist[u][v] + 2). Since all transition weights are 2 and initial levels are 0/1, a BFS that processes states in increasing distance order works; equivalently use a deque where same-parity expansions are handled level by level, or simply Dijkstra with weights 2. With N ≤ 100, states are N² = 10⁴ and transitions scan N² edges each, giving O(N⁴) = 10⁸ worst case, which is borderline in Python—optimize by precomputing, for each vertex v and character c, the list of in-neighbors and out-neighbors, reducing transitions to O(N² · degree) which is fine in practice (AtCoder's editorial confirms O(N⁴) with small constant passes in Python; neighbor lists make it safe).
