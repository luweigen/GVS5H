Model each ball color independently: a ball at box $i$ moves along the functional graph of permutation $P$ (red) or $Q$ (blue) when we operate on its current box. Operating on box $i$ is the only way to move balls out of $i$, and it moves every ball currently there one step along its color's permutation. Since $P,Q$ are permutations (disjoint cycles), a ball starting at $s$ reaches $X$ iff $X$ lies on its permutation's cycle containing $s$, and the number of operations on boxes of that cycle must be timed so the ball advances exactly the distance to $X$. Key observation: on each cycle, the number of times we operate on each box is nonincreasing along the direction of motion (a box must be operated at least as many times as its successor to push everything forward), and the total operations on a box equals the max requirement over balls passing through it. Compute, per color and per cycle, the minimal operations needed at each box so every ball of that color lands exactly on $X$ (or determine impossibility when a ball's cycle doesn't contain $X$), then sum over boxes.
