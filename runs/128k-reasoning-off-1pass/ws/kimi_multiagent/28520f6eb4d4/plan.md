Model visibility from point (0, h): building i is visible iff the ray from (0,h) to some point on building i is not blocked by any building j (j≠i). Since buildings are vertical segments at X_i with heights H_i, a building j blocks the view to building i (with X_j < X_i) iff the line from (0,h) to the top of building i passes below the top of building j at X_j — i.e., the "upper envelope" of lines from (0,h) over building tops. Building i is visible iff its top (X_i, H_i) lies on or above the convex-hull-like upper envelope of all previous building tops as seen from (0,h); precisely, i is visible iff the slope from (0,h) to (X_i,H_i) is strictly greater than the slope to every building j with X_j < X_i (ties mean the nearer building blocks). So i is visible iff h satisfies: (H_i - h)/X_i > max over j<i of (H_j - h)/X_j... but buildings after i don't block i. Thus each building i defines a threshold: i is invisible iff h ≤ max over j<i of the h where lines to i and j coincide: h_{ij} = (H_j X_i - H_i X_j)/(X_i - X_j). The answer is the maximum h such that some building is invisible, i.e., max over i of (max over j<i h_{ij}), but only counting pairs where j actually blocks (j is on the envelope). Compute, for each i, the maximum over j<i of h_{ij} using a convex hull trick (upper hull of points (X_j, H_j)); the blocking j for building i is the hull vertex maximizing h_{ij}, which corresponds to the tangent/last hull edge. Answer = max over i of that value; if max ≤ 0, output -1 if all visible at h=0 else 0.
