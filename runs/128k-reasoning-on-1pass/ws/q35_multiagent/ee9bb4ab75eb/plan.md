We first compute the frequency of every prefix across all words. Using these frequencies, we determine the global maximum prefix length `max_len` that appears at least `k` times, and the maximum length `max_L_gt_1` that appears more than `k` distinct times. For each word, we calculate `local_max`, the longest prefix of that word with frequency at least `k+1`. If `max_len` exceeds the current word's length, the answer is simply `max_len` since the optimal prefix cannot belong to the removed word. Otherwise, the answer is the maximum of `local_max` (prefixes surviving removal) and `max_L_gt_1` (prefixes not on the current word's path). This approach runs in linear time relative to the total number of characters.
