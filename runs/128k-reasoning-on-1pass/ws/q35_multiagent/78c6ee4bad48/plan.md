The problem asks us to minimize the sum of coordinates of $N$ pieces on a number line. We are given initial sorted coordinates $X_1 < X_2 < \dots < X_N$. The allowed operation involves choosing four consecutive pieces in sorted order (indices $i, i+1, i+2, i+3$) and reflecting the inner two pieces ($i+1, i+2$) across the midpoint of the outer two ($i, i+3$).

Analyzing the effect of this operation on the gaps between adjacent pieces ($d_j = X_{j+1} - X_j$), we find that the operation on index $i$ swaps the gaps $d_i$ and $d_{i+2}$ while keeping $d_{i+1}$ unchanged. This implies that we can arbitrarily permute the gaps at odd indices ($d_1, d_3, d_5, \dots$) among themselves, and similarly permute the gaps at even indices ($d_2, d_4, d_6, \dots$) among themselves. The first coordinate $X_1$ and the last coordinate $X_N$ remain invariant.

To minimize the sum of coordinates $\sum X_k$, we can express the sum in terms of $X_1$ and the gaps: $\sum X_k = N \cdot X_1 + \sum_{j=1}^{N-1} (N-j) d_j$. The coefficients $(N-j)$ are decreasing with $j$. To minimize the sum, we should assign the smallest gaps to the largest coefficients. Since odd-indexed gaps can only move to odd-indexed positions and even-indexed gaps to even-indexed positions, we sort the odd-indexed gaps and even-indexed gaps separately in ascending order and assign them to their respective positions (which have decreasing coefficients).
