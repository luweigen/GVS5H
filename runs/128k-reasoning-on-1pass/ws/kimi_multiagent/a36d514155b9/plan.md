Treat arrangements as unordered choices of k cells from N=m*n, since pieces are identical. By linearity, every unordered pair of cells contributes its Manhattan distance in exactly C(N-2, k-2) arrangements, so the answer is C(N-2,k-2) multiplied by the sum of distances over all unordered cell pairs. Split that pair-distance sum into row and column parts: rows contribute n^2 * sum_{r<s}(s-r), columns contribute m^2 * sum_{c<d}(d-c). Use the 1D identity sum_{i<j}(j-i)=L(L^2-1)/6 for length L, with modular inverse for division by 6. Precompute factorials up to N for the binomial coefficient and return everything mod 1e9+7 in O(N) time.
