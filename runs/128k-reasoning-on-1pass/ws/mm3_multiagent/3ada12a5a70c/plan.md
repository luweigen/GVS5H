The Manhattan distance between two points on the boundary of a square equals the minimum of the absolute difference of their perimeter coordinates and the complement of that difference (i.e., the circular distance on a circle of circumference \(C = 4 \times \text{side}\)). This transforms the problem into selecting \(k\) points on a circle to maximize the minimum circular distance between any two selected points. We binary search on the distance \(D\) and, for each \(D\), check if there exists a set of \(k\) points with pairwise circular distance at least \(D\). The check is done by trying each point as a starting point and greedily selecting the next points in clockwise order with spacing at least \(D\); if we can select \(k\) points and the wrap-around gap is also at least \(D\), then \(D\) is feasible. The maximum feasible \(D\) is the answer.
