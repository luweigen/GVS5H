The operation on the sorted pieces is equivalent to swapping the first and third gaps among four consecutive points, leaving the middle gap unchanged. Thus gaps can only be permuted by swapping indices that differ by two, which preserves the parity (odd/even) of the index. The leftmost and rightmost pieces never move, fixing \(x_1\) and \(x_N\). The total sum equals \(N x_1 + \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} (N-k) g_k\), where \(g_k\) are the gaps. To minimize the sum we want larger gaps to have smaller weights (larger \(k\)). Since swaps only allow permuting gaps within the same parity, the optimum is achieved by sorting the odd‑indexed gaps ascending and placing them at odd positions in increasing order, and doing the same for even‑indexed gaps.
