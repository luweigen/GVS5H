Write f(P) as a sum of per-element contributions: an element v with d(v) digits contributes v · 10^(total digits of everything placed after it). Fixing v, summing over all permutations gives v · G_{d(v)} where G_d = Σ_k k!·(N−1−k)!·[x^k] Π_{d'} (1+10^{d'}x)^{c_{d'}} / (1+10^d x), with c_{d'} = count of numbers in 1..N having d' digits (at most 6 distinct lengths). So build the single polynomial F(x)=Π_d (1+10^d x)^{c_d} (degree N) once, then for each length d obtain F/(1+10^d x) by exact synthetic division in O(N), form G_d, and answer = Σ_d (sum of all numbers with d digits) · G_d mod 998244353. The only nontrivial cost is the product of the ≤6 binomial-expansion polynomials (sizes ≈10, 91, 901, 9001, 90001, 10^5+1), which must be done fast — use Kronecker substitution (pack coefficients into a big integer with 80-bit / 10-byte blocks, multiply with Python big ints, unpack and reduce mod p). Verify with N=3 → 1332 (F=(1+10x)^3, F/(1+10x)=1+20x+100x², G=2+20+200=222, 6·222=1332), plus the two larger samples, and check N=1 → 1.
