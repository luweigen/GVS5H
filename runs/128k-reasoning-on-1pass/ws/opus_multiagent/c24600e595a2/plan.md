Model the process by noting each operation's cost equals the total weight of "on" elements after the flip, so the total cost equals Σ_k C_k·n_k where n_k is the number of operations after which element k is on. Argue that every position is flipped at most once, except positions with A_k=B_k=1 which may be flipped twice (temporarily turned off to reduce the weight during the whole process); positions with A_k=B_k=0 are never touched. For any fixed multiset of flips, the optimal order is all "turn-off" flips first in decreasing C, then all "turn-on" flips in increasing C (rearrangement inequality on cost = m·W0 + Σ d_j(m−j+1)). This yields the closed form cost = m·W_mid + minpairs(O) + Sum(Non) + minpairs(Non), where P = {A=1,B=0}, Q = {A=0,B=1}, R = {A=B=1}, T ⊆ R is the temporarily-toggled set, O = P∪T, Non = Q∪T, m = |P|+|Q|+2|T|, W_mid = SumR − SumT, and minpairs(X) = Σ_{unordered pairs} min. Prove that for a fixed |T| = t the optimum is the t largest elements of R (an exchange argument: swapping c→c′>c changes the pair-terms by ≤ (c′−c)(m−1) but the m·W_mid term by −(c′−c)m), then sweep t = 0..|R| with incremental updates in O(log N) each.
