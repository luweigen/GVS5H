Model each removal sequence as a non‑crossing perfect matching of the array (one element may survive when N is odd). Each matched pair contributes (larger − smaller), i.e. one "+" and one "−" sign, and one can show that *any* assignment of equal numbers of "+" and "−" inside a contiguous block of even length is realizable (repeatedly delete an adjacent differing‑sign pair), so the best value of a block B is g(B) = sum(top |B|/2) − sum(bottom |B|/2) = 2·sum(top half) − sum(B). For even N the answer is simply g(whole array). For odd N the surviving element cannot be enclosed by any pair, so it sits at an odd 1‑indexed position i and the answer is max over odd i of g(A[1..i−1]) + g(A[i+1..N]); both prefix and suffix values for all even lengths are computed incrementally in O(N log N) with a two‑heap (median‑style) structure maintaining the sum of the top ⌊len/2⌋ elements. Validate on the three samples (5, 14, 0) and stress‑test against brute force for small N.
