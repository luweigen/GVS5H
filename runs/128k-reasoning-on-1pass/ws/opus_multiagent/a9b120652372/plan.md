Model the state as (position of leftmost piece L, vector of gaps between consecutive *distinct* occupied squares); an operation at index i either translates everything (i outside [L,R]), decreases the first/last gap by 1 (i = leftmost/rightmost piece, cost‑1 reductions counted as u and v), decreases two currently adjacent gaps by 1 each (i = interior piece), or decreases one gap by 2 (i strictly inside a gap) — gaps never grow, so pieces can only merge. Since total gap shrinkage is forced to be D = (span of A) − (span of B) and each op shifts L by −1/0/+1, an accounting of op types yields **answer = min over feasible configurations of max(Δ + u, (D − Δ) + v)** where Δ = L_B − L_A, u = #left‑end unit ops, v = #right‑end unit ops, and v ≡ (u + D) mod 2. Feasibility reduces to choosing a monotone grouping of A's 1‑positions onto B's 1‑positions (block boundaries = kept gaps, needing a_j ≥ b_i) such that every *exactly matched* gap (a_j = b_i, i.e. zero reduction) has prefix‑reduction parity ≡ u; this can be tested for u = 0 and u = 1 by a linear two‑pointer greedy that always takes the earliest allowed boundary. The final answer is the min of the costs over the feasible u ∈ {0,1}, or −1 if neither works (this also covers |B ones| > |A ones|, span_B > span_A, and the k = 1 special case where only u = v = 0 is allowed). We must validate this formula with an independent brute force before shipping, and implement it with numpy/fast IO to handle sum N ≤ 10⁶ and T ≤ 2·10⁵.
