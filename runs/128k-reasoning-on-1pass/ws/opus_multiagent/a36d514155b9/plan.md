By symmetry, every unordered pair of distinct cells appears in exactly C(mn−2, k−2) valid arrangements (choose the remaining k−2 pieces among the other mn−2 cells). So the answer = C(mn−2, k−2) × S, where S is the sum of Manhattan distances over all unordered pairs of cells. Split S into row and column contributions: S = n²·T(m) + m²·T(n), where T(t) = Σ_{d=1}^{t−1} d(t−d) = (t+1)t(t−1)/6 = (t³−t)/6. Compute everything mod 1e9+7 using a precomputed factorial table up to mn (≤1e5) for the binomial, and modular inverse of 6 for T. Total work is O(mn) time, O(mn) memory, well within limits.
