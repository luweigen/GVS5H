Key insight: for a fixed subarray, an operation erases a value-interval [l,r] all of whose values are present, so the minimum number of operations equals the number of maximal blocks of consecutive integers in the *set of distinct values* of A[L..R]. Hence f(L,R) = (#distinct values in A[L..R]) − (#values v such that both v and v+1 appear in A[L..R]). Summing over all subarrays, the first term is the classic "sum of distinct counts" computed with prev-occurrence: Σ_i (i − prev[i])·(N − i + 1). The second term is Σ_{v=1}^{N-1} (number of subarrays containing at least one occurrence of v and at least one of v+1), which by inclusion–exclusion equals T − miss(v) − miss(v+1) + miss(v ∪ v+1) where T = N(N+1)/2 and miss(S) = Σ over gaps between consecutive positions of S (with sentinels 0 and N+1) of g(g+1)/2. Since Σ_v (cnt[v]+cnt[v+1]) = O(N), the whole computation is O(N log N) (or O(N) with a two-pointer merge), well within limits for N = 3·10⁵ in Python using fast I/O.
