Model the answer as: choose a target frequency `t`, then every letter must end with count exactly `t` or `0`. Since the only "transfer" operation is `c -> c+1` (cost 1, same as a delete or an insert), surplus characters of letter `i` can be pushed into a deficit of letter `i+1` for free relative to deleting them, so we scan letters `a..z` with a tiny DP whose state is "does letter `i` keep `t` copies or drop to 0". For a fixed `t`, transition cost from letter `i` (state `s`) to letter `i+1` (state `s'`) = surplus(i,s) (each surplus char costs 1 whether deleted or shifted) + max(0, deficit(i+1,s') − surplus(i,s)) (remaining deficit filled by inserts). Initialize with letter `a`'s deficit and finish by adding letter `z`'s surplus; take the min over both end states. Loop `t` from 1 to max frequency (plus the trivial "delete everything" = n baseline) — total work ≈ 26·2·2·maxFreq ≈ 2M steps, fast enough for n = 2·10⁴.
