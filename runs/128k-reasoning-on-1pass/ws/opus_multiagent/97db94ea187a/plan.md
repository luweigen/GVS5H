Every graph satisfying the condition is uniquely described by its BFS layer decomposition from vertex 1: layers $L_0=\{1\},L_1,\dots,L_k$ with edges allowed only inside a layer or between consecutive layers, and every vertex of $L_{i+1}$ having at least one neighbour in $L_i$. The parity condition is simply "sum of even-indexed layer sizes = sum of odd-indexed layer sizes = N/2". So we run a DP that builds layers vertex-by-vertex, carrying a polynomial in $x$ whose coefficient of $x^M$ counts configurations with $M$ edges: adding the $(c+1)$-st vertex of the current layer (previous layer size $a$) multiplies by $\big((1+x)^a-1\big)(1+x)^c$ and by the scalar $\mathrm{rem}\cdot (c+1)^{-1}$ (labels chosen, divided by $c!$ per layer), while "closing" a layer just moves $(p,a,c)\to(1-p,c,0)$ with no multiplication. State is $(p,\,a=\text{prev layer size},\,c=\text{current partial layer size},\,e=\#\text{vertices in even layers})$, processed in increasing order of $t=e+o$; caps $e\le N/2$, $o\le N/2$ enforce the balance condition automatically. The answer polynomial is the sum, over states at $t=N$ with $c\ge1$, of the row $e=N/2$. All arithmetic is mod the given prime $P\ge 10^8$ (so inverses of $1..15$ and binomials up to 30 exist), and the polynomial convolutions are batched with numpy int64 arrays (rows = value of $e$, columns = edge count) to stay fast.
