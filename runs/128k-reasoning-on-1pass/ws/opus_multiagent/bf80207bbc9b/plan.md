Since W ≤ 18, brute-force over all 2^W column-flip masks S. After applying S, each row r becomes r XOR S, and we may flip that row, so its optimal cost is g(r XOR S) = min(popcount(r^S), W − popcount(r^S)). Thus the total cost is f(S) = Σ_masks cnt[mask]·g(mask ^ S), which is exactly an XOR (dyadic) convolution of the row-count vector cnt with the weight vector g. Compute it in O(2^W · W) with a Walsh–Hadamard transform (vectorized with numpy, int64), then answer = min over S of f(S) (after dividing the inverse transform by 2^W). Fast input parsing is essential since H can be 2·10^5.
