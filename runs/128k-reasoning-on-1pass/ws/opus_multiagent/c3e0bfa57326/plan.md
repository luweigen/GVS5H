Model the balanced string as a forest of rooted ordered trees (each matched pair `()` is a node; nesting = parent/child). A "reverse+flip" of a balanced substring corresponds to mirroring a contiguous block of sibling subtrees, and since a block of length 1 mirrors a single subtree in place, composing operations lets us (a) mirror any subtree and (b) swap any two adjacent siblings — hence arbitrarily permute the children of every node. Therefore the reachable strings are exactly all ordered forests isomorphic to the original as an *unordered* forest, and their count is ∏ over all nodes v (plus a virtual root for the top level) of c_v!/∏_j m_{v,j}!, where c_v is the number of children and m_{v,j} are multiplicities of isomorphic child subtrees. Verify on samples: `(())()` → virtual root 2!/(1!1!) = 2; `()` → 1. Implementation: parse iteratively with a stack, canonically hash each subtree by the sorted tuple of its children's canonical ids (dictionary interning), and accumulate the multinomial factors modulo 998244353. N ≤ 5000 ⇒ ≤ 2500 nodes, so this is trivially fast; just avoid deep recursion.
