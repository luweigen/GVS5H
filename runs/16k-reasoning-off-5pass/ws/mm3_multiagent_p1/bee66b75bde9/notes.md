
## ideation
The problem asks whether we can color an N×N grid such that each row and column is a prefix of black cells followed by white cells. This is exactly the condition that the set of black cells forms a Young diagram (order ideal) anchored at the top-left. Equivalently, there must be no precolored black cell that is "down-right" of a precolored white cell. That is, for every black cell (i₁,j₁) and white cell (i₂,j₂), we cannot have i₁ ≥ i₂ and j₁ ≥ j₂. This condition is also equivalent to: for every black cell, no white cell lies in its up-left rectangle; and for every white cell, no black cell lies in its down-right rectangle. We can check both conditions efficiently by sweeping rows in both directions and using a Fenwick tree (BIT) over compressed column coordinates to query whether any conflicting cell exists in the required prefix/suffix. M ≤ 2×10⁵, so O(M log M) is fine.

The solution checks the necessary and sufficient condition: no precolored black cell is "down-right" of a precolored white cell. This is equivalent to the existence of a Young diagram containing all black cells and no white cells. We perform two sweeps: top-down to check that no white cell lies in the up-left rectangle of any black cell, and bottom-up to check that no black cell lies in the down-right rectangle of any white cell. Both sweeps use a Fenwick tree over compressed column coordinates to efficiently query for conflicts. The complexity is O(M log M).
