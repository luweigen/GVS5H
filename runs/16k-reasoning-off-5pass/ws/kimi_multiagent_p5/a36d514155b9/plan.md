The total sum of Manhattan distances over all arrangements can be decomposed into the sum over all unordered pairs of cells of (distance between the pair) × (number of arrangements containing both cells). The number of arrangements containing a fixed pair of cells is C(m·n − 2, k − 2), a constant factor. So the answer equals C(mn−2, k−2) × (sum of Manhattan distances over all unordered pairs of cells) mod (10^9+7). The pair-distance sum splits into row contributions and column contributions: for rows, sum over pairs of rows (|r1−r2| · n²) = n² · Σ_{d=1}^{m−1} d·(m−d); similarly for columns. Each 1D sum has the closed form L·(L²−1)/6 for a line of length L. Compute the binomial coefficient with modular inverse via Fermat's little theorem (mn ≤ 10^5, so factorials are cheap).
