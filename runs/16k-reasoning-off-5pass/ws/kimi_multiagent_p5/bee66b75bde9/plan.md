The condition means the black cells form a Young-diagram (Ferrers) shape anchored at the top-left: there is a non-increasing sequence of row black-counts r[1] ≥ r[2] ≥ … ≥ r[N] (equivalently non-decreasing column heights). Cell (x,y) is black iff y ≤ r[x]. A precolored B at (x,y) forces r[x] ≥ y; a precolored W at (x,y) forces r[x] < y. So each row gets a lower bound L[x] = max y over B's and an upper bound U[x] = min(y−1) over W's; we need L[x] ≤ U[x] per row, plus the existence of a non-increasing sequence r with L[x] ≤ r[x] ≤ U[x]. Feasibility of a monotone sequence between bounds is checked by propagating: the tightest feasible range for r[x] given previous rows is intersecting [L[x], U[x]] with (−∞, prev], i.e., we need max over suffix of L ≤ min over prefix of U for every split point. Compute prefix minimums of U and suffix maximums of L over the rows that have constraints (treating unconstrained rows as L=0, U=N) and verify max_suffix_L[x] ≤ min_prefix_U[x] for all x=1..N+1 boundaries. Since N is huge but M is small, compress coordinates to rows appearing in constraints, noting unconstrained stretches only contribute trivial bounds (0 and N) which never break the condition except through ordering, which coordinate compression preserves.
