The key constraint is C(N,K) ≤ 10^6, which means either K is small or K is close to N. If K ≤ N/2, we can enumerate all K-combinations directly (at most 10^6 of them), computing XORs incrementally with recursion and backtracking, tracking the maximum. If K > N/2, use the complement trick: choosing K elements is equivalent to excluding N−K elements, and since XOR of all elements XOR-ed with XOR of excluded elements gives XOR of chosen elements (because total ⊕ excluded ⊕ excluded... wait, chosen = total ⊕ excluded only when XOR of chosen ⊕ XOR of excluded = total, so chosen = total ⊕ excluded). So enumerate all (N−K)-subsets to exclude, compute chosen XOR = total_xor ⊕ excluded_xor, and take the max. This keeps enumeration within 10^6 in both cases. Each combination's XOR can be updated in O(1) via add/remove during DFS, giving overall O(C(N,K)) time.
