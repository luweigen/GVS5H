Model the binary string as the set of positions of 1s. Operation A moves a block of Y consecutive 1s left by X positions (into a block of zeros), and Operation B moves a block of X consecutive 1s right by Y positions. The key insight is to characterize reachability via "gaps": the operations preserve the cyclic structure of gaps between consecutive 1s modulo g = gcd(X, Y), and each gap can be adjusted by multiples of g as long as it stays non-negative. The answer is Yes iff the total number of 1s matches and the gap sequences (including the wrap-around gap) agree modulo g after a suitable rotation, with all intermediate gaps feasible.
