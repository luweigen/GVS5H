We need to choose final upper lengths $u_i \le U_i$ (lower lengths then forced as $d_i = H - u_i \le D_i$, i.e., $u_i \ge H - D_i$) minimizing total reduction $\sum (U_i - u_i) + \sum (D_i - d_i) = \sum(U_i+D_i) - N\cdot H$, subject to $u_i \in [L_i(H), U_i]$ and $|u_i - u_{i+1}| \le X$. For a fixed $H$, feasibility and the minimal achievable $\sum u_i$... actually cost decreases as $H$ grows, but feasibility requires $H \le U_i + D_i$ for all $i$; the optimal is the largest feasible $H$, and cost $= \sum(U_i+D_i) - N H$ provided we can pick $u_i$ in the intervals with the Lipschitz constraint. Feasibility for fixed $H$ is a classic interval-propagation (constraint tightening) check: intersect each interval with the $X$-neighborhood of neighbors via forward-backward passes. Then binary search the maximum feasible integer $H$ in $[0, \min_i(U_i+D_i)]$.
