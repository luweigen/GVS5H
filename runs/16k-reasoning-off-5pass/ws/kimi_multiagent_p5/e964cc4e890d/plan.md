The base graph is a directed path 1→2→…→2N; adding a matching edge from each white vertex to a black vertex must make the whole graph strongly connected. Since the path already gives all forward reachability, strong connectivity is equivalent to: for every cut between k and k+1 (1≤k<2N), some added edge goes "backward" across it, i.e., a white vertex at position ≤k paired with a black vertex at position >k. This is a counting problem over matchings with cut-covering constraints, solvable by a sweep/DP over positions using the balance of whites and blacks seen so far. The key is to derive a closed-form/DP that counts matchings where every prefix cut is crossed by at least one W→B edge, computable in O(N) or O(N log N) with combinatorial identities. Workers should derive the structural characterization, the counting formula, and verify against samples.
