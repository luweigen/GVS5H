The problem requires finding the largest integer $N \le A$ such that $N$ has exactly two distinct prime factors $p$ and $q$, and the exponent of each prime in the prime factorization of $N$ is even. This implies $N$ must be of the form $p^{2k} \cdot q^{2m} = (p^k \cdot q^m)^2$, meaning $N$ is a perfect square of a number with exactly two distinct prime factors. We can precompute all such numbers up to $10^{12}$ by iterating over pairs of primes $(p, q)$ and generating valid squares $p^{2k}q^{2m} \le 10^{12}$. Since the maximum value is $10^{12}$, the base $p^k q^m$ is at most $10^6$. We can generate these bases, sort them, square them, and then for each query, use binary search to find the largest valid number $\le A$.
