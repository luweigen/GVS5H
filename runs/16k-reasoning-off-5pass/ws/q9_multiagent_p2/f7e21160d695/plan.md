The problem asks to minimize the sum of bottleneck distances $f(A_i, B_i)$ by optimally permuting sequence $B$. Since $f(x, y)$ is the minimum max-edge weight on a path, it corresponds to the path in the Minimum Spanning Tree (MST) of the graph. We can sort both sequences $A$ and $B$ based on their "cost profiles" relative to the MST edges, but a more direct approach is to realize that for a fixed threshold $W$, the number of pairs $(A_i, B_i)$ with $f(A_i, B_i) \le W$ is maximized when we greedily match available $A$'s and $B$'s that are connected in the subgraph of edges with weight $\le W$. However, the optimal strategy for minimizing the sum of bottleneck distances given two sets of nodes is to sort the nodes in both sets based on their connectivity properties in the MST. Specifically, if we consider the edges of the MST in increasing order of weight, adding an edge merges two components. We can process edges from smallest to largest weight. For each edge with weight $w$ connecting components $C_u$ and $C_v$, any $A_i$ in $C_u$ and any $B_j$ in $C_v$ (or vice versa) can now be connected with cost $\le w$. To minimize the total sum, we should greedily match as many such pairs as possible with the current smallest available edge weights. This suggests a greedy approach: iterate through MST edges in increasing order of weight, maintain counts of unmatched $A$'s and $B$'s in each component, and whenever an edge merges two components, match as many $A$'s from one side with $B$'s from the other as possible, adding the edge weight to the total sum for each match.
