We model Takahashi's position as a connected region that can expand by absorbing any adjacent slime whose strength is < current_strength / X. Since strengths only increase, the condition only becomes harder over time, so we can process slimes in increasing order of their initial strength. Use a union-find (DSU) over cells, sorting all cells by S. Initially the DSU set for Takahashi contains just (P, Q) with strength S[P][Q]. Process cells in ascending S: for each cell (i, j) with strength v, if v is less than the current strength of the DSU component it belongs to divided by X, we merge it into the adjacent component(s) that have already been processed (i.e., whose root has larger strength). Because we process in increasing order, a cell can only be absorbed after all stronger adjacent cells have been considered, ensuring correctness. After merging, we update the component's total strength to sum of merged cells. Finally, the answer is the strength of the component containing (P, Q).
