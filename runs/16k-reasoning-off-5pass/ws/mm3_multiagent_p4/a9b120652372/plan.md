Each operation chooses a pivot `i` and moves all pieces one step toward `i`. This means the multiset of positions of pieces shifts so that the "center of mass" moves toward `i`, but the relative ordering of pieces is preserved (they cannot cross each other). So the sorted list of piece positions is invariant up to translation. Therefore, a necessary and sufficient condition for reachability is that the sorted positions of `1`s in `A` and `B` have the same length, and we can compute the minimum number of operations as the sum over each piece of the distance it must move, but pieces can be moved together in the same operation if their direction matches. The optimal strategy corresponds to matching pieces by sorted order and summing `|posA[k] - posB[k]|`. We also need to verify reachability by checking that the relative order is preserved (which the sorted matching ensures) and that the pieces never need to cross. Since sorted matching preserves order, the answer is `sum_k |posA[k] - posB[k]|` if counts match, else `-1`.
