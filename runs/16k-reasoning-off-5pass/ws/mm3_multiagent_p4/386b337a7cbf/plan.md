Since subsequence order is preserved and the alternating sum depends on the parity of positions in the subsequence, a DP over the original index is natural: `dp[i][p][s]` = maximum product achievable using a subsequence ending at original index `i`, whose length parity is `p` (0 = even count, 1 = odd count) and alternating sum is `s`. Values are small, so we can shift the sum index (sum range is roughly ±150·12 = ±1800). Products are bounded by `limit` (5000), so we cap at `limit+1`. For each element we either skip it or take it, updating parity and sum. The answer is the maximum product ≤ `limit` across all states with sum `k` and non‑empty (i.e., at least one element taken).
