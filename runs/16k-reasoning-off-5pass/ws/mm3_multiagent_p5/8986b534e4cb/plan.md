The line of towns with weighted edges is equivalent to choosing a path‐potential function $p(t)$ for each town, with $w_j = p(j+1) - p(j)$. Person $i$ going from $S_i$ to $T_i$ has stamina $p(\text{current}) - p(S_i)$ (or similar). Requirement: stamina is 0 at both ends and positive at every intermediate town, i.e., for all $t$ strictly between $S_i$ and $T_i$, $p(t)$ must be strictly between $p(S_i)$ and $p(T_i)$ (the lower of the two). This forces a *total order* on the towns induced by the $p$ values: along the line, $p$ must be monotone (say, weakly increasing), and for each person the intermediate towns must lie on one side of the endpoints. In fact, feasibility reduces to: there exists a permutation of towns consistent with all “betweenness” constraints of the selected people. Since all towns are on a line, each person with $|S_i - T_i| > 1$ demands that every town between $S_i$ and $T_i$ is NOT between the two endpoint values — equivalently, endpoints must be consecutive in the induced order among the towns of the segment. Aggregating over people yields forbidden pairs of towns that cannot be adjacent in the $p$ order. This is a classic “consecutive ones”/2‑SAT‑like problem solvable by checking whether the constraint graph contains a cycle. Preprocess for all people, then answer each $[L,R]$ query offline with a segment‑tree divide‑and‑conquer DSU roll‑back (or simply by binary search with prefix/suffix precomputation) to test feasibility in $O((N+M+Q)\log M)$.
