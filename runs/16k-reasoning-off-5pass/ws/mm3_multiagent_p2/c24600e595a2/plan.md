We need minimum total cost to make A equal to B, where each operation flips one bit A_i and costs the sum of A_k*C_k after the flip. The key observation: the cost of a single flip at position i is either the current sum of A*C with A_i unchanged then plus C_i (if after flip A_i becomes 1) or minus C_i (if becomes 0). By greedy strategy, we should always flip bits in order of increasing C_i among mismatched positions: flipping a 0→1 (needs to become 1) when current A-sum is low, and 1→0 (needs to become 0) when current A-sum is high. The optimal order is: for mismatches where A_i=0,B_i=1 we want to do them early (low C_i first); for mismatches where A_i=1,B_i=0 we want to do them late (high C_i first). More precisely, sort needed-to-set (0→1) by ascending C_i, and needed-to-unset (1→0) by descending C_i, then simulate flips accumulating cost: when flipping a 0→1 at cost C_i, we add (current_sum + C_i); when flipping a 1→0 at cost C_i, we add (current_sum - C_i) (which equals current_sum_without that bit). This is equivalent to the natural greedy order.
