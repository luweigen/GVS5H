We can model each operation as keeping the two outer pieces fixed and reflecting the two middle pieces across the midpoint between the outer pieces. For a fixed window of four consecutive pieces (i, i+1, i+2, i+3) in sorted order, the sum of their positions is invariant under the operation because the reflections are symmetric. Hence the total sum of all coordinates is invariant — but the problem asks for the minimum sum after any sequence of operations, which suggests we can reorder which pieces sit at which positions (since pieces are distinguishable only by current coordinate, not by index). The only invariant per quadruple is the sum of its four coordinates. The achievable final sorted order must be a permutation of the original coordinates such that every 4-consecutive window sum equals the original window sum. This forces a linear pattern: positions must be of the form A[i] = a*i + b, and the sum equals a known value. We can find the common difference `a` from the 4-window sums: let S[i] = X[i] + X[i+1] + X[i+2] + X[i+3]. Then S[i+1] - S[i] = a, so a is constant. Finally, the minimum total sum occurs when the sequence is as left-shifted as possible while maintaining distinct sorted order — equivalent to choosing `b` as small as possible such that A[i] are strictly increasing (which they are for a > 0) and match the required first coordinate A[0]. We can determine A[0] = X[0] and adjust b, or more simply compute the target sorted sequence directly and sum it.
