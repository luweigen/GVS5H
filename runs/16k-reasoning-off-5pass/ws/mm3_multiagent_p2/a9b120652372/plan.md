The operation moves all pieces one step toward a chosen square i. Equivalent viewpoint: a piece at position j can be moved arbitrarily as long as in each operation it either stays or steps toward i. The order of operations matters only in the sequence of chosen i's, not the timing of piece movement.

**Key insight (matching B to A):** Think of moving pieces so that the multiset of final occupied positions equals the target B (interpreted as a count of 1's = number of pieces). Since we have exactly k pieces (number of 1's in A) and B must have exactly k ones, otherwise answer is -1. Sort positions of pieces (indices where A_i=1) and target positions (indices where B_i=1). Each operation shrinks all gaps between current piece positions and the "center" chosen i by at most 1. The minimum number of operations to move from sorted configuration X to sorted configuration Y is the ceiling of the maximum distance any piece must travel: `max(|X[m] - Y[m]|)` for m=0..k-1. (Because in one operation, every piece can move at most one step toward the target.) So answer = max over matched pairs of |X[m] - Y[m]|. Compute sorted positions for A and B, then for each test case, if counts differ output -1, else output the max absolute difference.
