Model each person as a constraint on prefix sums of road strengths: the partial sums along their path must be strictly positive at interior towns and zero at endpoints. This translates to a system of difference constraints (inequalities of the form x_v - x_u ≥ c) on the line, where feasibility is equivalent to absence of positive cycles in the constraint graph. The key insight is that constraints decompose into "lower bound" edges (from left endpoints) and "upper bound" edges (from right endpoints), and infeasibility arises only from specific crossing pairs of people whose intervals interact badly. Precompute for each person the nearest "conflicting" person to the left and right using a stack/sweep structure, building a 2D range structure so each query [L,R] reduces to checking whether any conflicting pair lies entirely inside the range. Answer queries offline with a BIT/segment tree over right endpoints after sorting pair events.
