The key observation is that "change to next letter" is a directed operation (i→i+1), so characters only flow forward through the alphabet, while delete/insert cost 1 each. If we fix a target frequency k (each surviving character appears exactly k times), then for each letter we decide how many of its occurrences to keep, delete, or shift forward; letters shifted into position i+1 combine with that letter's own count. This yields a DP over the 26 letters for each candidate k, where the state is how many characters arrive at letter i (from letter i-1 shifts plus its own kept count) and we pay deletes/inserts to reach exactly k, with the option to shift surplus forward. Since n ≤ 2·10⁴, k ranges up to n, giving an O(26·n) or O(26·n·something small) solution. We take the minimum over all k (including k=0, i.e., delete everything, cost n).
