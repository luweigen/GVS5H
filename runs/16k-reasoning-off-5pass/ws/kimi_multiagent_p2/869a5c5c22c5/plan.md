Model the problem as finding a cyclic sequence of R red and B blue vertices where consecutive (and last-to-first) pieces occupy adjacent squares: red uses Manhattan adjacency, blue uses diagonal adjacency. Key observation: a blue move preserves (r+c) parity, while a red move flips it, so the board's two parity classes act like a bipartition with red pieces as "color flips". Analyze small cases: total 2 needs equal colors adjacent appropriately (R=2 works on a 2x2 cycle, B=2 works diagonally, R=B=1 impossible since a red and blue can never be mutually adjacent in both required directions consistently — verify by parity). For larger totals, construct explicit cycles: all-red uses a 2×k snake cycle; all-blue uses a diagonal cycle on a 2-row strip (blue moves connect (r,c) to (r±1,c±1), so a 2×k grid forms a cycle for even counts); mixed cases need at least 2 red pieces to switch parity classes, then alternate runs. Derive impossibility conditions: (R,B)=(1,1), and cases with B odd and R=0, plus R=1 with B>0 (single red can't close the parity cycle), then give constructive patterns for everything else.
