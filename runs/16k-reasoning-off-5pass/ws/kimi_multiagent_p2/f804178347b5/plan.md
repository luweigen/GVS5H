Model the repeated majority operation as a ternary tree of height N, where each internal node computes the majority of its three children and leaves are the input bits. For each node, compute the minimum number of leaf flips needed to make that node evaluate to 0 and to 1 using dynamic programming. For a leaf, the costs are 0 for its current value and 1 for the opposite value. For an internal node, the cost to make it value v is the sum of the two smallest child costs for value v, since majority requires at least two of three children to be v. The answer is the cost at the root for the opposite of its current value.
