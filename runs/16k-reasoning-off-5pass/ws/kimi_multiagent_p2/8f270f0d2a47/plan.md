We need to count numbers in [l, r] where the product of digits is divisible by the sum of digits. Since r < 10^9, numbers have at most 9 digits, so the digit sum is at most 81 and the product fits in standard integers (max 9^9). A brute-force check over up to 10^9 numbers is too slow, so we use digit DP: count beautiful numbers in [0, n] via memoized recursion over digit position, current digit sum, current digit product, and a tight flag, then answer f(r) - f(l-1). A key optimization is handling the "zero product" case: once a 0 digit appears, the product is 0, which is divisible by any positive sum, so all such numbers are beautiful. The DP state space is small (10 positions × 82 sums × manageable product values), making this easily fast enough.
