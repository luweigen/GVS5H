We need ord_M(A) = N exactly. The simplest sufficient construction: pick M = A^N - 1 with gcd(A, M) = 1 (automatic since A^N ≡ 1 mod M implies gcd(A,M)=1). Then ord_M(A) divides N; we must ensure no smaller d < N gives A^d ≡ 1 mod M. Since A^d - 1 < A^N - 1 = M for d < N, A^d - 1 cannot be a multiple of M unless A^d - 1 = 0, impossible for A ≥ 2. So (A, M) = (2, 2^N - 1) works whenever 2^N - 1 ≤ 10^18, i.e., N ≤ 59. For large N, use a different trick: take A = 10^18 (or any large base) and M = A - 1... no, that gives order 1. Instead use M | A^N - 1 structure: choose A = kN + 1 style won't work. Better: pick prime p with N | p - 1 and A a primitive root power — too complex. Simpler: use (A, M) = (N+1, N(N+1)+... ) — instead use lifting: A = 1 + N·c won't give exact order. Cleanest general construction: M = A^N - 1 requires A^N ≤ 10^18+1, so choose A = max(2, ceil(10^(18/N))) — but A^N may undershoot and order could be smaller divisor. Alternative robust approach: use A = 2, M = 2^N - 1 for N ≤ 59; for N ≥ 60, factor N and use cyclotomic/primitive-prime-divisor (Zsygmondy) ideas with M = Φ_N(A) evaluated at small A, guaranteeing exact order N when gcd conditions hold.
