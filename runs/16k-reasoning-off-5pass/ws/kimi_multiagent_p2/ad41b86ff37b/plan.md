A Snowflake Tree has a center (red), x middle vertices (blue), and y leaves per middle vertex (green); equivalently, it is a tree of diameter ≤ 4 where all non-center internal vertices have equal degree and all leaves are at distance exactly 2 from the center (with degenerate cases x=1 or y=1 giving smaller diameter). We need the largest induced connected subgraph of T that is a snowflake, minimizing deletions = N − (max kept). Since a snowflake is determined by its center c, we try each vertex as center: we must keep a connected subtree rooted at c where kept nodes are within distance 2, each kept middle vertex keeps the same number of leaf children, and we can delete arbitrary subtrees. This becomes a DP: for each root candidate, compute best kept count using child contributions, where each child subtree can be entirely deleted, kept as a middle vertex (then it contributes 1 + k of its leaf-children for the common k), or kept as a leaf. The common y across middle children makes this a "choose k maximizing sum of top values" problem per center, solvable in O(deg) per center with sorting, giving O(N log N) total. Handle degenerate cases (path-like snowflakes with x=1, or y=1 stars) carefully, possibly by also trying each edge's midpoint interpretation or treating x=1 separately.
