The Manhattan distance splits into row and column contributions, which can be computed independently and summed. For the row part: for each pair of rows (r1, r2), the contribution |r1-r2| occurs whenever two pieces occupy those rows; count arrangements by choosing 2 distinct cells in those rows (n² ways) and the remaining k-2 pieces from the other cells (C(mn-2, k-2) ways). The total row-pair weight sum over all row pairs equals n * sum_{d=1}^{m-1} d*(m-d) (each column contributes the same), giving a closed form n * m*(m²-1)/6; symmetrically for columns. Multiply by n² (or m²) and the binomial factor, all modulo 1e9+7, using modular inverse of 6. Edge cases: k=2 makes C(mn-2, 0)=1, and formulas hold for m=1 or n=1 since one dimension's sum is zero.
