Model the valid parenthesis sequence as an ordered rooted tree: each matched pair is a node whose children are the top-level pairs inside it, and the whole string is a forest under a virtual root. The "reverse" operation on a valid substring swaps parentheses while reversing order, which corresponds to reversing the order of children within the chosen node (or the top-level forest). Reachable strings are exactly those obtainable by independently reversing sibling orders at any subset of nodes, so the count is the product over nodes of (number of distinct orderings of that node's children achievable) — i.e., for each node, the number of distinct permutations of its children multiset is m! / ∏(multiplicity!), but since reversal only gives 2 orderings per node, we must check: reversal generates only identity or full reverse per node, giving 2^k unless symmetric. Actually each node's children sequence can be either kept or reversed, and distinct results count 2 unless the sequence is a palindrome of child-subtrees; answer is product over nodes of (1 or 2) depending on whether children sequence equals its reverse (comparing subtree isomorphism). Compute canonical hashes of subtrees bottom-up to test palindrome equality at each node's child list.
