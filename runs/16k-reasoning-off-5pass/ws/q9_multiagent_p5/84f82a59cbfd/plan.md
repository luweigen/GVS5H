The problem asks for the largest integer $\le A$ that has exactly two distinct prime factors $p, q$ where both $p$ and $q$ appear with an even exponent in the prime factorization. This structure implies the number is of the form $p^{2k} q^{2m} = (p^k q^m)^2$, meaning the number must be a perfect square. Let $X = p^k q^m$. Then $X^2 \le A \implies X \le \sqrt{A}$. We need to find the largest $X \le \sqrt{A}$ such that $X$ has exactly two distinct prime factors. Since $Q$ is large ($2 \times 10^5$) and $A$ up to $10^{12}$ (so $\sqrt{A} \le 10^6$), we can precompute the smallest prime factor (SPF) for all numbers up to $10^6$ using a sieve. For each query, we calculate $L = \lfloor\sqrt{A}\rfloor$. We then iterate downwards from $L$ to find the first number $X$ that has exactly two distinct prime factors. Given the density of such numbers, this search will be very fast on average.
