1. The problem asks for the sum of $f(P)$ over all $N!$ permutations of $(1, 2, \ldots, N)$.
2. $f(P)$ is formed by concatenating the decimal representations of the elements in the permutation.
3. We can compute the contribution of each digit position in the final concatenated string. However, since the lengths of the numbers vary, it's easier to think about the contribution of each number $A_i$ when it appears at a specific position in the permutation.

If a number $x$ with $d$ digits is placed at the $k$-th position (1-indexed) in the permutation, it contributes $x \cdot 10^{L_{k+1} + \dots + L_N}$ to the sum, where $L_j$ is the number of digits of the $j$-th element in the permutation. This is complex because the suffix lengths depend on the other elements.
5. Alternative approach: Consider the contribution of each number $x \in \{1, \ldots, N\}$ to the total sum. For a fixed $x$, it appears in each of the $N$ positions in exactly $(N-1)!$ permutations. When $x$ is at position $i$ (1-indexed), the numbers after it are some subset of size $N-i$ from the remaining $N-1$ numbers. The value contributed by $x$ is $x \cdot 10^{\text{sum of lengths of numbers after it}}$.
6. Let $len(x)$ be the number of digits in $x$. The total sum is $\sum_{x=1}^N x \cdot (N-1)! \cdot \mathbb{E}[10^{\text{sum of lengths of a random subset of size } N-i \text{ from } \{1,\ldots,N\}\setminus\{x\}}]$ summed over all positions $i$. This expectation is difficult.
7. Better approach: Linearity of contribution by digit position in the final string is hard due to variable lengths. Instead, consider that the total sum is $\sum_{P} f(P)$. We can rewrite $f(P) = \sum_{i=1}^N A_i \cdot 10^{S_i}$, where $S_i$ is the total number of digits in $A_{i+1}, \ldots, A_N$.
8. The total sum is $\sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{P} A_i \cdot 10^{S_i}$. For a fixed position $i$, and a fixed value $x$ at position $i$, the remaining $N-1$ numbers are permuted in the other $N-1$ positions. The suffix sum $S_i$ depends on the lengths of the numbers in positions $i+1, \ldots, N$.

20. Alternative: Group numbers by their length. There are only $\approx 6$ distinct lengths for $N \le 2 \times 10^5$ (1 to 6 digits). Let $cnt_d$ be the count of numbers with $d$ digits. The generating function is $\prod_{d=1}^6 (1 + z \cdot 10^d)^{cnt_d}$.
21. To exclude $x$ with length $L_x$, we divide by $(1 + z \cdot 10^{L_x})$. This is equivalent to multiplying by $(1 + z \cdot 10^{L_x})^{-1}$. We can precompute the full polynomial $P(z)$ and then for each length group, compute the "excluded" polynomial by dividing by $(1 + z \cdot 10^d)$.
22. Since there are only 6 groups, we can compute 6 "base" polynomials $Q_d(z) = P(z) / (1 + z \cdot 10^d)$. Then for any $x$ with length $d$, the sum of weights for subsets of size $m$ from $\{1,\ldots,N\}\setminus\{x\}$ is the coefficient of $z^m$ in $Q_d(z)$.
23. Algorithm:
    a. Count $cnt_d$ for $d=1,\ldots,6$.
    b. Compute $P(z) = \prod_{d=1}^6 (1 + z \cdot 10^d)^{cnt_d}$ using binary exponentiation and FFT/NTT.
    c. For each $d \in \{1,\ldots,6\}$, compute $Q_d(z) = P(z) \cdot (1 + z \cdot 10^d)^{-1}$. This can be done by polynomial division or by noting that $(1+z \cdot 10^d)^{-1} = \sum_{j=0}^\infty (-1)^j z^j 10^{dj}$. Since we only need up to degree $N$, we can compute this convolution.
    d. For each position $k$ from 1 to $N$, let $m = N-k$. For each length $d$, let $count_d$ be the number of elements with length $d$. The number of elements with length $d$ in the set excluding $x$ is $cnt_d - 1$ if $L_x=d$, else $cnt_d$.
    e. The sum of weights for subsets of size $m$ from $\{1,\ldots,N\}\setminus\{x\}$ is $Coeff(Q_{L_x}, m)$.
    f. The total answer is $\sum_{k=1}^N \sum_{x=1}^N x \cdot (N-1)! \cdot Coeff(Q_{L_x}, N-k)$.
    g. Rearranging: $\sum_{d=1}^6 \sum_{x: L_x=d} x \cdot (N-1)! \cdot \sum_{k=1}^N Coeff(Q_d, N-k)$.
    h. Let $S_d = \sum_{k=1}^N Coeff(Q_d, N-k) = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} Coeff(Q_d, j)$.
    i. Then the answer is $(N-1)! \cdot \sum_{d=1}^6 S_d \cdot (\sum_{x: L_x=d} x)$.
