1. Since the subsequence size is fixed at 5 and the "middle mode" must be the element at index 2 (0-indexed) of the subsequence, we iterate through each element in `nums` considering it as the potential middle element `m` of a subsequence.
2. For each candidate middle element `m` at index `i`, we need to choose 2 elements from the left part (`nums[0...i-1]`) and 2 elements from the right part (`nums[i+1...n-1]`).
3. The condition is that `m` must be the unique mode of the 5-element subsequence. This means the frequency of `m` in the subsequence must be strictly greater than the frequency of any other element in the subsequence.
4. Since `m` appears once as the middle element, we can pick 0, 1, or 2 more copies of `m` from the left and right. Let `k` be the total count of `m` in the subsequence (1 + count from left + count from right). The other 5-k elements must all have frequencies less than `k`.
5. We can precompute frequency counts for the entire array. Then for each index `i`, we consider the left and right parts separately. We iterate over possible counts of the middle element from the left (`l_cnt`) and right (`r_cnt`), where `l_cnt + r_cnt + 1 = k`.
6. For a fixed `k`, we need to count pairs of (left 2 elements, right 2 elements) such that no other element appears `>= k` times. We can use combinatorics: total ways to pick 2 from left and 2 from right minus ways where some other element violates the condition. Given constraints (n<=1000), an O(n^2) or O(n * max_freq) approach is feasible. A simpler O(n^2) approach: for each middle index, iterate over all possible values for the other elements? No, that's too slow if values are large. Instead, we can use frequency maps for left and right parts dynamically updated as we iterate the middle index.
