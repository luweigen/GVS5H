For each value v (1..N), compute its contribution to the concatenated number across all permutations. When v is placed at position i (1-indexed), the elements after it contribute a shift factor 10^{total digits of elements after position i}, and v itself is multiplied by that shift. By symmetry, each subset of elements appearing after v is equally likely; the number of permutations where a specific set of k elements follows v is k! * (N-1-k)!. Sum over all choices of which elements follow v: contribution of v = v * (N-1)! * sum over subsets S of {others} of 10^{digitsum(S)} / C(N-1, |S|)... simpler: contribution = v * sum_{S subset of others} |S|! (N-1-|S|)! 10^{d(S)}. Group by digit-length: precompute for each digit-length class c (number of elements with c digits, and sum of their digit counts), a DP/generating function over classes to compute g = sum over subsets of (subset size)! (N-1-subset size)! 10^{subset digit sum}. Then answer = sum_v v * (g with v removed). Removing v per element is expensive; instead compute total per class and adjust: for each class, compute g excluding one element of that class via a small "subtract" trick (compute DP with and without, or use the fact that classes are few — at most 6 digit lengths up to 2*10^5). With at most 6 classes, do a knapsack DP per class-exclusion (6 DPs of size N) — that's 6*N which is fine.
