Since each product has convex cost k²·P_i, the marginal cost of the j-th unit of product i is (2j−1)·P_i, which is increasing. The optimal strategy is to buy units in order of increasing marginal cost. For a candidate total K, the minimum cost is computed by taking, per product, k_i = min over j of how many marginal costs (2j−1)P_i fit under a threshold — equivalently, binary search on a marginal-cost threshold T: product i contributes k_i = floor(T / (2P_i) + 1/2) units (number of odd multipliers (2j−1) with (2j−1)P_i ≤ T). Binary search the answer K directly: check feasibility of buying K units by greedily taking the K cheapest marginal costs via a threshold, or simpler: binary search on threshold T to count units and cost, then derive max K with cost ≤ M by adjusting with the count of units at the boundary marginal price. Use 128-bit-safe arithmetic (Python ints are unbounded, so no overflow concerns).
