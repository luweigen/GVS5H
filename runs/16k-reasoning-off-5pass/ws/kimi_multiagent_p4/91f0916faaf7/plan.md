Each ratio S_i/S_{i+1} in lowest terms is p_i/q_i with p_i q_i = A_i, so each edge corresponds to choosing an ordered factorization of A_i into coprime parts. Chaining ratios forces S_i = c·(product of chosen numerators up to i-1)·(product of denominators from i onward) for a common scale c; the gcd=1 condition forces c=1 after dividing by the overall gcd, meaning valid sequences correspond to assignments where a certain global gcd is exactly 1. Since A_i ≤ 1000, each A_i has few coprime ordered factorizations (essentially assignments of its prime power factors to numerator or denominator), so we can model this as a chain/DP over primes: each prime independently contributes a path of exponents, and sequences correspond to per-prime exponent assignments. The score product factorizes over primes, so we can compute, per prime, the sum over exponent-paths of p^{sum of exponents}, with a global constraint that not all positions share a common factor — handled by subtracting the "all entries divisible by some prime" cases via the per-prime DP tracking whether the prime divides every S_i. Finally combine independent primes multiplicatively with inclusion-exclusion (Möbius-style) for the gcd=1 condition.
