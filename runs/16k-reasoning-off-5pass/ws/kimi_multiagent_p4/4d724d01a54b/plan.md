Model the process as moving each value v from its initial position pos[v] to its target position v using adjacent swaps, where swapping across boundary i costs i. Each value v must cross every boundary between v and pos[v] exactly once, so the total cost is at least the sum over all v of the boundary costs in that interval; this bound is achievable (e.g., by moving elements into place one at a time, since each required boundary crossing is unavoidable and sufficient). Thus the answer is sum over v of (sum of i for i between min(v,pos[v]) and max(v,pos[v])-1). Compute this efficiently with a difference array over boundaries: for each v, add v's contribution to the range of boundaries it crosses, then take prefix sums. Use 64-bit integers; complexity O(N).
