Write the string as alternating runs. Both operations act on a window of length X+Y consisting of a 0-run piece and a 1-run piece; characters outside the window are unconstrained, so the runs involved may extend beyond the window.

*Operation A* applies at a "01-boundary": a 0-run of length a ≥ X immediately followed by a 1-run of length b ≥ Y. It replaces
`0^a 1^b` → `0^(a-X) 1^Y 0^X 1^(b-Y)`, then deletes zero-length runs and merges:
- if a = X, the moved `1^Y` merges with the 1-run on the left (if any);
- if b = Y, the right 1-run vanishes and the new `0^X` merges with the following 0-run (if any).

*Operation B* is the mirror image at a "10-boundary": a 1-run of length b ≥ Y followed by a 0-run of length a ≥ X:
