We need shortest palindrome-labeled walks between all pairs. Model this as a BFS over pairs of vertices (u, v) representing the two ends of a partially built palindrome: a palindrome of even length 2k corresponds to reaching (j, j) from (i, i) by simultaneously extending both ends with matching letters; odd length 2k+1 corresponds to reaching (j, mid) where there's an edge mid→j... equivalently, BFS from each (i, i) and each edge (i, j) as initial states with distances 0 and 1, transitioning (u, v) → (x, y) via edges x→u and v→y with equal labels, adding 2 to the length. Since all BFS layers add cost 2 uniformly, a single multi-source BFS over the N² state graph computes all answers: dist[u][v] = shortest palindrome length from u to v. N ≤ 100 gives at most 10⁴ states and up to 26·N² transitions per state naively, so we precompute for each vertex and letter the in-neighbor lists to keep transitions efficient (worst case ~10⁸ is too high in Python; instead iterate over in-neighbors of u and out-neighbors of v grouped by letter, intersecting by letter).
