Model the repeated majority-of-3 reduction as a ternary tree of height N, where each internal node is the majority of its three children and leaves are the input bits. For each node, compute two values: the minimum flips in its subtree to make the node evaluate to 0, and to make it evaluate to 1. For a leaf, cost is 0 for its own value and 1 for the opposite. For an internal node, to force value v, at least two of its three children must be v, so take the sum of the two smallest child costs for value v. The answer at the root is the cost to force the opposite of its current value. This is O(3^N), feasible since 3^13 ≈ 1.6M.
