A Snowflake Tree has exactly three "layers" around a center: the center (red), its x neighbors (blue), and each blue vertex has exactly y leaf children (green). So in the final tree, every vertex is within distance 2 of the center, blue vertices all have equal degree y+1, and all depth-2 vertices are leaves. We choose a center c and root T at c; then we must delete vertices so that the kept subtree is exactly: c, some set S of its children (|S| = x ≥ 1), each kept child keeps exactly y of its own children (all leaves), and nothing deeper survives. For a fixed center c, the optimal choice is: for each child subtree, either drop it entirely or keep it and keep some number of its leaf-children; we need at least one kept child and all kept children must agree on a common y ≥ 1, maximizing kept vertices (equivalently minimizing deletions). Since any kept depth-2 vertex must be a leaf in the final tree, for child v with k grandchildren we may keep any y in 1..k of them (they're all leaves of T? No—grandchildren may have their own children, which must then be deleted, but the grandchildren themselves become leaves after deleting their descendants, so any grandchild can serve as a leaf). Thus for fixed c, each child v offers value (1 + y) at cost of choosing y ≤ deg_children(v), and we maximize over y the count of children with child-count ≥ y, times (1+y), plus 1 for the center. Trying all centers naively is O(N^2); we need to aggregate. Key observation: the answer's center can be assumed to be a vertex where we root; we can compute for each vertex c the multiset of child-subtree sizes (number of children of each neighbor when rooted at c) via rerooting: for each directed edge c→v, the "child count" of v relative to c is deg(v) - 1 (its children when c is parent), except we need the count of v's children excluding c, which is simply deg(v)-1 for neighbors v of c. Wait: when rooted at c, child v's children are its neighbors except c, so the number of available leaves under v is deg(v) - 1 (for v ≠ c). So for center c, the relevant multiset is { deg(v) - 1 : v ∈ N(c) }, and we may keep child v with y leaves iff y ≤ deg(v) - 1. Kept vertices = 1 + max over y≥1 of (1+y) * #{v ∈ N(c) : deg(v) - 1 ≥ y}. Also we could keep a child with y=0? No, y ≥ 1 required, and x ≥ 1. Also the whole tree could be a single edge? N ≥ 3 and a Snowflake Tree always has 1 + x + x*y ≥ 3 vertices, fine. So answer = N - max over c of best(c), where best(c) = 1 + max_{y≥1} (1+y) * cnt_c(y), cnt_c(y) = number of neighbors v of c with deg(v) ≥ y+1. This is O(deg(c) log deg(c)) per vertex via sorting neighbor degrees, total O(N log N). Edge case: a center with no neighbor having deg ≥ 2 gives best(c) = 1 (just center), but problem guarantees feasibility overall; also consider that the final snowflake's center must have x ≥ 1, so best(c) requires at least one neighbor with deg(v) ≥ 2; taking max over all c handles it.
