We need to count numbers in [l, r] where digit product is divisible by digit sum. Since r < 10^9, numbers have at most 9 digits, so digit sum is at most 81 and digit product is at most 9^9 (~3.8e8). A digit DP over the decimal representation of r (and l-1) can track position, tightness, whether the number has started (leading zeros), digit sum, and digit product. However, the product state space is large; we can cap it by noting that we only need product mod sum at the end, but sum isn't known until the end. A cleaner approach: enumerate possible sums (1..81) and for each sum, run a DP tracking product modulo sum (or track product exactly but capped). Alternatively, since any number containing a zero digit has product 0, which is divisible by any sum, those are automatically beautiful — this simplifies many cases. The safest concrete plan: digit DP with memoization on (pos, sum, product, started) where product is capped (e.g., store product exactly but prune: if product exceeds a bound like 81*something we still need exact mod for each sum). Better: for each target sum s in 1..81, DP counting numbers with digit sum s and product % s == 0, tracking product mod s. Sum over s. Complexity: 81 sums * 10 positions * 81 sum-state * s mod-states * 10 digits — trivially fast.
