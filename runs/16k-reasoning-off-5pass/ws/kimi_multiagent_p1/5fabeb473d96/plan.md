The distance between u and v equals the sum of A_i over edges i on the path. By linearity, the total over all (N-1)! trees is the sum over edges i of A_i times the number of trees where edge i lies on the u–v path. Edge i (connecting i to its parent P_i < i) is on the path iff exactly one of u, v is in the subtree of i. Since P_i can be any vertex in [1, i-1], the subtree of i is a "recursive" structure: we need, for each i and each target vertex x, the probability that x is in subtree(i), which satisfies a DP: f(i, x) = [P_i = x or P_i's subtree contains x] averaged over P_i ∈ [1, i-1]. This gives f(i, x) = (g(x))/(i-1) where g depends on ancestors; concretely, x is in subtree(i) iff following the chain from x upward reaches i, and the probability factors as a product: Pr[x ∈ subtree(i)] = (1/(i-1)) · Π over the chain. Actually the clean formulation: Pr[x ∈ subtree(i)] = (1/(i-1)) · Σ over intermediate, yielding f(i,x) = (1/(i-1)) · Π_{k} ... — the workers should derive that Pr[x ∈ subtree(i)] = (1/(i-1)) · H(x, i) where H is a product of (1 + 1/(k-1)) terms = product of k/(k-1) over the chain, telescoping. Then the count for query (u,v) is (N-1)! · Σ_i A_i · [f(i,u)(1-f(i,v)) + f(i,v)(1-f(i,u))], and since f(i, x) only depends on the chain between x and i, f(i,u) and f(i,v) are nonzero only when i > u or i > v respectively, and for i > v both may be nonzero with a shared suffix product. Precompute prefix products of modular inverses to answer each query in O(1) or O(log) after O(N) preprocessing.
