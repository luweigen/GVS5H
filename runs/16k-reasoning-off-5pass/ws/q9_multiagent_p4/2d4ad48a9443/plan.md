The problem asks us to count the number of subarrays that can be made non-decreasing with at most $k$ operations. Instead of checking every subarray (which would be $O(n^2)$), we can use a sliding window (two-pointer) approach. We will expand the right pointer of the window and maintain the minimum operations required to make the current window non-decreasing. If the required operations exceed $k$, we shrink the window from the left until the cost is within $k$. To efficiently calculate the cost for the current window, we can use a monotonic stack or a segment tree to track the "steps" needed to raise elements to match the next larger element, allowing us to compute the total cost in logarithmic or amortized constant time. Given the constraints and the nature of the cost function (sum of differences), a monotonic stack approach to maintain segments of equal values and their cumulative costs is efficient.
