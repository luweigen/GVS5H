The problem requires finding the largest integer $\le A$ that has exactly two distinct prime factors $p, q$ where both $p$ and $q$ appear with an even exponent in the prime factorization. This implies the number must be of the form $p^{2k} \cdot q^{2m} = (p^k \cdot q^m)^2$. Thus, we are looking for the largest perfect square $\le A$ that is the product of exactly two distinct prime bases raised to any powers (which effectively means the square root is a number with exactly two distinct prime factors). We can precompute all such "valid roots" up to $\sqrt{10^{12}} = 10^6$. For each query $A$, we calculate $S = \lfloor\sqrt{A}\rfloor$, find the largest precomputed valid root $\le S$, and output its square.
