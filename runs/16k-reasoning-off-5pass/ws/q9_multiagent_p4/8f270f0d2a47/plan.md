To solve this problem efficiently within the constraint $r < 10^9$, we cannot iterate through every number in the range $[l, r]$ because the time limit would be exceeded. Instead, we will implement a Digit DP (Dynamic Programming) approach. The strategy involves creating a helper function `count(n)` that calculates the number of beautiful integers in the range $[1, n]$. The final answer will be `count(r) - count(l - 1)`. Inside `count(n)`, we will use memoization to traverse digit by digit, tracking the current product of digits, the current sum of digits, and whether we are still restricted by the digits of $n$. Since the product can grow very large, we will cap the product at a value larger than the maximum possible sum (which is $9 \times 9 = 81$ for 9 digits) to keep the DP state manageable, as any product exceeding the sum will remain divisible by the sum regardless of further digit additions.
