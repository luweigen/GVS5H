The problem asks for the sum of Manhattan distances between all pairs of pieces across all valid arrangements. Since the grid is rectangular and the pieces are identical, the total distance can be decomposed into the sum of horizontal distances and vertical distances independently. For each dimension (rows and columns), we can calculate the contribution of the distance between any two cells by considering how many times that specific distance appears across all pairs and all arrangements. The key insight is that the number of ways to choose the remaining $k-2$ pieces for a specific pair of cells depends only on the number of available empty cells, which is $mn - 2$. We can precompute the number of pairs of cells with a specific distance in one dimension and then multiply by the number of ways to place the rest of the pieces.
