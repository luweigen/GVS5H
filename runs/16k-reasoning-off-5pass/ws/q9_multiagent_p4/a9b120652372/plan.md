The strategy is to identify the positions of the pieces in both strings. If the number of pieces in A is less than in B, it's impossible. Otherwise, we have $K$ pieces and $M$ target locations. We need to map the $K$ pieces to the $M$ targets such that the relative order is preserved and all targets are covered. The optimal mapping involves stacking the excess $K-M$ pieces at some position (either at the start, end, or distributed). Since the cost function is the sum of gap reductions, and the gap reduction depends on where we stack the extra pieces, we can iterate through all possible stack positions (from 0 to $K-M$) and find the minimum cost. The cost for a given stack position is the sum of differences between initial gaps and the resulting target gaps, considering only positive differences.
