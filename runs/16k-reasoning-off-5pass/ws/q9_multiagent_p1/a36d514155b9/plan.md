The problem asks for the sum of Manhattan distances between all pairs of pieces across all valid arrangements. Since the grid is symmetric and pieces are identical, we can separate the Manhattan distance into row differences and column differences. We calculate the contribution of row differences independently and column differences independently, then sum them. For rows, we consider choosing $k$ rows out of $m$ and placing pieces in those rows; similarly for columns with $n$ and $k$. The number of ways to choose specific rows and columns determines the multiplier for the distance sum of those dimensions. We precompute factorials and their modular inverses to efficiently calculate combinations. Finally, we sum the contributions from rows and columns modulo $10^9 + 7$.
