The problem asks for the maximum strength Takahashi can achieve by absorbing adjacent slimes that are strictly less than $S/X$ of his current strength. Since absorbing a slime increases Takahashi's strength, it might enable him to absorb larger slimes later. This suggests a greedy approach: always absorb the smallest available valid neighbor to maximize the chance of meeting the condition for future, larger slimes. We can simulate this process using a priority queue (min-heap) to efficiently retrieve the smallest valid neighbor. We start with Takahashi's initial strength and current position, then iteratively check the 4 adjacent cells. If a neighbor is valid, we absorb it, update Takahashi's strength and position, and add the new neighbors of the absorbed cell to our consideration set. We repeat this until no valid moves remain.
