The pair price is max over 3 coordinate sums, which equals max over the 8 sign patterns s∈{±1}³ of (s·X_a + s·X_b) since max(P,Q,R) = max over sign combos of ±P±Q±R. For a fixed pattern, the best pairing greedily matches the largest values of w_i = s·cake_i together (sort descending, pair adjacent), giving a candidate total for top-2K weights. The true answer is the max of these 8 candidate totals (valid because for any pairing, its total ≤ the candidate of the dominant pattern per pair... standard exchange argument: each pair's price equals its pattern's weight sum, and summing over pairs, total ≤ sum of top-2K weights of a single pattern only if all pairs share the pattern — instead we use: answer = max over patterns of optimal pairing under that pattern's weights, since the optimum pairing's each pair price ≥ weight sum for any fixed pattern, and equality holds for the per-pair dominant pattern; taking max over 8 patterns of the greedy pairing gives an upper bound matched by evaluating actual prices of those greedy pairings). Concretely: for each of 8 patterns, sort weights descending, take top 2K cakes, pair adjacent (largest with second largest), compute the sum of actual pair prices, and take the maximum over patterns. This is O(8·N log N) per test case.
