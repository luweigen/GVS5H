The key observation is that pieces are indistinguishable and the operation moves every piece one step toward a chosen target i (pieces already at i stay). Track each piece's position over time: after a sequence of targets, a piece's final position depends only on the sequence of chosen targets relative to its current position. Crucially, the relative order of pieces is preserved (they never cross), so the k-th piece in A must end at the k-th 1-position in B; hence the counts of 1s must match. Each piece's trajectory is a walk that moves ±1 per operation (or stays if a target equals its current square), and all pieces share the same target sequence. Model each piece's required displacement and derive constraints on the target sequence: choosing target i moves all pieces left of i right and all right of i left, so each operation is fully described by its target. Simulate greedily: process pieces and required destinations, and determine the minimal number of operations by checking, for each operation count, feasibility via the constraint that a piece at p needing to reach q must have targets bracketing its path appropriately. A cleaner reformulation: a piece starting at s ends at t iff among the chosen targets, the net movement works out — equivalently, the sequence of targets must "guide" it; the minimal operations equals the max over pieces of |s−t| plus parity/coverage adjustments, computable by scanning with two pointers and validating with a sweep.
