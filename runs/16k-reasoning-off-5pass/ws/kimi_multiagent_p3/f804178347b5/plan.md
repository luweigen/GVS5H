Model the repeated majority operation as a ternary tree of height N whose leaves are the characters of A; each internal node takes the majority of its three children. Compute, for every node, the minimum number of leaf flips needed to force that node to output 0 and to output 1, via a bottom-up DP. For a node with children costs (c0_i, c1_i), the cost to output 1 is the sum of the two smallest c1_i (flip the two cheapest children to 1), and symmetrically for 0. The answer is the cost at the root to produce the opposite of the current final value. A bottom-up pass over the string (repeatedly folding groups of three) avoids recursion-depth issues and runs in O(3^N) time and memory.
