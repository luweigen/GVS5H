A valid coloring is a "staircase" (Young diagram) shape: there is a monotone boundary such that cell (r,c) is black iff c <= f(r) for a non-increasing row function, equivalently black cells form an order ideal of the product poset. The key characterization: the set of black cells must be "convex" in the sense that if (r,c) is black then all (r',c') with r'<=r and c'<=c are black; equivalently, there is no pair with a black cell strictly southeast of a white cell... more precisely, no white cell (r1,c1) and black cell (r2,c2) with r2>=r1 and c2>=c1 (with at least one strict, cells distinct). So the answer is No iff there exist a white-fixed cell (x1,y1) and a black-fixed cell (x2,y2) with x2>=x1 and y2>=y1 (cells distinct). We need to check this efficiently for M up to 2e5: for each white cell, ask whether any black cell lies in its southeast quadrant (including same row/col). This is a 2D dominance query solvable by sorting and sweeping with a BIT/segment tree over coordinates, or by sorting by row and maintaining max column of black cells seen.
