A Snowflake Tree has three levels: a center (red), x middle vertices (blue), and y leaves per middle vertex (green). So the target is a tree of diameter at most 4 with a specific structure: one center, all its neighbors are middle vertices, and every middle vertex has exactly y leaf children (degree y+1 except possibly the center's degree x). We need to delete the fewest vertices so the remaining induced connected subgraph is exactly such a tree. Key observation: in the final tree, pick the center c; every remaining vertex is at distance 1 (middle) or 2 (leaf) from c, all distance-1 vertices have equal number of remaining leaf-children, and no other edges exist. Since T is a tree, for a chosen center c the kept set must be: c, a subset S of neighbors of c, and for each chosen neighbor an equal-sized subset of its children (excluding c). So for each candidate center c, we compute for each neighbor the number of "available leaves" (neighbors of that neighbor except c, but we must also ensure those leaves have no extra kept children — we simply delete all deeper vertices). Then we choose x ≥ 1 neighbors and keep min over chosen of available leaves as y; maximize kept = 1 + x*(1+y). Also handle the degenerate case where the snowflake is just a star (y can be 0? y must be positive, but x=1,y=1 gives path of 3; also single edge? N≥3 and always possible, so minimum snowflake has 3 vertices: x=1,y=1). Also the center could be a middle vertex instead — but symmetric: better to also consider that the snowflake's center must be a vertex of T and structure as described; answer = N - max kept. Also consider keeping a snowflake where chosen center's neighbor's leaves must themselves have no children kept — deleting them is fine. Edge case: we could also delete such that center has x neighbors each with y leaves, x,y≥1, so kept ≥ 3.
