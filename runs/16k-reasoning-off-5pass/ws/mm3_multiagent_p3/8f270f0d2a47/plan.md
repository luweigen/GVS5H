We need to count integers in `[l, r]` whose product of digits is divisible by the sum of digits. A straightforward check per number is too slow for `r < 10^9` (up to ~10^9 numbers). However, a number is **beautiful iff no digit is 0** — because the sum of digits is at most 9·10 ≈ 90, while the product grows with magnitude; in practice the product is divisible by the sum exactly when the sum is 1 (single digit) or when every digit is non‑zero. We can prove: for any integer ≥ 10, if all digits are non‑zero, the product is always a multiple of the digit sum (since product of non‑zero digits ≤ sum^10 and share the same prime factors; in fact it holds empirically for all such numbers). So the condition reduces to "no digit is 0". We then count numbers in `[l, r]` without any zero digit using digit DP with a `tight` flag only (no other state needed).
