The cost of flipping a single index i depends on the current values of A before the flip; it equals the sum of C_k over all k where A_k = 1. Therefore, flipping a position with value 1 costs the current total sum S, while flipping a 0 costs S plus C_i (since A_i becomes 1). To minimize total cost, we should only flip positions that are wrong: all 1→0 flips first (cost S each, reducing future costs), then all 0→1 flips (cost S plus C_i each). Since C_i > 0, ordering 1→0 before 0→1 is optimal, giving total cost = S * (# of 1→0 flips) + Σ_{i in 0→1} (S + C_i). This can be computed in O(N) with a single pass.
