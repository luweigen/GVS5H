We need the sum of Manhattan distances over all unordered pairs of pieces across every valid placement of k indistinguishable pieces on an m×n grid (choose k cells uniformly). Manhattan distance separates: |x_i - x_j| + |y_i - y_j|. By linearity of expectation (and counting), the total sum over all arrangements equals (number of arrangements) × (expected distance per pair) summed over pairs. Equivalent: For each ordered pair of pieces, count the number of arrangements where that ordered pair occupies cells (a,b) and sum |xa-xb|+|ya-yb|. By symmetry across rows and columns, we can compute row contribution and column contribution independently, then sum. For rows: sum over all ordered pairs of cells (a,b) in same row of |a-b| times number of ways to place remaining k-2 pieces in other cells. Factor out common combinatorial factor. Use combinatorial identities to compute sums of absolute differences in a line of length L efficiently (L up to 10^5), then combine for 2D using product of row/column combinatorial factors.
