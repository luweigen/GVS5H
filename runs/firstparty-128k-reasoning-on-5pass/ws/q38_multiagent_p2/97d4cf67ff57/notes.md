- **Reduction:** In any alkane, if Q is the number of degree-4 vertices, leaves are 2Q+2 and total vertices are 3Q+2. The degree-4 vertices form a connected set in T; each has original degree at least 4 and at most 4 neighbors inside the set. Conversely, any connected set S of vertices with original degree >=4 and internal degree <=4 can be extended to an alkane by adding 4-k_v outside neighbors as leaves for each v in S; outside neighbors are distinct because T is a tree and S is connected. Thus maximize |S| and output 3|S|+2, or -1 if no such S.
- **Eligible forest:** Build the forest induced by vertices with original degree >=4. The problem becomes finding the largest connected subset with maximum degree at most 4.
- **DP:** Root each eligible component. Let f[u] be the best size of a valid connected subset in u's subtree containing u when u's parent is selected, so u may take at most 3 child branches. Since all branch sizes are positive, f[u]=1+sum of top 3 f[children]. The best subset whose highest node is u has no selected parent, so u may take at most 4 child branches: 1+sum of top 4 f[children]. The global answer is the maximum of these top values.
- **Implementation:** Iterative DFS over the eligible forest gives parent and a preorder; reversed preorder is a postorder. For each u, scan adjacency and consider only v with parent[v]==u. Maintain the top four f[v] values with four variables, no sorting. Use bytearray for eligibility and delete input/degree arrays to save memory.
- **Edge cases:** No eligible vertex -> -1. Isolated eligible vertex -> ans=1 -> output 5. Nodes with more than four eligible children are handled by taking top 3 for f and top 4 for the top value. Dropped eligible neighbors may later be used as leaves in the extension, which is allowed.
- **Complexity:** O(N) time and O(N) memory; no recursion and no sorting.
