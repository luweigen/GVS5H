A valid coloring is exactly a down-left closed black region: if (r,c) is black, then every (r'<=r,c'<=c) is black. Thus a precolored white cell makes completion impossible iff it lies in the downward closure of some precolored black cell, i.e. x_w <= x_b and y_w <= y_b. If no such pair exists, coloring the downward closure of all black cells black and all other cells white is a valid completion. Check this dominance condition in O(M log M) by sorting points by row descending, maintaining the maximum column among black points seen, and testing each white point against that maximum (processing same-row blacks before whites).
