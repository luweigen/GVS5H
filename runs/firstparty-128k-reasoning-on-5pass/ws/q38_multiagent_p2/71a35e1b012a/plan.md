Model each paid operation as covering either its interval (type 1) or the complement of its interval (type 2), so the goal is a minimum-cardinality cover of all positions by chosen sets. The minimum cost is 1 iff some interval is exactly [1,N]; it is 2 iff either two type-1 intervals cover [1,N], one interval contains another (type 1 on the container, type 2 on the contained), or two intervals are disjoint (type 2 on both). If M>=3 and no cost-1 or cost-2 case exists, then no two intervals are disjoint and no interval contains another, so after sorting by left endpoint the right endpoints are strictly increasing and the first and last intervals overlap. Choosing type 1 on the first and last intervals and type 2 on any middle interval covers the middle interval by the two type-1 intervals and everything outside it by type 2, giving cost 3. For M<3, only the cost-1 and cost-2 conditions can succeed; otherwise the answer is -1.
