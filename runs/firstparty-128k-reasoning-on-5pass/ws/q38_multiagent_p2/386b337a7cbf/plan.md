Model subsequences by their current length parity (next sign) and product, and store all achievable alternating sums as a bitset. The total absolute sum is at most 1800, so use an offset and mask to keep sum bitsets small. For positive products, keep only products 1..limit; this is safe because multiplying by positive integers never decreases product, so any valid positive final subsequence has all prefixes within limit. Maintain separate all-subsequence and zero-containing sum bitsets to handle final product 0, since a prefix product may exceed limit before a zero resets the product. For each number, copy states to represent skipping, add the one-element subsequence, and append the number by shifting sum bitsets (+x for even length, -x for odd length) and mapping product p to p*x when x>0. Finally, check the bitset for sum k across both parities and return the highest positive product, else 0 if a zero-containing subsequence reaches k, else -1.
