Because the operation preserves the order of pieces and never increases any gap, a target configuration B is reachable only by partitioning the 1s of A into contiguous blocks, one per 1 of B, with each cut A-gap at least the corresponding B-gap. For a fixed partition, let d_i be the displacement of the i-th A piece to its assigned B position; d is nonincreasing, and the minimum operations for that partition depends only on d_1, d_n, and a binary parity p_1 that must be consistent with cuts where the A-gap equals the B-gap. Propagating this parity over the A gaps shows that for each starting parity p∈{0,1}, feasibility is a greedy subsequence match of the B gaps to A gaps: choose the earliest A gap that is larger than the B gap, or equal with the required prefix parity. Preprocess A gaps with a segment tree for first larger gap and exact (gap, prefix parity) lists to answer these queries in O(log N). If no starting parity is feasible print -1; otherwise take the minimum of the two closed-form costs computed from D=d_1-d_n and E=d_1+d_n.
