- **Prime-wise independence:** For a fixed prime \(p\), let \(e_i=v_p(A_i)\). The valuation difference \(v_p(S_i)-v_p(S_{i+1})\) must be \(+e_i\) or \(-e_i\) when \(e_i>0\), and must be zero when \(e_i=0\). Choices for different primes are independent, so the final answer is the product of their contributions.
- **Normalized valuation walk:** For one prime, the valuation sequence is a walk whose edge differences are the chosen signed exponents. Subtracting the minimum valuation from every vertex gives the unique sequence with minimum zero, corresponding to gcd normalization.
- **Dynamic programming state:** `dp[h]` stores the sum of score contributions for processed vertices whose current valuation is `h` above the minimum. The weight already includes \(p^{\text{sum of normalized valuations}}\).
- **Transition without a new minimum:** If the next raw height is nonnegative, the new vertex contributes its height directly, so the transition multiplier is \(p^{h'}\).
- **Transition creating a new minimum:** If the current height is \(h<e_i\) and the \(-e_i\) edge is chosen, the raw next height is \(h-e_i<0\). All previously processed \(i\) vertices are shifted upward by \(e_i-h\), giving multiplier \(p^{(e_i-h)i}\), while the new normalized height is zero.
- **Complexity:** For each prime, the number of height states is at most the total exponent of that prime across all edges. The total work is \(O\!\left(N\sum_p\sum_i v_p(A_i)\right)\), bounded comfortably by the constraints.
- **Edge cases:** If every \(A_i=1\), no primes occur and the answer is one, corresponding to the all-ones sequence.
