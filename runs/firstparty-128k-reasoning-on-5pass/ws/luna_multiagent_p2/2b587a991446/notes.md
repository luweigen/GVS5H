- **Reduction:** A legal exchange is equivalent to sending two units of flow from \(S\) to \(T\), representing two internally vertex-disjoint \(S\)-\(T\) paths. The two paths are traversed in opposite directions by the two labeled pieces.
- **Lower bound lemma:** Consider the two time-ordered walks traced by the pieces during any legal sequence. Each starts at one endpoint and finishes at the other, and a move contributes one edge to exactly one walk. The standard uncrossing argument for two alternating walks removes repeated excursions and resolves internal intersections while preserving the endpoints; legality (the pieces are never simultaneously at one vertex) guarantees that the resulting pair can be chosen internally vertex-disjoint. No edge occurrence is added, so the sum of their lengths is at most the number of moves.
- **Upper bound:** Given internally vertex-disjoint paths \(P,Q\) from \(S\) to \(T\), move A along \(P\) until it is adjacent to \(T\), move B along \(Q\) all the way to \(S\), then make A’s final move to \(T\). The paths have no common internal vertex, so every move is legal. The number of moves is \(|P|+|Q|\).
- **Optimization:** Therefore the answer is the minimum total length of two internally vertex-disjoint \(S\)-\(T\) paths.
- **Flow network:** Split every vertex \(v\) into \(v_{\text{in}}\) and \(v_{\text{out}}\). Add an arc \(v_{\text{in}}\to v_{\text{out}}\) of capacity 1 and cost 0 for internal vertices, and capacity 2 for \(S,T\). For every undirected edge \(\{u,v\}\), add both directed arcs \(u_{\text{out}}\to v_{\text{in}}\) and \(v_{\text{out}}\to u_{\text{in}}\), each with capacity 1 and cost 1.
- **Min-cost flow:** Send two units from \(S_{\text{out}}\) to \(T_{\text{in}}\). Integral flow decomposes into the desired paths, and its cost is their total length. Successive shortest augmenting paths with Dijkstra and potentials are sufficient because only two units are required.
- **Complexity:** The network has \(O(N+M)\) vertices and arcs. Two augmentations take \(O((N+M)\log N)\) time and \(O(N+M)\) memory.
