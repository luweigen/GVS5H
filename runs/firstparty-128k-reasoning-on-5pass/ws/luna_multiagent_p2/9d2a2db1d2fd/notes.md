- **Graph model:** Each building is a vertex. An edge between cardinally adjacent buildings has capacity equal to the smaller floor count of its endpoints. A walkway route can be used at floor \(x\) exactly when every vertex on the route has at least \(x\) floors.
- **Bottleneck value:** For two buildings \(u,v\), let \(M(u,v)\) be the maximum, over all grid paths between them, of the minimum building capacity on that path. Thus \(M\) is the greatest floor level that can be maintained while walking between the two buildings.
- **Reconstruction tree:** Process all graph edges in decreasing capacity order with Kruskal's algorithm. Whenever two DSU components merge at capacity \(w\), create an internal reconstruction-tree node of weight \(w\), with the two component roots as children. The weight of the LCA of two distinct original vertices equals their maximum bottleneck value.
- **Stair-cost formula:** If a common walkway floor \(x\) is chosen, the stair cost is \(|Y-x|+|Z-x|\), with \(1\le x\le M\). Its minimum is attained at \(x=\min(Y,Z)\) when \(M\ge\min(Y,Z)\), otherwise at \(x=M\). Therefore the answer is \(Y+Z-2\min(Y,Z,M)\).
- **Lower bound justification:** If \(M<\min(Y,Z)\), the two endpoint buildings cannot be connected while staying above floor \(M\), so any route must descend from \(Y\) to at most \(M\) and later ascend to \(Z\), costing at least \((Y-M)+(Z-M)\). If \(M\ge\min(Y,Z)\), the endpoint floor interval permits a common floor and the unavoidable cost is at least \(|Y-Z|\).
- **Same building:** No walkway is needed; moving directly by stairs costs \(|Y-Z|\).
- **Complexity:** The graph has \(O(HW)\) edges. Kruskal sorting costs \(O(HW\log(HW))\); each query uses an LCA in \(O(\log(HW))\). Memory usage is \(O(HW\log(HW))\).
