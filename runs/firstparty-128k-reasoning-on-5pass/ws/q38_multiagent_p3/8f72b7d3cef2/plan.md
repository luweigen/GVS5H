Model the final state of slime K as the smallest “stable” interval containing K, where an interval is stable if each adjacent outside slime (or sentinel) has size at least the interval’s total size. A stable interval can be represented by its outside boundaries x and y, and it is valid exactly when the sum between x and y is at most both A[x] and A[y]. Precompute prefix sums, then for every left boundary x compute how far right it can support, and for every right boundary y compute how far left it can support. Use a segment tree over the left-support values to find, for each x, the rightmost compatible right boundary, giving the range of positions for which x can be the left boundary of their minimal stable interval. Sweep positions left to right with a max-heap to choose the largest compatible left boundary for each K, then query the segment tree for the smallest compatible right boundary; the answer is the prefix-sum difference of that interval.
