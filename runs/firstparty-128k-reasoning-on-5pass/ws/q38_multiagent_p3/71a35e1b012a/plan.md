Any feasible instance has an optimal solution with at most three operations: operation-2 choices can be reduced to at most two, and if a solution still uses more than three operations, interval endpoint arguments yield a two- or three-operation solution. Therefore we only need to test costs 1, 2, and 3. Cost 1 exists exactly when some interval is [1,N] and is used as operation 1. Cost 2 exists exactly when either two operation-1 intervals cover [1,N], one interval is contained in another (contained as operation 2, container as operation 1), or two intervals are disjoint (both as operation 2). If no cost 1 or 2 exists and M>=3, taking a maximum-L interval and a minimum-R interval as operation 2 and any third interval as operation 1 always works, because their intersection is contained in the third interval. If M<3 and no cost 1/2 exists, the goal is impossible.
