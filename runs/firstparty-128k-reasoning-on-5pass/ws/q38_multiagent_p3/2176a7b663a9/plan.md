Because an edge exists exactly when two intervals are disjoint, a direct edge is optimal for disjoint query endpoints since all vertex weights are positive. For intersecting endpoints, any path can be shortened to at most three edges without increasing its vertex-weight sum: either some internal vertex is disjoint from both endpoints, or the first and last internal vertices lie on opposite sides of the two intervals. If \(a\) is the endpoint with smaller \(L\) and \(b\) the other, the only non-dominated three-edge form uses one middle vertex with \(L > R_a\) and another with \(R < L_b\), and these two middle vertices are automatically disjoint. Therefore each query only needs the minimum weight among intervals with \(R < x\) and with \(L > x\) for a few values of \(x\). Precompute prefix minima over \(R\) and suffix minima over \(L\) in \(O(N)\), then answer each query in \(O(1)\).
