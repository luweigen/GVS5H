A valid coloring is completely described by \(a_r\), the number of black cells in row \(r\). The column condition is equivalent to \(a_1 \ge a_2 \ge \dots \ge a_N\). Each given black cell \((x,y)\) forces \(a_x \ge y\), and each given white cell forces \(a_x \le y-1\), so row \(x\) has interval \([L_x,U_x]\) where \(L_x\) is the maximum black column in that row and \(U_x\) is the minimum white column minus one. Because \(N\) is huge, only rows appearing in the input have non-default bounds; other rows are \([0,N]\). Feasibility is exactly that for every row \(r\), the maximum \(L_s\) over all \(s \ge r\) is at most \(U_r\), i.e. no white cell has a black cell at or below it and at or to its right. We can check this sparse suffix-max condition in \(O(M \log M)\) by sorting constrained rows in decreasing row order.
