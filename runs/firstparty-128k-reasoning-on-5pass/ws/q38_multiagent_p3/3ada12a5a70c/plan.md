Map every boundary point to a unique coordinate on the square's perimeter in clockwise order, giving a circle of circumference 4*side. For k >= 4 the answer is at most side, and for any candidate distance D <= side, Manhattan constraints are equivalent to requiring the perimeter gap between consecutive selected points in cyclic order to be at least D. Thus the problem reduces to choosing k points on a discrete circle to maximize the minimum cyclic gap. Binary search the integer answer and check feasibility by, for each possible first point, greedily taking the earliest next point at least D away; D is feasible if k points fit in one lap and the wrap-around gap is also at least D. This check can be done in O(n*k) per D using sorted coordinates and next-jump pointers, which is easily fast for n <= 15000 and k <= 25.
