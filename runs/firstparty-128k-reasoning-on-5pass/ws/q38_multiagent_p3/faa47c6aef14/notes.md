- **Color independence:** Red balls only follow P, blue balls only follow Q. Since P and Q are permutations, each color stays inside one cycle. A ball can end at X iff it starts in the cycle containing X for its color; otherwise the answer is -1.
- **Distance and chains:** For each color, traverse the inverse permutation from X: order[0]=X, order[d]=inv^d(X). A ball at distance d must have boxes order[d], order[d-1], ..., order[1] operated in that order. The farthest initial ball of that color determines the whole required chain; empty intermediate boxes are included because farther balls pass through them. Balls already at X have distance 0 and require no operations.
- **Operation coupling:** One operation on a box is one symbol that can serve the red chain and/or the blue chain. Any successful sequence must contain the red chain and the blue chain as subsequences, by following the farthest ball of each color. Conversely, any sequence containing both chains works: the chain occurrences force every ball's distance to X down to 0, extra operations only move balls closer to X, and X itself is never operated.
- **Minimum count:** The answer is the shortest common supersequence length of the two chains: len(R)+len(B)-LCS(R,B). Repeated operations are allowed and are exactly what the supersequence may introduce when shared boxes appear in opposite relative order.
- **LCS computation:** Each chain has distinct box names. Build a position array for Bchain, then take the positions of R elements that appear in Bchain and compute the longest strictly increasing subsequence using bisect_left. This gives LCS in O(N log N).
- **Implementation details:** Use 0-based indices. Build invP and invQ. Traverse until the next inverse step returns to X; this sets distances only for the X-cycle and leaves other boxes at -1. D_R and D_B are maximum distances among initial red/blue balls. Chains are orderP[D_R:0:-1] and orderQ[D_B:0:-1], empty when D=0.
- **Edge cases:** All balls absent or only at X gives 0. Self-loop at X gives chain length 0. A ball outside the relevant X-cycle gives -1 even if the other color is fine. If one chain is empty, the answer is the other chain length. If chains are identical or one is a prefix, the answer is the longer length. If chains are reverses, LCS is small and repeated operations are needed.
- **Pitfalls avoided:** Do not topologically sort unique boxes; conflicts may require operating the same box twice. Do not omit empty boxes on the farthest path. Do not include X in chains. Use inverse traversal from X, then reverse the chain to operation order. Use strict LIS with bisect_left.
- **Validation:** Manual traces give sample outputs 4, 0, -1, 8. Sample 1 chains in 1-based notation are red [2,1,4] and blue [5,1], LCS 1, answer 4. Sample 4 chains are red [7,3,9] and blue [5,6,3,9,8,2,4], LCS 2, answer 8.
- **Complexity:** O(N log N) time and O(N) memory, suitable for N up to 2e5.
