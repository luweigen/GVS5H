Treat red and blue balls independently: red moves along P and blue moves along Q, and because P and Q are permutations a ball can reach X only if it starts on the corresponding cycle containing X. If any red or blue ball starts outside its color's X-cycle, the answer is -1. For each color, the boxes that must be operated form a chain in decreasing distance to X, up to the farthest initial ball of that color. One operation on a box can serve both colors, so the minimum number of operations is the length of the shortest common supersequence of the red and blue chains. Compute the chains by inverse-permutation traversal from X, then compute their LCS length with an LIS over positions and output len(R)+len(B)-LCS.
