- **Prefix model:** Let P_t be the prefix sum of road strengths. For a person with normalized interval [l,r], S<T means P_l=P_r and P_x>P_l for l<x<r (strict minimum); S>T means P_l=P_r and P_x<P_l (strict maximum).
- **Feasibility criterion:** A subset is feasible iff it contains no bad pair: two intervals with the same left endpoint, two with the same right endpoint, or two same-sign intervals crossing l1<l2<r1<r2. Sharing a town as the right endpoint of one and left endpoint of the other is allowed; touching r1=l2 is allowed; nested intervals and opposite-sign crossing are allowed. Opposite identical intervals are caught by the same-endpoint rules.
- **Why pairwise:** Endpoint equalities form directed paths, and the strict inequalities between endpoint components and interior components are acyclic exactly when the bad pairs are absent. A topological ranking then gives integer P values.
- **Crossing detection:** For a new interval (l,r) of sign s, a same-sign crossing exists iff some active same-sign interval has a right endpoint in (l,r) with left endpoint < l, or a left endpoint in (l,r) with right endpoint > r. Maintain per sign a min tree over right endpoints (leaf stores left endpoint) and a max tree over left endpoints (leaf stores right endpoint). One range query over [l+1,r) can collect both the minimum left and maximum right.
- **No heaps needed:** The sliding window is always feasible, so no two active intervals share a left or right endpoint. Each leaf therefore holds at most one value; removal is a point update to INF or -1.
- **Sliding window:** limit[L] is the largest exclusive R such that persons L..R-1 are feasible. try_add checks global left/right counts and the combined sign-specific range query, then updates counts and trees. After recording limit[L], remove person L. R only increases, so each person is added and removed once.
- **Queries:** For a 1-indexed query L,R, use l0=L-1 and exclusive end R. Answer yes iff R <= limit[l0].
- **Complexity:** O((M+Q) log N) time and O(N+M+Q) memory. Four segment trees of size the next power of two at least N, two count arrays, person arrays, and the query list.
- **Implementation pitfalls:** Use 0-indexed towns; the interior range is half-open [l+1,r). Strict comparisons avoid flagging touching endpoints. Counts are global because same-side endpoint conflicts are bad regardless of sign. Crossing trees are sign-specific. A single person is always feasible, so limit[L] >= L+1.
