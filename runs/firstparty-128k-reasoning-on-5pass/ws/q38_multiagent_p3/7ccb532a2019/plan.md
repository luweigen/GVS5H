Model the string as 26 character counts, and represent a good final string by a common frequency k and a subset of letters that each have count k. For any fixed target counts d_i, use the baseline of deleting all original characters and inserting all final characters, costing n + sum d_i. A kept character saves 2 operations, a change to the next letter saves 1 operation, and any other change is no better than delete plus insert. After matching same letters first, the remaining savings are only adjacent next-letter matches, giving a closed-form cost in terms of min(c_i,d_i) and min(excess_i, deficit_{i+1}). For fixed k, d_i is either 0 or k, so the best subset is found by a 2-state DP over the 26 letters with unary and adjacent pairwise terms. Enumerate k from 1 to 2n, since larger k cannot beat the delete-all upper bound, and take the minimum cost.
