Count beautiful numbers in `[l, r]` by computing `f(r) - f(l - 1)`, where `f(N)` counts beautiful numbers up to `N`. Since any number containing an actual zero has digit product `0`, it is automatically beautiful, so count all numbers and subtract the “bad” numbers: positive numbers with no zero digits whose digit sum does not divide the digit product. Use digit DP over the digits of `N` with state for position, tightness, whether a number has started, current digit sum, and capped prime exponents of the digit product. Cap the exponents of `2, 3, 5, 7` at the maximum needed for any digit sum up to `81`, namely `(6, 4, 2, 1)`. At the end, a started nonzero-digit number is bad if the required prime exponents of its sum exceed the stored product exponents.
