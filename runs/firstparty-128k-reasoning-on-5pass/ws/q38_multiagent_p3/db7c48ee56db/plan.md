Use the guarantee \(\binom{N}{K} \le 10^6\) to brute force the smaller side of the selection. If \(K \le N-K\), enumerate all \(K\)-index subsets and compute their XOR directly; otherwise enumerate all \(N-K\) omitted indices and use \(\text{XOR(chosen)} = \text{XOR(all)} \oplus \text{XOR(omitted)}\). This symmetry keeps the number of enumerated combinations at most \(10^6\), and the binomial bound also forces \(\min(K, N-K)\) to be small, so the total XOR work is easily feasible. Track the maximum candidate over all generated combinations and output it.
