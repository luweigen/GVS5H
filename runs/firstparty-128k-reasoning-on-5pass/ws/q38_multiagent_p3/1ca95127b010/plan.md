Treat the operation as swapping adjacent blocks \(0^X\) and \(1^Y\); it moves a consecutive block of \(Y\) ones by \(X\) positions and a consecutive block of \(X\) zeros by \(Y\) positions, while preserving the relative order of all 1s and all 0s. Hence the position of the \(k\)-th 1 modulo \(X\) and the position of the \(k\)-th 0 modulo \(Y\) are invariant. These ordered residue sequences are also sufficient: the one-directional rewrite \(1^Y0^X \to 0^X1^Y\) is terminating and confluent, and its normal form is uniquely determined by exactly these residues. Therefore \(S\) can be transformed to \(T\) iff the number of 1s is the same and the ordered residues of the 1-positions modulo \(X\) and of the 0-positions modulo \(Y\) match. The check can be done in a single linear scan with \(O(N)\) memory.
