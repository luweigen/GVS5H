Model each additional unit of product i as a marginal cost P_i(2j-1) for j=1,2,..., and note that because each product's marginal costs increase, an optimal purchase is always a prefix of the globally sorted marginal costs. Define C(x) and S(x) as the number and total cost of all marginal units with cost at most x; for product i, c_i=floor((x+P_i)/(2P_i)) and its contribution is P_i c_i^2. Binary search the smallest integer T such that S(T)>M; this T is the first marginal cost where the budget is exhausted. The answer is then C(T-1)+floor((M-S(T-1))/T), because all cheaper marginal units are taken and the remaining budget buys as many T-cost units as possible. The algorithm runs in O(N log U) time with 64/128-bit or Python integer arithmetic, where U can be a safe upper bound such as 10^18 or a derived bound from min(P_i).
