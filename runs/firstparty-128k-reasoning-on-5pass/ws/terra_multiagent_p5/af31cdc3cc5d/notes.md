- **Binary threshold characterization:** For an even-length binary array with equally many `0` and `1`, all elements can be removed in opposite-valued pairs. Repeatedly choose any adjacent unequal pair; removing it preserves equal counts, so this continues to completion. Thus the maximum number of threshold-crossing pairs is the smaller color count.
- **Even-length structural result:** For any even segment of length `2k`, its optimum is independent of its original order:
  `sum(k largest values) - sum(k smallest values)`.
  Upper bound: orient every removed pair from smaller to larger. The sum of its `k` larger endpoints is at most the sum of the global `k` largest elements, while the sum of its smaller endpoints is at least the sum of the global `k` smallest.
- **Achievability for even segments:** Classify the `k` smallest values as low and the `k` largest as high. The balanced binary-array result gives a legal removal sequence pairing every low with a high. Its total score is exactly `sum(high) - sum(low)`, attaining the upper bound.
- **Threshold interpretation:** `|x-y|` is the integral/sum over thresholds of whether `x,y` lie on opposite sides. For even segments, the low/high construction attains the corresponding maximum threshold contribution consistently; equivalently, the sorted-extremes formula is achievable by one noncrossing removal sequence.
- **Odd-length caveat:** A single survivor prevents the naive whole-array sorted-extremes formula. Example `(1,2,3)` has optimum `1`, not `2`. If original position `i` survives, no removal pair may cross it, because it is never deleted and hence can never be passed through. Therefore all removals split into independent prefix and suffix problems.
- **Odd survivor positions:** Both sides of the survivor must have even lengths, so with 1-indexing the survivor must be at an odd position. The answer is `max_i F(A[1..i-1]) + F(A[i+1..N])` over odd `i`, where `F` is the even-segment sorted-extremes formula.
- **Implementation:** Maintain the smallest `floor(m/2)` values in a max-heap and all remaining values in a min-heap, together with their sums. For even `m`, the heap-sum difference equals `F`. Scan left-to-right for even prefixes and right-to-left for even suffixes. Complexity is `O(N log N)` time and `O(N)` memory.
- **Greedy maximum-adjacent-difference rule:** Disproved; it must not be used.
