- **Validation conclusion:** The constant-exception formulation remains consistent with exhaustive reasoning: after assigning every cake to a fixed coordinate attaining its individual maximum, any parity repair can be represented using at most two non-canonical assignments. With two odd canonical bucket sizes, either one direct reassignment fixes parity or two reassignments via the third coordinate can have smaller loss.
- **Reduction:** Color every selected cake by the coordinate used for its pair. If each color bucket has even size, cakes can be paired inside buckets and the sum of assigned coordinate values is attainable. Conversely, coloring each actual pair by an attaining coordinate gives such buckets.
- **Canonical assignment:** Let `M_i=max(X_i,Y_i,Z_i)` and assign a fixed canonical color attaining it. Non-canonical assignment only loses value. Parity corrections need at most two non-canonical cakes; parity-neutral extra cycles cannot improve the value.
- **Count locality:** For a fixed number `q<=2` of exceptional cakes, the canonical remainder has `2K-q` cakes. Relative to the unrestricted top-prefix canonical selection, exclusions shift counts by at most `q`, and enforcing parity shifts counts by at most two more. Enumerating count triples within radius `q+2` is sufficient.
- **Candidate locality:** For a source group and target coordinate, relative to a remaining top prefix of size `t`, a special cake inside the prefix has score `target_value-M`; outside it has score `target_value`. Keeping the best three candidates in each region suffices because at most two special cakes are selected and at most one candidate can be blocked by the other selection.
- **Performance fix:** The previous implementation recomputed global prefix color counts by scanning the first `L` sorted cakes on every count-triple query, causing excessive repeated work. The updated code computes needed base counts only once for `2K`, `2K-1`, and `2K-2`.
- **Complexity:** Sorting costs `O(N log N)`. Candidate scans are cached by `(source,target,prefix_length)`; only constantly many prefix lengths occur. Remaining enumeration is constant-sized, so total complexity is effectively `O(N log N)` per aggregate input.
