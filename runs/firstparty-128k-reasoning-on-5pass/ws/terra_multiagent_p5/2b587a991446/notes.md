- **Configuration characterization:** A swap is possible by either routing the two pieces along two internally vertex-disjoint simple `S-T` paths, or by using a spare neighbor at an internal vertex of a shortest `S-T` path as a temporary buffer.
- **Disjoint-path case:** If two internally vertex-disjoint paths have lengths `p` and `q`, the pieces can traverse them in opposite directions in exactly `p+q` moves. The minimum such value is a min-cost flow of value two after vertex splitting, with capacity one on every non-endpoint vertex.
- **Buffer case:** Let `d = dist(S,T)`. If an internal vertex of a shortest `S-T` path has degree at least three, its third incident neighbor is outside that shortest path; otherwise it would be a chord and contradict shortestness. One piece can wait there while the other crosses, requiring exactly `2d+2` moves.
- **Why one BFS path suffices:** If the disjoint-path construction is unavailable, the block-cut route from `S` to `T` has a bottleneck. Any relevant detour forces a branching vertex on every selected shortest route through that bottleneck. If different shortest routes avoid such a vertex, their divergence/rejoin supplies two internally vertex-disjoint paths instead, already covered by flow.
- **Tree/star behavior:** In a tree, the flow case is impossible. Swapping is possible exactly when the unique `S-T` path contains an internal degree-at-least-three vertex, yielding `2d+2`. This includes the four-vertex star counterexample, whose answer is six.
- **Complexity:** BFS is `O(N+M)`. The flow has only two augmentations and uses Dijkstra with potentials, so it is `O((N+M) log N)` time and `O(N+M)` memory.
- **Validation:** The prior exhaustive ordered-state BFS validator through all connected labeled graphs up to six vertices tested this formula against exact configuration-space distances without finding a mismatch.
