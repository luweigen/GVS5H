- **Core graph fact:** The graph is a co-interval graph: its complement is the interval-overlap graph. It is induced-\(P_5\)-free. Indeed, if the disjointness graph contained an induced \(P_5\), its complement on those five vertices would be the complement of \(P_5\), which contains an induced \(C_4\); interval graphs are chordal and cannot contain induced \(C_4\).
- **Bounded optimal path length:** Since every vertex weight is positive, a minimum-weight path is simple and chordless: any chord permits replacing a subpath by one edge and removing positive-weight internal vertices. A chordless path with at least four edges contains an induced \(P_5\), impossible above. Therefore every optimum has 1, 2, or 3 edges.
- **Direct path:** Vertices \(s,t\) are adjacent exactly when \(R_s<L_t\) or \(R_t<L_s\). Its extra internal cost is zero.
- **One intermediate:** A vertex is disjoint from both \(s,t\) iff it lies wholly left of both, \(R_x<\min(L_s,L_t)\), or wholly right of both, \(L_x>\max(R_s,R_t)\). Thus its minimum weight is `min(pref[min(Ls,Lt)-1], suff[max(Rs,Rt)+1])`.
- **Two intermediates structural reduction:** Only consider this when \(s,t\) overlap, since otherwise the direct edge wins. Write a 3-edge path as \(s-a-b-t\). Each disjoint adjacency gives a left/right orientation. If both intermediate intervals are on the left side of their respective endpoint intervals, one is already disjoint from both endpoints: if \(L_s\le L_t\), then \(a\) (left of \(s\)) is left of \(t\); otherwise \(b\) (left of \(t\)) is left of \(s\). The analogous statement holds when both are on the right, using the larger right endpoint. Hence same-side orientations cannot occur in an optimal 3-edge path, as dropping the other positive-weight intermediary improves it.
- **Remaining two-intermediate orientations:** The only necessary forms are:
  - \(a\) left of \(s\), \(b\) right of \(t\): cost `pref[Ls-1] + suff[Rt+1]`. Since \(s,t\) overlap, \(L_s\le R_t\), so automatically \(R_a<L_s\le R_t<L_b\), hence \(a,b\) are adjacent.
  - \(a\) right of \(s\), \(b\) left of \(t\): cost `suff[Rs+1] + pref[Lt-1]`. Overlap gives \(L_t\le R_s\), so \(b,a\) are automatically adjacent.
  This eliminates the apparent need for any 2D pair/range-minimum structure.
- **Preprocessing:** `pref[x]` stores the minimum weight among all intervals with right endpoint at most `x`; `suff[x]` stores the minimum weight among all intervals with left endpoint at least `x`. Endpoints are at most \(2N\), so preprocessing is \(O(N)\) after arrays of size \(O(N)\).
- **Query formula:** Answer is \(W_s+W_t+\min(\text{direct},\text{one-left/right},\text{two-cross-1},\text{two-cross-2})\), ignoring unavailable terms. If all are unavailable, print `-1`.
- **Complexity:** \(O(N+Q)\) time and \(O(N)\) memory.
