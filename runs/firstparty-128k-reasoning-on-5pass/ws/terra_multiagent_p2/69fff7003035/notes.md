- **Insertion recurrence:** For an old permutation of length `n`, insert a new value `y` of decimal length `d` at a cut whose old suffix has numeric value `z` and digit length `L`. If the old concatenation is `x`, the new concatenation is `10^d*x - (10^d-1)*z + y*10^L`. Summing this directly requires aggregate suffix statistics over all cuts.
- **Why simple insertion aggregates do not close:** Let an element at old position `i` contribute `v*10^(suffix length)`. Summing over all insertion cuts changes its position-dependent multiplicity: insertion before it occurs `i` times, while insertion after it occurs `n-i+1` times and multiplies its decimal shift by `10^d`. Thus updating the ordinary sum requires a position-weighted sum; updating that requires higher powers of positions, creating an unbounded hierarchy. A direct constant-statistic insertion recurrence is therefore not suitable.
- **Fixed-element decomposition:** Fix value `x`. If a subset `S` of the other `N-1` values is after `x`, its contribution multiplier is `10^(sum digit lengths in S)`. There are `|S|!(N-1-|S|)!` permutations with exactly that suffix set. Hence the required multiplier sum is `sum_S 10^(length(S)) |S|!(N-1-|S|)!`.
- **Integral identity:** Over the modulus, `|S|!(N-1-|S|)! = N! * integral_0^1 t^(N-1-|S|)(1-t)^|S| dt`. Therefore the fixed-element multiplier sum equals `N! * integral_0^1 product_{i != x}(10^(len(i)) + (1-10^(len(i)))t) dt`.
- **Digit-length grouping:** All values with the same decimal length have the same excluded product. There are at most six groups for `N <= 200000`. Let `P(t)=product_j (a_j+b_j t)^c_j`, with `a_j=10^j`, `b_j=1-10^j`, and `c_j` the group count. For group `j`, divide `P` by `a_j+b_j t`, integrate the quotient coefficientwise, multiply by `N!` and by the sum of values in that group.
- **Efficient polynomial coefficient construction:** Avoid NTT. Let `Q(t)=product_j(a_j+b_j t)` and `R(t)=sum_j c_j*b_j*product_{h!=j}(a_h+b_h t)`. Logarithmic differentiation gives `Q(t)P'(t)=R(t)P(t)`. Comparing coefficients computes all `P[0..N]` in `O(N*g)`, where `g <= 6`.
- **Complexity:** `O(N*g)` time and `O(N)` memory; modular inverses through `N+1` are precomputed linearly.
