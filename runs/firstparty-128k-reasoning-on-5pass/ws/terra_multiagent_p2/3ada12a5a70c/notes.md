- **Validation:** The supplied examples produce `2`, `1`, and `1` respectively under the cyclic-gap checker. Corner-only cases such as `side = 1`, all four corners, `k = 4` return `1`. The corner ownership mapping assigns perimeter positions `0, side, 2*side, 3*side` to bottom-left, bottom-right, top-right, and top-left without duplicates.
- **Upper bound:** For any `k >= 4` selected boundary points, their `k` cyclic perimeter gaps sum to `4 * side`. At least one gap is at most `4*side/k <= side`. That consecutive pair has Manhattan distance equal to that gap, so the optimum is at most `side`.
- **Perimeter reduction:** For a tested `d <= side`, two boundary points have Manhattan distance below `d` exactly when their shortest cyclic perimeter separation is below `d`. A perimeter arc shorter than `side` crosses at most one corner and has length equal to Manhattan distance. Points on opposite sides have Manhattan distance at least `side`, so they cannot violate a threshold `d <= side`.
- **Feasibility equivalence:** A selected set has all pairwise Manhattan distances at least `d` iff every cyclic gap between consecutive selected points is at least `d`. If any pair violates the threshold, its short perimeter arc is below `d` and contains a consecutive selected-pair gap below `d`. Conversely, if all consecutive cyclic gaps are at least `d`, every arc between selected points contains at least one such gap.
- **Greedy checker:** For each potential first point, `nxt[i]` gives the earliest later point at perimeter distance at least `d`. Repeatedly taking this earliest point produces the earliest possible final selected point after `k-1` jumps. Therefore, if this greedy chain cannot leave a final wraparound gap of at least `d`, no selection beginning at that start can. Trying every original start covers every feasible cyclic selection.
- **Complexity:** Sorting costs `O(n log n)`. Each feasibility test costs `O(n * k)` time and `O(n)` memory; `k <= 25`. Binary search has `O(log side)` iterations.
