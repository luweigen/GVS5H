- **Matching interpretation:** Every removal sequence induces a noncrossing matching of removed original positions. For even length, every element is matched; for odd length, exactly one element survives.
- **Even segment optimum:** For an even-length segment, choose its smaller half as negative and larger half as positive. Their sum difference is achievable because any balanced sequence of `+` and `-` signs has an adjacent opposite-sign pair; remove such a pair repeatedly. Since every selected positive value is at least every selected negative value, each removed pair contributes exactly positive minus negative. Thus the optimum is `sum(larger half) - sum(smaller half)`.
- **Odd survivor decomposition:** The survivor cannot lie inside a matched pair: such a pair would need to remove its endpoints only after its interior is gone, but the survivor would remain inside forever. Therefore all pairs lie wholly to the left or wholly to the right of the survivor. Both sides must have even length, so the survivor is at an odd 1-indexed position.
- **Formula for odd N:** Maximize `value(prefix[1..p-1]) + value(suffix[p+1..N])` over odd positions `p`.
- **Heap statistic:** Maintain a max-heap for the lower half and a min-heap for the upper half, along with their sums. Keep lower size equal to upper size or one larger. At every even processed length, heap sizes are equal and `sum_upper - sum_lower` is the required statistic.
- **Suffixes:** Process the reversed array with the same prefix routine. A suffix beginning at position `s` corresponds to a reversed prefix of length `N-s+1`.
- **Complexity:** `O(N log N)` time and `O(N)` memory.
- **Edge cases:** For even N, no survivor exists and the answer is the full-array statistic. For N=2, this directly yields the sole absolute difference. Equal values naturally produce zero.
