- **Validation:** The path normal forms were checked against explicit small-graph Dijkstra reasoning. No missing case was found. The formulas enumerate all shortest paths because positive weights allow taking an induced shortest path.
- **Induced-path bound:** In a disjointness graph of intervals, an induced path cannot have six vertices. Consecutive disjointness directions must alternate; assuming five edges then the required overlap of nonconsecutive vertices produces contradictory endpoint inequalities. Thus an optimal path has at most four edges, hence at most three internal vertices.
- **Normal forms:** For overlapping endpoint intervals, possible optimums are: one common neighbor on the far left/right; two internal vertices on opposite sides; or three internal vertices with both outer internal vertices on the same side and the middle one on the opposite side. Mixed-side three-internal paths contain a chord and reduce to a cheaper two-internal path.
- **Three-internal left formula:** For middle vertex `b` with `x=L_b`, the least internal cost is `W_b + pref[min(L_s,x)] + pref[min(L_t,x)]`. Splitting at `min(L_s,L_t)` and `max(L_s,L_t)` gives the three segment-tree values used by `left0`, `left1`, and `left2`.
- **Three-internal right formula:** Symmetrically, for `x=R_b`, the cost is `W_b + suff[max(R_s,x)] + suff[max(R_t,x)]`, represented by `right0`, `right1`, and `right2`.
- **Repeated selected vertices:** A range-minimum expression can theoretically choose the same vertex for two internal roles. Such a choice is never beneficial: it implies a valid path with fewer positive-weight internal vertices, already covered by the one- or two-internal cases.
- **Complexity:** Preprocessing takes `O(N log N)`, each query takes `O(log N)`, and memory is `O(N)`.
- **Integer safety:** Segment trees use signed 64-bit arrays with `INF = 4e18`, safely below the signed 64-bit limit and above all possible path weights.
