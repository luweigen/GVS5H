- **Reduction to colored selection:** Assign every chosen pair one coordinate attaining its maximum. Its price then equals the sum of that coordinate over its two cakes. Thus the problem equals selecting exactly `2K` cakes, assigning each selected cake one of three colors, and maximizing the assigned coordinate sum, subject to every color count being even. Conversely, cakes of each color can be paired arbitrarily, and each resulting pair price is at least its assigned-coordinate sum, proving equality.

- **Baseline solution:** For cake `i`, let `M_i=max(X_i,Y_i,Z_i)` and choose one maximizing coordinate as its baseline color. Ignoring parity, select the top `2K` values `M_i`; this has value `B`. Let the XOR of selected baseline colors be `t`, encoding colors as `0,1,2` in `F_2^2`. Since the number selected is even, `t=0` exactly when all three color counts are even.

- **Operations relative to baseline:** A recoloring of selected item `i` from baseline color `p` to `c` has parity delta `p xor c` and nonnegative cost `M_i-value[i][c]`. An exchange drops selected item `i` of baseline color `p`, adds unselected item `j` assigned color `c`, and has delta `p xor c` and nonnegative cost `M_i-value[j][c]`.

- **Constant correction proof:** Any feasible solution can be decomposed into exchanges for dropped/added items and recolorings of remaining selected items. Every operation has nonnegative cost. Remove operations with zero parity delta. In `F_2^2`, any multiset of nonzero deltas with XOR equal to nonzero target `t` contains a submultiset of at most two operations with XOR `t`: if a minimal such submultiset had at least three elements, its three possible nonzero group elements would force a nonempty zero-XOR subset. Removing all other operations preserves feasibility and cannot increase cost. Therefore an optimum differs from the unconstrained baseline by at most two recolorings/exchanges.

- **Candidate compression:** For a recoloring parity delta, retaining its three cheapest choices suffices: another operation can forbid at most one selected item. For an exchange type `(baseline color p, new color c)`, cost separates into removable-source cost `M_i` and destination benefit `value[j][c]`. Retain the three smallest baseline `M_i` for each `p` and the three largest outside coordinate values for each `c`; after excluding one conflicting source and one conflicting destination, an optimum remains among these. Generate all resulting constant-many operations and test one operation or every compatible pair.

- **Complexity:** Sorting by `M_i` costs `O(N log N)` per test case. All other work is linear plus a constant-sized operation enumeration. Total `N` is at most `10^5`.
