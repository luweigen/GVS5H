Normalize each conflicting pair as `(min(a,b), max(a,b))`, since a subarray includes both exactly when its left boundary is at or before the smaller endpoint and its right boundary reaches the larger endpoint. Sweep the right endpoint `r` from `1` to `n`, maintaining the largest forbidden left endpoint among all pairs ending at or before `r`; this determines the number of valid subarrays ending at `r`. To evaluate removing one pair efficiently, track both the largest and second-largest active left endpoints, including which pair provides the unique maximum. The baseline valid-subarray count plus the accumulated gain from removing each candidate pair yields the answer.
