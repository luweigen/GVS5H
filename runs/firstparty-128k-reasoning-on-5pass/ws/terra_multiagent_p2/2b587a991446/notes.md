- **Counterexample to the previous formula:** The endpoint buffer condition is false. Smallest counterexample: \(N=3\), edges \((1,2),(1,3)\), \(S=1,T=2\). The previous code sees shortest distance 1 and `deg(S)=2`, hence returns \(2\cdot1+2=4\). The true answer is `-1`.
- **Why the counterexample is impossible:** Initially A is at the star center 1, B is at leaf 2, and vertex 3 is empty. The only possible first move is A: \(1\to3\); then B must move \(2\to1\). From there, moving B back to 2 forces the original relative situation, while A cannot enter 1 while B occupies it. A side branch at an endpoint cannot let the pieces pass each other.
- **Correct buffer criterion:** A \(2d+2\) exchange maneuver works when an internal vertex of a shortest \(S\)-\(T\) path has a spare neighbor. It is enough to test a vertex \(v\ne S,T\) with `distS[v] + distT[v] == distS[T]` and degree at least 3. The third neighbor is outside a chosen shortest path and serves as the temporary buffer.
- **Two-path case:** Two internally vertex-disjoint \(S\)-\(T\) paths permit direct opposite traversal, with operation count equal to the sum of their lengths. The minimum total length is obtained by sending two min-cost-flow units through a vertex-split graph, with capacity 1 on internal vertices and capacity 2 at \(S,T\).
- **Final formula:** Minimum of the two-disjoint-path min-cost-flow result, if it exists, and \(2\operatorname{dist}(S,T)+2\), if an internal shortest-path buffer exists. Otherwise output `-1`.
- **Complexity:** Two BFS traversals are \(O(N+M)\). The min-cost flow sends only two units, each using Dijkstra over \(O(N+M)\) residual arcs, for \(O((N+M)\log N)\) time and \(O(N+M)\) memory.
