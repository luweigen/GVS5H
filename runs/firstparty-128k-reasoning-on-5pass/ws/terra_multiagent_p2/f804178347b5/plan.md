Model the repeated majority operations as a ternary tree whose leaves are the bits of `A`. For every subtree, compute the minimum number of leaf flips needed to make that subtree’s resulting value equal to `0` and equal to `1`. At an internal node, to force a target bit, at least two of its three children must be forced to that target; choose the two cheapest such children, while the remaining child can keep its original resulting value at zero additional cost. The answer is the cost to force the root to the opposite of its original value.
