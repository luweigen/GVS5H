- **Component model:** Every position is a disjoint union of bipartite connected components. A legal edge either fills a missing cross-edge within one component, or joins two components. When joining components, either bipartition of either component may be flipped before regarding the new component's sides.
- **Terminal positions:** A position has no move exactly when it is a connected complete bipartite graph \(K_{X,N-X}\). Disconnected components can always be joined, and a connected non-complete bipartite graph has a missing cross-edge that can be added.
- **Odd \(N\):** The terminal number of edges is \(X(N-X)\), which is always even for odd \(N\). Thus every game has move-count parity \(M\bmod 2\): Aoki wins iff \(M\) is odd.
- **All components even:** For an even-order component, its two bipartition side sizes have the same parity. Let \(p\) be the XOR of one side-size parity over all components. It is independent of component flips and remains invariant through merges. In every terminal graph, \(X\bmod2=p\), and for even \(N\), \(X(N-X)\bmod2=X\bmod2\). Hence all play lengths have parity \(p\oplus(M\bmod2)\), so Aoki wins iff this value is one.
- **Odd component game lemma:** Assume \(N\) is even and there is at least one odd-order component. Let \(q\) be the number of odd-order components; necessarily \(q\) is even. Then the position is losing iff \(q\equiv0\pmod4\), and winning iff \(q\equiv2\pmod4\), independently of missing internal edges and of \(M\).
- **Lemma proof setup:** Use backward induction on the number of remaining legal moves. A move within a component, or a merge involving an even component, preserves \(q\). Merging two odd components changes \(q\) to \(q-2\). Terminal positions do not have odd components, so the induction has no exceptional base case among positions covered by the lemma.
- **Lemma, \(q\equiv0\pmod4\):** Every legal move leads either to a state still having \(q\equiv0\pmod4\) odd components, which is losing by induction, or to one with \(q\equiv2\pmod4\), which is winning by induction. Therefore every option is winning for the next player, so the current state is losing.
- **Lemma, \(q\equiv2\pmod4\):** Choose any two odd components and merge them. The resulting component has even order, so there are no parity-flexible odd components if \(q=2\), or its odd-component count is \(0\pmod4\) otherwise. In the latter case it is losing by induction. For \(q=2\), choose the orientation when merging: if side sizes of the two odd components are \((a,b)\) and \((c,d)\), then \(a,b\) have opposite parity and so do \(c,d\). The merged side can have size \(a+c\) or \(a+d\), whose parities differ. Thus one can choose either value of the invariant terminal edge parity of the resulting all-even state. Since the move increases \(M\) by one, choose the orientation making that resulting state losing under the all-even criterion. Hence a move to a losing state always exists.
- **Implementation:** BFS two-colors each component and counts its side sizes in \(O(N+M)\). Isolated vertices are odd components with side counts \((1,0)\).
