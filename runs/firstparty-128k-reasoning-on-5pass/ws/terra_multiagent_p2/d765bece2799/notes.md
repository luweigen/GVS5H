- **Validation result:** The implementation matches both supplied examples: `([1,2,3], 2) -> 20` and `([1,-3,1], 2) -> -6`.
- **Exhaustive verification:** Compared the monotonic-stack solution with a direct brute-force implementation over small arrays containing negative values, zero, positive values, and duplicates, for every `k` from `1` through `n`. The results agree.
- **Bounded extension formula:** For an extremum index, let `L` and `R` be the numbers of possible left and right extensions, including the index itself. The desired number is the count of pairs `x in [1,L]`, `y in [1,R]` with `x+y-1 <= k`. With `u=x-1`, `v=y-1`, and `q=k-1`, inclusion-exclusion gives `T(q)-T(q-L)-T(q-R)+T(q-L-R)`, where `T(s)=(s+1)(s+2)/2` for `s >= 0` and zero otherwise.
- **Duplicate ownership:** Minima use previous strictly smaller plus next smaller-or-equal boundaries; maxima use previous strictly larger plus next larger-or-equal boundaries. Equal-valued subarrays are therefore assigned exactly once, to their rightmost eligible extremum index.
- **Edge cases checked:** `k=1` counts only singleton subarrays; `k=n` permits all subarrays; all-equal arrays assign each subarray once; negative extrema preserve signs correctly; arrays of length one return twice their only value.
- **Complexity:** Four monotonic stack scans and O(1) contribution work per index give `O(n)` time and `O(n)` memory. Python integer arithmetic safely handles the maximum possible total.
