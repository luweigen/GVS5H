Let \(N=m\cdot n\). For any unordered pair of cells, its contribution appears in exactly \(\binom{N-2}{k-2}\) valid arrangements, since the remaining \(k-2\) pieces can occupy any other cells. Compute the sum of Manhattan distances over all cell pairs by separating row and column contributions: the row part is \(n^2\sum_{i<j}(j-i)\), and the column part is \(m^2\sum_{i<j}(j-i)\). Use \(\sum_{i<j}(j-i)=d(d-1)(d+1)/6\) for dimension \(d\), then multiply by the combination modulo \(10^9+7\).
