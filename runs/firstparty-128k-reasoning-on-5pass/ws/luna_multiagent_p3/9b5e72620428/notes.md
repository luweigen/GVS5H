- **Model:** Let the final common sum be \(S\). A known \(A\)-value \(a\) paired with a known \(B\)-value \(b\) is valid exactly when \(a+b=S\). Any unknown entry can be assigned the required non-negative complement.
- **Required fixed-fixed pairs:** Let \(p\) and \(q\) be the numbers of known entries in \(A\) and \(B\). If \(t\) known \(A\)-entries are paired with known \(B\)-entries, then the remaining \(p-t\) known \(A\)-entries must use unknown \(B\)-positions, of which there are \(N-q\). Hence \(t\ge p+q-N\). Define \(K=p+q-N\).
- **Case \(K\le0\):** No known-known pair is required. Choose \(S\) at least as large as every known value and use unknown entries for all necessary complements, so the answer is always Yes.
- **Case \(K>0\):** For a fixed sum \(S\), values \(a\) and \(b=S-a\) can form at most \(\min(\mathrm{count}_A[a],\mathrm{count}_B[b])\) fixed-fixed pairs. Summing this over all value pairs with \(a+b=S\) gives the maximum possible number of such pairs.
- **Candidate sums:** It is sufficient to examine sums \(a+b\) of known values. Also require \(S\ge\max(\text{known }A,\text{known }B)\), ensuring all complements are non-negative.
- **Correctness:** If some sum reaches at least \(K\) fixed-fixed pairs, select \(K\) of them. The remaining known \(A\)-entries fit into the \(N-q\) unknown \(B\)-positions, and the remaining known \(B\)-entries fit into the \(N-p\) unknown \(A\)-positions. Conversely, every valid arrangement must contain at least \(K\) fixed-fixed pairs, all having the same sum \(S\), so the corresponding accumulated count must reach \(K\).
- **Complexity:** With \(d_A,d_B\) distinct known values, the algorithm uses \(O(d_A d_B)\subseteq O(N^2)\) expected time and \(O(d_A d_B)\) worst-case auxiliary memory.
