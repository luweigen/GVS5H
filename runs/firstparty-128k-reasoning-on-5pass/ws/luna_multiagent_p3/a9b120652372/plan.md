Model each initial piece as an ordered labeled particle; labels never cross, and after any sequence of operations their displacement values are nonincreasing from left to right. Thus, assign every initial piece to a final occupied position in \(B\), allowing multiple pieces per position, while ensuring all positions marked 1 in \(B\) receive at least one piece. For a fixed operation count \(K\), test whether such an order-preserving assignment exists with each displacement in \([-K,K]\) and nonincreasing; feasibility is monotone in \(K\), so binary search the minimum \(K\). Derive an \(O(N)\) greedy or scan-based feasibility check so the total complexity is \(O(N\log N)\) per aggregate input size.
