- **Bounded-hop theorem:** With positive vertex weights, a minimum-weight path is induced: a chord permits deleting the vertices strictly between its endpoints, strictly reducing the vertex-weight sum. An induced path in a co-interval graph has at most four edges, hence at most three intermediate vertices.
- **Proof of the four-edge bound:** Orient an edge `u-v` as `u<v` when `R_u<L_v`. In an induced path, two consecutive oriented edges cannot have the same direction, because transitivity would make the vertices two steps apart adjacent, producing a chord. Thus directions alternate. A hypothetical induced six-vertex path has, after reversal, pattern `v0>v1<v2>v3<v4>v5`. The required nonedges force `v1` to overlap `v4`, while the disjointness and overlap requirements through `v0,v3,v2,v5` imply `R_v1 < L_v0 <= R_v3 < L_v2 <= R_v5 < L_v4`, contradicting that overlap. Therefore no induced `P6` exists.
- **Canonical path costs:** For overlapping query intervals, direct travel is impossible. A two-edge path uses one interval wholly left of both or wholly right of both. A relevant three-edge path has one intermediate left of one endpoint and the other right of the other endpoint; the two orientations give `left(s)+right(t)` and `right(s)+left(t)`. Any same-direction case has a chord and is dominated by a shorter path.
- **Four-edge structure:** Alternation leaves two mirrored cases. In the left-valley/right-peak case, choose `a,c,b` satisfying `R_a<L_s`, `R_c<L_t`, and `L_b>max(R_a,R_c)`. Its symmetric counterpart is obtained by coordinate reflection.
- **Valley recurrence:** Let `A[r]` be the least weight of an interval with right endpoint `r`, `P[r]=min_{u<=r}A[u]`, `F[r]=min weight of an interval with left endpoint >r`, and `D[r]=A[r]+F[r]`. Splitting by `R_a>=R_c`, the valley value is `min(prefixH[min(x-1,y-1)], P[y-1]+min(D[r], y<=r<=x-1))`, where `prefixH[k]=min_{r<=k}(A[r]+F[r]+P[r])`. Swapping `(x,y)` covers `R_c>R_a`.
- **Complexity:** Endpoint range is at most `2N`. Preprocessing two reflected structures is `O(N + N log N)` due to segment-tree construction; each query is `O(log N)` and uses constant many range minima. Memory is linear.
- **Walk duplicates:** The formulas can select repeated vertices in degenerate cases. Such a selection is a valid walk, and removing its cycles gives a strictly cheaper valid path because weights are positive. Since every true optimum is also included by the induced-path theorem, these harmless candidates cannot decrease the reported minimum below the true distance.
