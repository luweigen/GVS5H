- **Functional graph structure:** Each edge is `i -> A_i`, and the inequality is `x_i <= x_{A_i}`. Every directed cycle forces all its vertices to have one equal value. Vertices not on cycles form incoming rooted trees attached to cycle vertices.
- **Cycle detection:** Indegree peeling identifies cycle vertices. Initialize indegrees as counts of incoming functional-graph edges, repeatedly remove indegree-zero vertices, and decrement the indegree of their parent. Vertices left with positive indegree are exactly cycle vertices.
- **Bottom-up order:** The peeling removal order is leaf-to-root for all off-cycle trees. When processing an off-cycle vertex, all of its off-cycle children have already contributed to it.
- **DP meaning:** For a non-cycle vertex `v`, let `dp_v[t]` be the number of assignments in its attached off-cycle subtree when `x_v=t`. Then `dp_v[t]` equals the product, over children `u`, of `sum(dp_u[s] for s<=t)`. Thus a child's contribution to its parent is the prefix sum of its DP array.
- **Memory reduction:** `acc[v]` stores only the product of already received child contributions. Once an off-cycle node is processed, its array is converted to a prefix-sum array and transferred into its parent, then released. Arrays use `array('I')`, keeping worst-case storage practical for `N,M <= 2025`.
- **Cycle aggregation:** For one directed cycle, all cycle vertices share a value `t`. At each `t`, multiply the accumulated attached-tree contributions of all its cycle vertices, then sum over `t=1..M`. Multiply these totals for all disjoint cycles.
- **Complexity:** The total work is `O(NM)` and memory is `O(NM)` in the worst case, with compact 32-bit arrays.
