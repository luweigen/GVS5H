- **Internal skeleton reduction:** In any alkane, call its degree-4 vertices internal vertices. They form a connected subtree because a degree-1 vertex cannot lie between two other vertices on a path. Every internal vertex must have original-tree degree at least 4, and its degree within the internal subtree is at most 4.
- **Conversely constructing an alkane:** Let \(S\) be any connected subtree consisting only of vertices whose original degree is at least 4, with every vertex having at most 4 neighbors in \(S\). For each \(u \in S\), select exactly \(4-d_S(u)\) neighbors outside \(S\) as leaves. Enough exist because \(\deg_T(u)\ge4\). An outside vertex cannot be adjacent to two vertices of \(S\), since that would create a cycle in the original tree. Thus these selected vertices are distinct leaves and produce an alkane.
- **Size conversion:** If the internal skeleton has \(I\) vertices, it has \(I-1\) internal edges. The number of attached leaves is \(\sum_{u\in S}(4-d_S(u))=4I-2(I-1)=2I+2\). Therefore the alkane size is \(I+(2I+2)=3I+2\). This includes \(I=1\), yielding the valid 5-vertex star centered at any original vertex of degree at least 4.
- **Directed DP message:** Restrict attention to eligible vertices, meaning original degree at least 4. For a directed eligible edge \(u\to v\), let its message be the maximum internal-skeleton size on the \(u\)-side when edge \(uv\) is selected. Vertex \(u\) already uses one skeleton edge toward \(v\), so it may select at most three other incident eligible branches. Hence the message is \(1+\) the sum of the largest three incoming messages from eligible neighbors other than \(v\).
- **Rerooting:** Root the original tree arbitrarily. `down[u]` computes the message from \(u\) to its parent using eligible child messages. During preorder, `up[u]` provides the parent-to-\(u\) message. At each eligible vertex, collect messages from all eligible neighbors; the best skeleton rooted there is \(1+\) the sum of its largest four messages. For each eligible child, its `up` message is \(1+\) the largest three collected messages excluding that child.
- **Correctness:** Every connected degree-at-most-4 eligible skeleton has a topmost vertex under the chosen root, and its selected neighbor branches are represented by the corresponding directed messages. Conversely, each recurrence combines disjoint tree-side branches, so it constructs a valid skeleton. Maximizing all top vertices gives the maximum \(I\), and the final answer is \(3I+2\).
- **Complexity:** Each adjacency is processed a constant number of times. Maintaining only the best four values costs constant time per adjacency, giving \(O(N)\) time and \(O(N)\) memory.
