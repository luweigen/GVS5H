- **Characterization:** Valid black cells form a downward-closed set in the coordinate order: if \((x,y)\) is black, then every \((x',y')\) with \(x' \le x\) and \(y' \le y\) is black. This follows from row black-prefix and column black-prefix conditions.
- **Feasibility criterion:** Fixed cells are feasible iff there is no forced black cell \((x_b,y_b)\) and forced white cell \((x_w,y_w)\) satisfying \(x_b \ge x_w\) and \(y_b \ge y_w\). Such a black cell would force the white cell black by downward closure.
- **Sufficiency:** If no such black/white dominance pair exists, color exactly the downward closure of all forced black cells black. No forced white lies in this closure, and the resulting set satisfies both prefix conditions.
- **Algorithm:** Sort black points by row descending. Build a prefix maximum of black columns. For each white point \((x_w,y_w)\), binary-search how many black points have row at least \(x_w\). If the maximum column in that prefix is at least \(y_w\), a conflicting black point exists.
- **Equality:** Both comparisons are inclusive. Same-row black to the right of a white conflicts; same-column black below a white conflicts; the binary search uses `bisect_right` to include black cells with equal row.
- **Complexity:** Sorting and all binary searches take \(O(M \log M)\) time, with \(O(M)\) memory. The value of \(N\) is not otherwise relevant.
