- **Bipartite characterization:** Every connected component has a bipartition `(a,b)`. A legal edge inside a component joins opposite colors; a legal edge between components always exists and merges those components.
- **Terminal states:** Eventually all components are merged and every opposite-side pair is present. The final graph is complete bipartite with side sizes `s` and `N-s`, so it has `s*(N-s)` edges.
- **Fixed terminal parity:** If `N` is odd, `s*(N-s)` is always even. If `N` is even and every component has even order, the parity of `s` is unaffected by component orientations. Thus all maximal plays have the same length parity, computed using any orientation.
- **Two components:** When exactly two components remain, the merging player can choose either relative orientation. The two possible terminal edge counts are `(a+x)*(b+y)` and `(a+y)*(b+x)`. Aoki wins iff at least one leaves an odd number of moves. If both are even, all other internal moves preserve the same eventual parity, so Takahashi wins.
- **At least three components with variable parity:** This occurs exactly when `N` is even and at least one component has odd order. Exhaustive minimax on small states shows the correct invariant is simply the parity of the current edge count: Aoki wins iff `M` is odd. In particular, an empty graph on three or four vertices is losing for Aoki, disproving the former `M + component_count` rule.
- **Complexity:** Bipartite coloring and counting take `O(N+M)` time and `O(N+M)` memory.
