- **Boundary encoding:** Each boundary point is mapped to a unique clockwise perimeter coordinate in `[0, 4*side)`. The branches assign bottom, right, top, and left-side points while avoiding duplicate corner representations.
- **Distance characterization:** For two boundary points, Manhattan distance equals the smaller of their two cyclic perimeter separations. Since `k >= 4`, any feasible answer is at most `side`; therefore requiring every consecutive selected perimeter gap to be at least `D` is equivalent to requiring all pairwise Manhattan distances to be at least `D`.
- **Feasibility test:** For a fixed first point, repeatedly choose the earliest point at least `D` farther around the perimeter. This greedy sequence leaves the largest possible closing gap. If it reaches `k` points and the final cyclic gap is at least `D`, the candidate is feasible.
- **Wraparound:** The sorted perimeter list is doubled by adding the perimeter length to every coordinate. Index bounds `current < start + n` prevent selecting the same point twice.
- **Binary search:** The answer lies in `[0, side]`. The necessary condition `k * D <= 4 * side` is checked inside feasibility, while binary search finds the largest feasible value.
- **Validation:** Corner-only, same-side, opposite-side, adjacent-side, and wraparound configurations are handled by the perimeter representation. Exhaustive small-square subset comparisons against direct subset brute force are consistent with the consecutive-gap characterization.
- **Complexity:** Each feasibility check costs `O(nk)` after linear next-pointer construction. Overall complexity is `O(nk log side)` with `O(n)` auxiliary memory.
