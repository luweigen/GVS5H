- **Characterization:** In a retained Snowflake Tree, choose its center \(c\). Every intermediate vertex must be a neighbor of \(c\). If a neighbor \(v\) is used as an intermediate, one edge connects it to \(c\), leaving at most \(\deg(v)-1\) possible leaf neighbors.
- **Fixed center and leaf count:** For a fixed positive \(y\), exactly neighbors with \(\deg(v)-1\ge y\) can be intermediates. Selecting every such neighbor is optimal, because each selected intermediate contributes \(y+1\) retained vertices: itself and \(y\) leaves. The retained size is therefore \(1+k(y+1)\).
- **Prefix optimization:** Sort the capacities \(\deg(v)-1\) of all neighbors of each center in descending order as \(a_1,a_2,\ldots\). If exactly \(k\) intermediates are selected, the largest feasible common \(y\) is \(a_k\), giving size \(1+k(a_k+1)\). Scanning all positive capacities evaluates every possible optimum.
- **Validity of selecting leaves:** Since the input is a tree, branches from distinct neighbors of the center are disjoint, and no unwanted edges exist among selected intermediate or leaf vertices. Any unused vertices can be deleted.
- **Complexity:** For a center of degree \(d\), sorting costs \(O(d\log d)\). Summed over all centers this is \(O(N\log N)\), with \(O(N)\) memory.
- **Edge cases:** Capacities zero correspond to leaf neighbors and cannot support the required positive \(y\), so they are excluded. Since \(N\ge3\), some valid Snowflake Tree is always obtainable.
