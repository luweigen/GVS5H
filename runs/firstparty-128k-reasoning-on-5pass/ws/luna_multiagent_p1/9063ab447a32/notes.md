- **Marginal costs:** Buying the first, second, third, ... units of product \(i\) has marginal costs \(P_i, 3P_i, 5P_i,\ldots\). Since these are nondecreasing, an optimal purchase consists of globally cheapest marginal costs.
- **Threshold count:** For threshold \(X\), product \(i\) contributes \(q_i=((X//P_i)+1)//2\) units, because these are exactly the odd multiples of \(P_i\) not exceeding \(X\).
- **Threshold cost:** The cost of those units is \(P_iq_i^2\). Therefore \(C(X)=\sum_iP_iq_i^2\) is monotone in \(X\), allowing binary search for the largest feasible integer \(X\) with \(C(X)\le M\).
- **Upper bound:** Let \(p_{\min}\) be the smallest \(P_i\), and \(k=\lfloor\sqrt{M/p_{\min}}\rfloor+1\). The \(k\)-th unit of that product costs \(p_{\min}(2k-1)\), and \(k^2p_{\min}>M\), so this threshold is infeasible.
- **Tie handling:** After selecting all marginals at most \(X\), let \(T=\min_i P_i(2q_i+1)\). Maximality of \(X\) implies \(T=X+1\); otherwise the cost would remain unchanged at \(X+1\), contradicting maximality. Every marginal equal to \(T\) can be bought at the same price, and the remaining budget buys \(\lfloor(M-C(X))/T\rfloor\) of them. This count is smaller than the number of available marginals tied at \(T\), because including all of them would exceed \(M\).
- **Complexity:** \(O(N\log U)\) time, where \(U\) is the binary-search threshold range, and \(O(N)\) memory for the input.
