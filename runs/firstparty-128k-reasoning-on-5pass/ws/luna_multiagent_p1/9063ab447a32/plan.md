View each product’s purchases as a sequence of marginal costs \(P_i,3P_i,5P_i,\ldots\); the minimum cost for any number of units is obtained by taking the globally cheapest marginal costs. For a threshold \(X\), product \(i\) contributes \(q_i=\left\lfloor (X/P_i+1)/2\right\rfloor\) units, with total cost \(P_iq_i^2\). Binary-search the largest threshold whose total cost is at most \(M\), using capped arithmetic to avoid overflow. All remaining affordable units have the same next marginal cost \(T=\min_i P_i(2q_i+1)\), so add \(\lfloor(M-\text{cost})/T\rfloor\) units.
