- **Configuration characterization:** A state is an ordered pair `(a,b)` with `a != b`; one move changes exactly one coordinate along an edge. Direct BFS over configurations is only used for validation on small graphs, not in the submitted algorithm.
- **Direct case:** If there are two internally vertex-disjoint `S-T` paths, the minimum answer is the minimum total length of such paths. A unit-capacity vertex-split min-cost flow computes this value. The paths can be executed without collision by advancing each piece toward the opposite endpoint and leaving the final endpoint moves until the other piece has cleared it.
- **No-disjoint-path case:** By the vertex form of Menger’s theorem, an optimal successful schedule must use a temporary parking vertex `x`. If A parks at `x`, the phases are exactly `S -> x` avoiding `T`, `T -> S` avoiding `x`, and `x -> T` avoiding `S`. Their lengths are summed. The symmetric choice of parking B is equivalent after reversing the schedule.
- **Endpoint avoidance:** The first and third phases must explicitly avoid the currently occupied endpoint. This handles the `S-T` plus endpoint-attached branch counterexample, where an unblocked-distance formula incorrectly reports a finite answer.
- **Distance computation:** For vertices outside a fixed shortest `S-T` path `P`, the middle distance avoiding `x` remains the shortest-path distance `d`. For internal vertices of `P`, replacement-path distances are computed using BFS trees rooted at `S` and `T`, with range minimum updates over path-index intervals.
- **Validation:** Exhaustive ordered-configuration BFS on small connected graphs, including trees, articulation branches, endpoint-attached branches, cycles, triangles, and the two-vertex graph, agrees with the direct-flow branch or parking formula. In particular, trees with no usable branch return `-1`, while a branch attached to an internal path articulation gives the expected three-phase route.
- **Complexity:** The min-cost flow, BFS traversals, and replacement-path processing require `O((N+M) log N)` time and linear memory.
