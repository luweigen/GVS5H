- **Cycle encoding:** Let \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\) describe the direction of cycle edge \(\{i,i+1\}\), with \(x_i=1\) meaning the edge points toward \(i+1\). At vertex \(i\), the cycle contribution to indegree is \(x_{i-1}+1-x_i\).
- **Spoke contribution:** If \(s_i=0\), the ordinary degree is exactly \(x_{i-1}+1-x_i\). If \(s_i=1\), it may additionally increase by 1 depending on the spoke direction.
- **Apex degree is redundant:** The sum of all indegrees equals the number of edges \(N+\sum s_i\). Therefore \(d_N\) is uniquely determined by \(d_0,\ldots,d_{N-1}\). It suffices to count distinct ordinary-vertex degree strings.
- **Relation automaton:** For a fixed output prefix, maintain the relation consisting of all pairs \((a,b)\), where \(a=x_{N-1}\) is the initial cycle variable and \(b\) is the current variable after processing the prefix. The relation is a 4-bit mask.
- **Initialization and cycle closure:** Initially the relation is the identity \(\{(0,0),(1,1)\}\), since the initial variable is \(x_{N-1}\). After all \(N\) vertices are processed, accept exactly masks containing \((0,0)\) or \((1,1)\), enforcing that the final current variable equals the initial one.
- **Determinization:** For every relation mask and possible degree output, the next relation mask is uniquely obtained by extending every pair through both possible next cycle variables and the possible spoke choices. Thus dynamic programming over at most 16 states counts each distinct degree string once.
- **Complexity:** \(O(N)\) time and \(O(1)\) memory, with a small constant factor; all counts are modulo 998244353.
