Represent the valid parenthesis sequence as an ordered rooted forest, where each matching pair is a node and its children are the immediately nested pairs. Reversing a valid substring corresponds to reversing a consecutive block of siblings and replacing every sibling by its mirrored/reachable form; using length-one and length-two operations, siblings can be independently transformed and arbitrarily permuted. Therefore, each node is characterized by the multiset of equivalence classes of its children, while its number of reachable encodings is computed by counting ordered sequences formed from those child classes. Parse the tree bottom-up, assign canonical IDs to child-class multisets, compute the reachable-count formula with factorials and modular combinations, and output the count for the top-level forest.
