- **Particle ordering:** Each operation is a nondecreasing, 1-Lipschitz map on positions. Therefore pieces never cross, and the distance between any two pieces never increases.
- **Final assignment:** Let initial occupied positions be `p[0] < ... < p[n-1]` and final occupied positions be `q[0] < ... < q[m-1]`. Every initial piece must end at some final occupied position, and the assigned final positions form a nondecreasing sequence. Since every `q` must be occupied, the initial pieces split into `m` nonempty contiguous groups, with group `j` ending at `q[j]`.
- **Distance condition:** If all pieces move for at most `K` operations, every piece assigned to `q[j]` must lie in `[q[j]-K, q[j]+K]`.
- **Gap condition:** If the boundary between groups `j` and `j+1` is after initial position `p[r-1]`, then the two neighboring source pieces are `p[r-1]` and `p[r]`. The 1-Lipschitz property requires `q[j+1]-q[j] <= p[r]-p[r-1]`.
- **Feasibility for fixed `K`:** For each final position, compute the interval of initial indices that can be assigned to it under the distance bound. Scan group boundaries from left to right, always choosing the earliest boundary satisfying both the next group's lower index bound and the required source gap. Choosing the earliest valid boundary cannot hurt future groups.
- **Minimum operations:** The existence of a sequence of operations realizing any such monotone assignment with maximum displacement `K` follows by repeatedly removing one operation: a suitable target can reduce every remaining displacement by at least one while preserving the monotone 1-Lipschitz constraints. Thus the answer is the minimum feasible `K`.
- **Complexity:** Each feasibility check is linear in the number of occupied positions. Binary search over `K` takes `O(N log N)` per test case, with total `N` at most `10^6`.
