- **Characterization:** A valid swap is equivalent to choosing two internally vertex-disjoint paths from \(S\) to \(T\). Executing one path with A and the other with B gives a valid sequence: B leaves \(T\), A traverses its path, then B finishes at \(S\).
- **Optimality:** Any valid sequence induces two such paths after removing unnecessary cycles and exchanging redundant path portions. Therefore the minimum number of moves equals the minimum total length of two internally vertex-disjoint \(S\)-\(T\) paths, i.e. the shortest cycle containing both terminals.
- **Flow reduction:** Build a directed vertex-split network. Each nonterminal vertex has an in-to-out edge of capacity 1 and cost 0. Every undirected graph edge becomes two directed movement edges of capacity 1 and cost 1. Send two units from \(S_{\text{out}}\) to \(T_{\text{in}}\).
- **Terminal handling:** Edges entering \(S\) and edges leaving \(T\) are omitted, preventing paths from revisiting terminals. The terminals themselves have capacity 2 through the choice of source and sink nodes.
- **Algorithm:** Run successive shortest augmenting paths twice with Dijkstra and potentials. There are \(O(N+M)\) network edges, and only two augmentations, giving \(O((N+M)\log N)\) time and linear memory.
- **Impossibility:** If two units of flow cannot be sent, no two internally vertex-disjoint terminal paths exist, so the answer is \(-1\). This includes the single-edge graph and path graphs.
