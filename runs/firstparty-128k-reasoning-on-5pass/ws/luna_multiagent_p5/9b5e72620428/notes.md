- **Fixed common sum:** Let the final common value be `S`. A known `B_i = y` requires its paired A-value to be exactly `S-y`; similarly, a known A-value `x` placed at an unknown-B position requires `B_i = S-x`.
- **Necessary lower bound:** We need `S >= max(known A-values)` and `S >= max(known B-values)`, because every final value must be non-negative. Thus `S` must be at least `minimum_sum`.
- **How many known A-values must match known B-values:** Let `K` be the number of known A-values and `U_B` the number of unknown B-values. At most `U_B` known A-values can be placed at unknown-B positions. Therefore at least `q = max(0, K-U_B)` known A-values must be paired with known B-values.
- **Fixed-sum matching capacity:** For a chosen `S`, an A-value `x` can match only a B-value `S-x`. With frequencies `countA[x]` and `countB[S-x]`, the number of such matches is `min(countA[x], countB[S-x])`. Summing this over all `x` gives the maximum number of known-known matches for `S`.
- **Efficient computation:** Iterate over every distinct known A-value `x` and known B-value `y`, and add `min(countA[x], countB[y])` to the capacity of sum `x+y`. There are at most `N` distinct values in each sequence, so this takes `O(N^2)` time and `O(N^2)` space in the worst case.
- **Sufficiency:** If a sum `S >= minimum_sum` has capacity at least `q`, use enough matching known A/B pairs to cover the required `q` pairs. Put every remaining known A-value at an unknown-B position and assign its B-value as `S-A_i`, which is non-negative. Fill all remaining positions using previously unknown entries.
- **Special cases:** If there are no known B-values, choose any sufficiently large `S`; all B-values can be assigned accordingly. If `q=0`, all known A-values fit into unknown-B positions, while unknown A-values fill the known-B positions, so `S=minimum_sum` works.
- **Edge checks:** Duplicate values are handled by frequency minima, not by counting all pair products. Candidate sums below `minimum_sum` are rejected. The logic also covers all-unknown entries and cases where one sequence has no unknown positions.
