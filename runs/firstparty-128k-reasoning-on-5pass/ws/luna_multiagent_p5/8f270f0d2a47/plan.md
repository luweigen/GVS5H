Use digit DP to count beautiful numbers from 1 to a bound, then return `count(r) - count(l - 1)`. For each digit prefix, track the digit sum and the product of nonzero digits via its prime-exponent representation for primes \(2,3,5,7\); zero digits make the product zero, which is automatically divisible by any positive sum. At the end of each number, test whether the reconstructed product is divisible by its digit sum, while handling leading zeros separately so they do not affect the number. Since the bound is below \(10^9\), the digit sum is at most 81 and the DP state remains small.
