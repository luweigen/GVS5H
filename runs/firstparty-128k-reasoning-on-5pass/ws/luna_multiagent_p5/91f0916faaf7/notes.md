- **Prime-wise decomposition:** If the exponent of a prime \(p\) in \(S_i\) is \(e_i\), and its exponent in \(A_i\) is \(d_i\), then \(|e_i-e_{i+1}|=d_i\). Each edge with \(d_i>0\) therefore has two sign choices, while \(d_i=0\) has only one.
- **GCD normalization:** For a fixed prime, a sign assignment determines all exponents up to adding the same constant. The global gcd condition requires the minimum exponent to be zero, so each walk is shifted down by its minimum.
- **Reflected-walk DP:** `dp[h]` stores the sum of \(p^{\text{sum of normalized exponents}}\) for paths ending at normalized height `h`. Initially the path consists of one exponent equal to zero.
- **Transitions:** For an edge with exponent difference `d`, the upward transition goes to `h+d` and multiplies by \(p^{h+d}\). If `h>=d`, the downward transition goes to `h-d` and multiplies by \(p^{h-d}\). If `h<d`, the new minimum decreases by `d-h`; all `i` previous vertices shift upward, giving multiplier \(p^{i(d-h)}\), and the new height is zero.
- **Zero exponent edges:** When `d=0`, the two sign choices coincide and must be counted only once; the sole transition keeps height `h` and adds the new vertex contribution \(p^h\).
- **Factorization of the answer:** Choices for distinct primes are independent, and both sequence scores and gcd normalization factor by prime. Hence the total sum is the product of the prime-specific DP totals.
- **Complexity:** For prime \(p\), the DP uses \(O(ND_p)\) time and \(O(D_p)\) memory, where \(D_p=\sum_i v_p(A_i)\). The total factor-exponent bound is small under \(A_i\le1000\).
