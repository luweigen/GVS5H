Model a move as either opening an unmarked index (reducing the number of unmarked indices by one and adding A_i-1 delay tokens) or spending a delay token on an already marked index. The game ends when the last unmarked index is opened, so only the parity of the total available delay tokens and the parities of unopened A_i affect optimal play. Derive small cases: with one unopened index the player to move wins; with two, the player to move loses exactly when the delay-token count is even; with three, losing positions are all-even with even delay or all-odd with odd delay. For four or more unopened indices, prove by induction that a position is losing iff the delay-token parity equals the parity of the number of odd unopened A_i. Apply this to the initial state (delay parity 0) to get simple cases by N and the count of odd A_i.
