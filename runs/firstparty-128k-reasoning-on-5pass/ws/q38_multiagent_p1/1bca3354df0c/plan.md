The graph is bipartite, and every legal move keeps it bipartite, so the game ends only at a complete bipartite graph. The state can be reduced to connected components classified by the parities of their bipartition sizes, plus the parity of the total number of currently missing internal edges. Internal missing-edge moves form a path-like heap, so only their parity matters for the Sprague-Grundy outcome. Derive a finite SG recurrence over component types (even-even, odd-odd, odd-even non-isolated, isolated) with values 0, 1, or “at least 2”, and show the value depends only on small residues of the component counts. Then process the input graph to obtain these counts and the missing-edge parity, look up the SG category, and decide the winner by whether the combined SG is nonzero.
