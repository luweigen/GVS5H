Treat each removal as forming a noncrossing matching of original positions, with one unmatched element if N is odd. For any even-length segment, the maximum score equals the sum of its largest half minus the sum of its smallest half: this is an upper bound from assigning + to the larger element of each pair and - to the smaller, and is attainable by noncrossingly matching the top-half values against the bottom-half values. If N is odd, the remaining element must be at an odd 1-indexed position, so the sequence splits into an even-length prefix and an even-length suffix; the answer is the maximum over such splits of the two segment scores. Compute the even-segment score for all even prefixes and all even suffixes in O(N log N) using two heaps that maintain the lower and upper halves with their sums. For even N, output the global even-segment score; for odd N, combine prefix[t] + suffix[M-t] for t=0..M and output the maximum.
