- **Model:** A sequence of adjacent removals is exactly a noncrossing matching on original positions; for odd N one position remains unmatched. Any matched pair has opposite original parity because all positions between it are removed in pairs.
- **Even segment formula:** For 2k elements, each pair contributes larger minus smaller. Marking the larger element of each pair as plus gives k plus and k minus signs, so the total is at most the sum of the k largest values minus the sum of the k smallest values. This bound is attainable: mark the k largest values plus and the k smallest minus. Any balanced plus/minus word has an adjacent opposite pair; repeatedly deleting such pairs yields a noncrossing matching that pairs plus with minus. Since every plus value is at least every minus value, the total equals the bound.
- **Odd N:** The unmatched position must be odd, and no pair can cross it. Thus the array splits into an even prefix and an even suffix. The answer is the maximum over prefix length 2t of score(prefix) + score(suffix), t=0..M where M=N//2.
- **Implementation:** `even_scores(seq)` scans the sequence and maintains a max-heap `low` for the smaller half and a min-heap `high` for the larger half, plus `total` and `sum_low`. After each even prefix, the heaps have equal size and the segment score is `total - 2 * sum_low`. Rebalancing moves the boundary element when one heap is too large.
- **Prefix/suffix:** Compute `pref = even_scores(A)` and `suff = even_scores(reversed(A))`; reversed prefixes are original suffixes, and the score depends only on the multiset. For even N output `pref[N//2]`; for odd N output `max(pref[t] + suff[M-t])`.
- **Complexity:** O(N log N) time and O(N) memory. Python integers handle the maximum sum around 1.5e14.
- **Verification:** The samples produce 5, 14, and 0. Small N=3,4,5 cases were checked against exhaustive adjacent-removal DP, and the sign-reduction argument covers all cases.
