Use a digit DP to count beautiful numbers less than or equal to a bound, then return `count(r) - count(l - 1)`. Track the digit position, tightness, whether the number has started, the digit sum, and the prime-exponent signature of the digit product. Since the digit sum is at most 81, only prime powers up to `2^6`, `3^4`, `5^2`, and `7^2` matter, so cap the exponents of 2, 3, 5, and 7 at those limits. Also track whether any digit is zero, because a zero digit makes the product zero and therefore divisible by any positive digit sum. At the end, accept the number if its sum is positive and either the product is zero or the capped exponents cover the prime factorization of the sum.
