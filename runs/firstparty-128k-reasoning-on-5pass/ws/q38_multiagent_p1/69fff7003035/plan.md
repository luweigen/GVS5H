Use linearity over each value x: its contribution is x times the sum over permutations of 10^{total digits to its right}. For a fixed x of digit length d, the elements to its right form a subset S of the other N-1 numbers, and if |S|=k there are k!(N-1-k)! permutations, so the needed factor is ∑_k k!(N-1-k)! e_k, where e_k is the k-th elementary symmetric sum of values 10^{digit(y)} over y≠x. Since numbers have at most six digit lengths, group by digit length and compute the elementary symmetric polynomial P(z)=∏_{l}(1+10^l z)^{c_l} once. Recover the polynomial after removing one number of each digit length by dividing out (1+10^l z), then dot its coefficients with k!(N-1-k)! to get the factor for that digit length. Multiply by the sum of all N numbers of that digit length and sum over lengths modulo 998244353.
