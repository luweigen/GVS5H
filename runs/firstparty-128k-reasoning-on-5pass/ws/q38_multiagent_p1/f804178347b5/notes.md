- **Model:** The repeated majority operation is evaluation of a full ternary tree. Leaves are the input bits, and each internal node is the majority of its three children.
- **Single-cost DP:** `cost[i]` is the minimum number of leaf flips in node `i`'s subtree needed to change node `i`'s current value to the opposite value. Every leaf has cost `1`.
- **Recurrence:** For a node with child values `v0, v1, v2` and current majority `p`, making the parent `1-p` requires at least two children to become `1-p`. Child `k` contributes `cost[k]` if `v_k == p`, otherwise `0`. The parent cost is the sum of the two smallest of these three requirements.
- **Pattern shortcut:** There are only eight possible child-value patterns. If all three children equal `p`, the parent cost is the sum of the two smallest child costs. Otherwise exactly two children equal `p`, so the parent cost is the minimum of those two child costs. Precompute the majority value and the indices of the two matching children for each pattern.
- **Parsing:** Read all stdin bytes and split once to separate `N` from the bit stream. Translate ASCII `0`/`1` to byte values `0`/`1` and delete whitespace, storing the result in a `bytearray`. Pad or truncate to `3**N` for safety.
- **Array initialization:** Use `array('H', [1]) * expected` for leaf costs. Do not construct `array('H', ...)` directly from raw bit bytes, because unsigned short itemsize is 2 and the bit length is odd, causing a length error.
- **Bounds:** The flip cost of a node of height `h` is at most `2**h`. For `N = 13`, the root cost is at most `8192`, so unsigned short is safe.
- **Memory and complexity:** Keep only the current level's values and costs plus the next level. Total work is linear in `3**N`; for `N = 13` there are about `797,161` internal nodes.
- **Pitfalls:** The answer is `cost[0]`, not a minimum of two root costs. Do not parse the digits of `N` as bits. Handle both contiguous and space-separated bit input. Do not force the third child when only two children are needed to flip the parent.
