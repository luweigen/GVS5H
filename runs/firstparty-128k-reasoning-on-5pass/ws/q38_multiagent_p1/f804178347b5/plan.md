Model the given string as the leaves of a full ternary tree of depth N, where each internal node is the majority of its three children. For every node, compute two DP values: the minimum number of leaf flips in its subtree needed to make that node 0 and to make it 1. A leaf has costs (0,1) or (1,0) depending on its current bit. For an internal node, forcing it to value v requires at least two children to be v, so take the sum of the three children's cheaper costs plus the two smallest extra costs to force v. Process the tree bottom-up level by level, reducing the length by a factor of 3 each time, until one root pair remains. The current root value is the one with zero cost, and the answer is the opposite root cost.
