- **Reduction:** For each prime p, let c_i be the p-adic exponent of S_i and e_i = v_p(A_i). The reduced-ratio condition becomes |c_i - c_{i+1}| = e_i, and gcd(S_1,...,S_N)=1 becomes min_i c_i = 0. The score factorizes over primes, so the answer is the product of independent per-prime weighted sums.
- **Height bound:** If a p-height sequence contains a zero, every height is at most W = sum e_i, because from the zero position the height can increase by at most the total remaining step size. This makes finite DP possible.
- **Total-minus-positive DP:** For fixed p, T[h] stores the weighted sum of all paths ending at height h using heights 0..W, and P[h] stores the weighted sum of paths ending at h using only heights 1..W. Initialize T[h] = p^h for all h, and P[h] = p^h for h >= 1. Each step e moves h to h±e and multiplies by p^{new h}; P[0] is always 0. The desired per-prime contribution is sum(T) - sum(P), i.e. paths with at least one zero.
- **Gcd scaling optimization:** Let g = gcd(e_i). Any path that hits zero has all heights divisible by g, while paths with other residues never hit zero and cancel in T-P. Divide heights and steps by g, use base B = p^g, and bound W/g. This preserves the contribution and often reduces the state size.
- **Zero-run grouping:** Consecutive zero steps keep the height unchanged and multiply T[h], P[h] by B^{z h} for z zeros. Apply this in one pass instead of z separate passes.
- **Transition implementation:** For nonzero step a, split the target height range into regions where only h+a, both h-a and h+a, or only h-a is valid. This avoids per-index bounds checks.
- **Complexity:** The sum of original W over all primes is the total prime-factor multiplicity of all A_i, at most about 9000 under N <= 1000 and A_i <= 1000. After gcd scaling, total DP work is O(N * sum W/g), comfortably small. Memory is O(max W).
- **Checks:** N=2, A=9 gives 18. Sample 1 gives p=2 part 194 and p=3 part 4843476, whose product is 939634344. All A_i=1 gives 1. Small N=2 cases such as A=1,2,4,6,36 match direct coprime-pair enumeration.
