Treat red and blue balls as independent token systems that are coupled only by the fact that one operation on a box can advance both colors at once. For a color, a ball can only move along the permutation cycle of its color, so any initial ball not on the cycle containing X makes the goal impossible. On the X-cycle, a ball at distance d from X requires operations on the d boxes preceding X in reverse order, and all balls of that color reduce to one required chain from the maximum distance down to 1. Thus the whole problem becomes finding the shortest sequence of box operations that contains both the red chain and the blue chain as subsequences, i.e. the shortest common supersequence of two sequences. Compute the two chains by walking backwards from X using inverse permutations, then compute the SCS length as len(R)+len(B)-LCS(R,B), with LCS via LIS after mapping positions. Output -1 if any ball is on the wrong cycle, otherwise output the SCS length.
