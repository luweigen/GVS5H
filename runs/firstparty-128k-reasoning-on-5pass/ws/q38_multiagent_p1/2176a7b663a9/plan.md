The graph is the comparability graph of the interval order where an edge means one interval is completely to the left of the other. For any two vertices, if their intervals are disjoint the answer is direct; if they overlap, the distance is at most 3, and at most 2 when one interval contains the other. Hence each query reduces to \(W_s+W_t\) plus the minimum extra weight of either one common disjoint interval or two disjoint middle intervals. The one-interval case is just prefix/suffix minima over \(R\) and \(L\). The two-interval same-side case is a 2D offline minimum \(F(x,y)\) over intervals ending before \(x/y\), which can be answered with Fenwick trees, and the right-side case is obtained by reflecting coordinates.
