- **Core reduction:** In any valid alkane, let S be the set of degree-4 vertices. S is nonempty and connected: the unique path between two S vertices cannot contain a degree-1 vertex, so all internal vertices on it are also degree 4. Every leaf is adjacent to S. Each v in S has four selected neighbors, hence original degree at least 4. Conversely, if S is any nonempty connected subset of H = {v | deg_T(v) >= 4} and each v has at most 4 neighbors inside S, then S can be extended to an alkane by choosing 4 - k_v outside neighbors of v as leaves, where k_v is its number of S-neighbors. These outside neighbors are distinct in a tree. Since S is a tree, total vertices = |S| + sum_v (4 - k_v) = |S| + 4|S| - 2(|S|-1) = 3|S| + 2. Therefore the answer is 3 * max|S| + 2, or -1 if H is empty.
- **DP states:** Root the tree at vertex 0. For an H vertex u, g[u] is the maximum size of a connected S-subset inside u's subtree that contains u and assumes u's parent is already in S, so u may take at most 3 H children. f[u] is the maximum size when u is the highest vertex of S, so it may take at most 4 H children. Recurrences: g[u] = 1 + sum of the largest up to 3 g[child] values over H children; f[u] = 1 + sum of the largest up to 4 g[child] values over H children. The maximum possible |S| is max f[u] over H vertices.
- **Why top-k is enough:** Child subtrees are disjoint, and every feasible child branch has positive value. There is no other coupling between branches, so for a fixed u the optimal choice is simply the largest allowed number of child branches. Missing children contribute 0.
- **Implementation details:** Build 0-indexed adjacency. Mark H by original degree >= 4; if none, output -1. Use an iterative depth-first search to get a parent array and a preorder list; reversed preorder is a valid postorder. For each H vertex in postorder, scan its adjacency list, keep only children v with parent[v] == u and is_h[v], and maintain the top four g[v] values with four variables. Compute g[u] and f[u], update max_f. Finally print 3*max_f+2.
- **Edge cases:** N=1 or no degree-4-capable vertex gives -1. A single H vertex gives max_f=1 and answer 5 (a star with four leaves). A vertex with more than four H children is handled by taking only the best four in f and best three in g. Non-H vertices, including high-degree vertices not chosen in S, may serve as leaves; excluded H child subtrees contain no S vertices, so they do not conflict.
- **Complexity:** The traversal and postorder scan each edge a constant number of times, so time is O(N). Memory is O(N) for adjacency, parent, order, and g. Iterative traversal avoids recursion depth issues.
- **Samples:** Sample 1 has H = {2,3}, max_f=2, answer 8. Sample 2 has H empty, answer -1. Sample 3 has H path 5-1-9, max_f=3, answer 11.
