Model the operations as a noncrossing matching of adjacent elements, where each removed pair contributes the difference between its larger and smaller value. If \(k=\lfloor N/2\rfloor\), any solution uses exactly \(k\) larger endpoints and \(k\) smaller endpoints, so its score is at most the sum of the \(k\) largest values minus the sum of the \(k\) smallest values. Prove that this bound is always attainable by a noncrossing matching, including the possibility of leaving one element unmatched when \(N\) is odd. Therefore, sort the sequence and compute the difference between the sums of the largest and smallest \(k\) elements in \(O(N\log N)\) time.
