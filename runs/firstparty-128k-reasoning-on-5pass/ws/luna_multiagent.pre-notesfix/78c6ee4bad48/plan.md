Represent the configuration by the \(N-1\) consecutive gaps between sorted piece coordinates. An operation on four consecutive pieces reverses the three corresponding gaps, which is equivalent to swapping the two gaps at positions \(i\) and \(i+2\). Therefore, gaps at odd indices can be permuted arbitrarily among odd positions, and gaps at even indices independently among even positions, while the leftmost coordinate remains fixed. Since each gap contributes a coefficient \(N-j\) to the total sum, use the rearrangement inequality: sort each parity class’s gaps increasingly along increasing indices, then compute the resulting weighted sum.
