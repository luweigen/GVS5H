By linearity, count each unordered pair of cells’ Manhattan distance across all arrangements independently. For any fixed pair of cells, it appears in exactly \(\binom{mn-2}{k-2}\) arrangements, so compute the sum of Manhattan distances over all unordered cell pairs and multiply by this factor modulo \(10^9+7\). The total horizontal contribution is \(m^2\) times the sum of pairwise differences among \(n\) column indices, while the vertical contribution is \(n^2\) times the analogous sum among \(m\) row indices. Use \(\sum_{0\le i<j<q}(j-i)=q(q-1)(q+1)/6\), and compute the binomial coefficient efficiently with modular inverses.
