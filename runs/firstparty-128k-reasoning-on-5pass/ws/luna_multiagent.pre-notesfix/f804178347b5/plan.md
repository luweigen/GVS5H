Model the majority operation as a ternary tree whose leaves are the original bits and whose root is the final value. For every subtree, compute the minimum number of leaf changes needed to make its resulting bit equal to 0 or 1. At a leaf, the cost is 0 for its current value and 1 for the opposite value; at an internal node, combine the three child costs by choosing the two cheapest costs consistent with the desired majority. The answer is the minimum cost for the root to have the value opposite to its original computed majority.
