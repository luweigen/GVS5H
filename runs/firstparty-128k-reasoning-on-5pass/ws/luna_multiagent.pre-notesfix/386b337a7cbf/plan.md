Use dynamic programming over subsequences, tracking the alternating sum, the subsequence length parity, and every achievable product not exceeding `limit`. For each number, either skip it or append it to an existing subsequence; appending contributes `+num` when the current length is even and `-num` when it is odd, while flipping the parity. Handle the empty subsequence separately so it is never accepted as a result, and preserve product `0` states even though multiplying by zero loses prior product information. The answer is the largest product among non-empty states with alternating sum `k`, or `-1` if none exists.
