- **Gap transformation:** Let four consecutive sorted piece coordinates be \(a<b<c<d\), with consecutive gaps \(p=b-a\), \(q=c-b\), \(r=d-c\). The operation reflects \(b,c\) around \((a+d)/2\). Their new sorted order is \(a, a+d-c, a+d-b, d\), whose gaps are \(r,q,p\). Thus, on the full adjacent-gap array, an operation reverses three consecutive gaps, equivalently swapping gap positions \(i\) and \(i+2\) while leaving \(i+1\) unchanged.
- **Reachability:** Every operation swaps two gap positions of the same parity, so odd-indexed gaps and even-indexed gaps can never mix. Conversely, for each parity class, swaps \((i,i+2)\) are adjacent swaps after compressing that parity subsequence. Adjacent swaps generate every permutation, so odd gaps can be permuted arbitrarily independently of even gaps.
- **Objective:** If gaps are \(g_j=X_{j+1}-X_j\) for \(1\le j<N\), the coordinate sum is \(N X_1+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(N-j)g_j\). The leftmost coordinate stays fixed because every operation preserves its two outer coordinates, and all gap weights are positive.
- **Optimization:** Within either parity class, weights decrease as gap index increases. By the rearrangement inequality, assign the smallest gap to the largest available weight. Therefore sort odd-indexed gaps ascending and place them into odd positions from left to right; do the same for even-indexed gaps.
- **Complexity:** Computing gaps and summation is \(O(N)\); sorting both parity classes is \(O(N\log N)\). Python integers safely handle the maximum result.
