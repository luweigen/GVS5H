Model the repeated majority operations as a ternary tree whose leaves are the bits of `A`. For each subtree, compute the minimum number of leaf changes needed to make that subtree evaluate to `0` and to `1`. At an internal node, to force value `b`, at least two of its three children must be forced to `b`; choose the cheapest two corresponding costs. The answer is the cost at the root to force the opposite of its current evaluated value.
