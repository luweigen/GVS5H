- **Reduction:** Red balls always follow permutation `P`; blue balls always follow `Q`. A ball initially outside the cycle containing `X` for its own color can never reach `X`, so this is exactly the feasibility condition.
- **Relevant paths:** Cut the `P`-cycle containing `X` at `X`. The resulting forwarding path is `P[X], P[P[X]], ..., P^{-1}[X]`, followed by `X`. If the earliest red ball on this path is at index `k`, every vertex from `k` through the final vertex must be fired, in that order. Thus red requirements form one suffix. Blue requirements analogously form one suffix of the cut `Q`-cycle.
- **Relative-permutation viewpoint:** At a box `v`, firing sends red to `P[v]` and blue to `Q[v]`; after relabeling the red destination by `P^{-1}`, the blue destination is `(P^{-1} o Q)[v]`. Hence all interaction between the two color routes is determined by their common boxes and their different orders induced by the relative permutation. The direct implementation obtains those orders from the two cycle paths.
- **Scheduling characterization:** Each required color-path vertex must occur in the operation list in its forwarding order. Conversely, any common supersequence of the required red and blue vertex sequences clears all balls: when a vertex occurrence is reached, all required predecessor occurrences for the corresponding colors have already happened.
- **Minimum:** The minimum number of firings is the shortest common supersequence length: `len(red_seq) + len(blue_seq) - LCS(red_seq, blue_seq)`. Each path has no repeated vertex, so LCS equals LIS of red indices encountered while scanning the blue sequence.
- **Complexity:** Cycle extraction and feasibility checks are `O(N)`. LIS is `O(N log N)`. Memory is `O(N)`.
- **Validation:** This characterization can be exhaustively checked for small `N` by BFS over red/blue box-presence states, comparing the BFS minimum with the suffix/SCS formula for every pair of permutations, target, and initial bitmask configuration.
