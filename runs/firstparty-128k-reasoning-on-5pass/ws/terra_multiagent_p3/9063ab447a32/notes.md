- **Marginal-cost formulation:** Buying the \(k\)-th unit of product \(i\) increases its cost from \((k-1)^2P_i\) to \(k^2P_i\), so its marginal cost is \((2k-1)P_i\). These marginal costs are increasing within every product.
- **Optimality of cheapest marginals:** Any feasible purchase corresponds to taking a prefix of each product's marginal-cost sequence. Since each sequence is increasing, selecting globally cheapest marginal units automatically respects prefix requirements. Therefore an optimum consists of all marginal units below some boundary price and possibly some units at one equal boundary price.
- **Threshold count:** For integer threshold \(x\), product \(i\) has marginal costs at most \(x\) when \((2k-1)P_i\le x\). Let \(q=\lfloor x/P_i\rfloor\); the number of positive odd integers at most \(q\) is \(k=\lfloor(q+1)/2\rfloor\). Thus selected units for this product are `k = (x // p + 1) // 2`.
- **Threshold cost:** Taking these `k` units of product \(i\) costs exactly \(k^2P_i\), because they are its first `k` units. Summing gives `C(x) = sum(k_i^2 P_i)`. This is monotone nondecreasing in \(x\).
- **Binary search:** Search the largest integer \(x\in[0,M]\) such that \(C(x)\le M\). Zero is always feasible. It is sufficient to search only through \(M\), since any individual marginal cost greater than \(M\) cannot be purchased under the total budget.
- **Final boundary:** Let \(x\) be the largest feasible threshold and let remaining budget be \(R=M-C(x)\). If \(x<M\), threshold \(x+1\) must introduce at least one new marginal unit; otherwise \(C(x+1)=C(x)\) would also be feasible, contradicting maximality. Every newly introduced unit has identical cost \(x+1\).
- **Exact finishing step:** Let `boundary_count` be the number of products whose count at threshold \(x+1\) exceeds that at \(x\). Since each product's consecutive marginal costs differ by \(2P_i\ge2\), each product contributes at most one such unit. Buy `min(boundary_count, R // (x+1))` of them. This is optimal because every unselected marginal unit costs at least \(x+1\), and all available units at that minimum remaining cost are interchangeable.
- **Case \(x=M\):** No marginal unit of cost greater than \(M\) can be purchased, so the threshold selection is already optimal; no boundary step is needed.
- **Complexity:** Each feasibility calculation is \(O(N)\); binary search performs \(O(\log M)\) iterations. Total complexity is \(O(N\log M)\), with \(O(1)\) extra memory besides the input array.
- **Integer safety:** Python integers safely handle products such as \(k^2P_i\). The implementation exits a feasibility sum early after exceeding \(M\), which also reduces unnecessary work.
