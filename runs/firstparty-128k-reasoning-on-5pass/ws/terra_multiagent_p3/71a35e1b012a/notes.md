- **Brute-force validation:** Independently checked the solution logic against exhaustive operation-choice semantics: each selected operation covers either `[L,R]` or its complement, and every `3^M` assignment can be used as the oracle for minimum cost. No counterexample was found in the classification or reconstruction logic.
- **Cost bound:** Any achievable instance has an optimum of at most 3. Cost 1 requires an interval `[1,N]`. Cost 2 can be two complements of disjoint intervals, a complement plus an ordinary operation on a distinct containing interval, or two ordinary endpoint intervals.
- **Disjoint intervals:** Sorting by left endpoint and retaining the largest previous right endpoint finds a disjoint pair exactly when that right endpoint is smaller than the current left endpoint. Their complement operations cover all positions.
- **Containment construction:** For complement of `I` plus ordinary `J`, coverage is equivalent to `I` being contained in `J`. Intervals are processed by nondecreasing left endpoint. A Fenwick tree over reversed right ranks finds an interval with right endpoint at least the queried one. Storing two IDs per node permits excluding the queried interval itself.
- **Endpoint ordinary operations:** If no disjoint pair exists, an interval beginning at 1 and a distinct interval ending at N necessarily overlap, so their ordinary operations cover `[1,N]`.
- **Cost 3 construction:** After rejecting disjoint pairs, all intervals pairwise intersect. Let `A` have maximum left endpoint and `B` minimum right endpoint. Their complements leave the core `[L_A,R_B]`, which is contained in every interval. Any third distinct interval can cover it ordinarily. If the same interval supplies both extrema, use a different interval with second-smallest right endpoint.
- **Edge cases:** `M < 3` cannot use the cost-3 construction. `N = 1` is handled by the cost-1 full-interval test. All selected operation IDs are distinct.
- **Complexity:** `O(M log M)` time and `O(M)` memory.
