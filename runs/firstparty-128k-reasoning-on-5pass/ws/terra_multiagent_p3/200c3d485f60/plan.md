Observe that among the \(n-1\) adjacent pairs, exactly \(k\) must be equal and the remaining \(n-1-k\) must change. Choose which \(k\) transitions are equal, choose the first element in \(m\) ways, and for every changing transition choose any value different from the previous one in \(m-1\) ways. Thus the result is \(\binom{n-1}{k} \cdot m \cdot (m-1)^{n-1-k}\) modulo \(10^9+7\). Precompute factorials and inverse factorials up to \(n\) for combinations, using Fermat’s inverse under the prime modulus.
