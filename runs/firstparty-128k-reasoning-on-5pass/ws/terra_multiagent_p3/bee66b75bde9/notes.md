- **Shape formulation:** Let \(a_r\) be the number of black cells in row \(r\). The row condition says row \(r\) has black cells exactly in columns \(1,\dots,a_r\). The column condition is equivalent to \(a_1 \geq a_2 \geq \dots \geq a_N\): if a lower row has a black cell in a column, every row above must have one too.
- **Constraint inequalities:** A prescribed black cell \((x,y)\) requires \(a_x \geq y\). A prescribed white cell \((x,y)\) requires \(a_x < y\).
- **Exact feasibility criterion:** The constraints are feasible iff there is no prescribed black cell \((x_B,y_B)\) and prescribed white cell \((x_W,y_W)\) with \(x_W \leq x_B\) and \(y_W \leq y_B\). Such a black cell is weakly southeast of the white cell.
- **Why necessary:** If \((x_B,y_B)\) is black, northwest closure forces every cell \((x,y)\) with \(x \leq x_B\) and \(y \leq y_B\) to be black. Therefore a white cell weakly northwest of it is impossible.
- **Why sufficient:** If no such pair exists, take the northwest closure of all prescribed black cells as the black region. It satisfies both prefix conditions. If it contained a prescribed white cell, that white cell would be weakly northwest of one of the original black cells, contradicting the criterion.
- **Detection algorithm:** Sort constraints by row increasingly. Maintain the minimum column among all white constraints seen in rows up to the current row. For each row, add its white cells before testing its black cells, because equal rows also participate in the conflict condition. A black cell at column \(y\) conflicts exactly when the maintained minimum white column is at most \(y\).
- **Complexity:** Sorting costs \(O(M\log M)\); scanning costs \(O(M)\); memory is \(O(M)\). The value of \(N\) is never used for grid construction, so \(N\le 10^9\) is harmless.
