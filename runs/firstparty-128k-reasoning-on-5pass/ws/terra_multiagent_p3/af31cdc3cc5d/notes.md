- **Matching structure:** Every removal sequence induces a non-crossing matching of original indices. Conversely, an adjacent-removal order exists for every non-crossing matching by removing innermost pairs first.
- **Even-length segment optimum:** For an even segment of length \(2m\), any pairing has score at most the sum of its largest \(m\) values minus the sum of its smallest \(m\) values: orient each pair from its larger endpoint to its smaller endpoint, then the selected larger endpoints cannot sum to more than the global top half, and selected smaller endpoints cannot sum to less than the global bottom half.
- **Attainability for an even segment:** Label any \(m\) smallest elements Low and any \(m\) largest elements High, resolving equal-value ties arbitrarily. Every High value is at least every Low value. Repeatedly remove an adjacent unlike-label pair. Such a pair always exists while both labels remain; deleting it preserves equal label counts. Each removal gets exactly High minus Low, so total score is the upper bound.
- **Odd-length leftover restriction:** If one original element at index \(k\) is never removed, no matched pair can have endpoints on opposite sides of \(k\). Such a pair would enclose \(k\), and to become adjacent all enclosed elements, including the never-removed one, would have to disappear. Thus the left and right portions are independent segments, both needing even length. Therefore \(k\) must be odd in 1-based indexing.
- **Odd-\(N\) formula:** For odd \(N\), the answer is \(\max_{k\text{ odd 1-based}} F(A_1,\ldots,A_{k-1}) + F(A_{k+1},\ldots,A_N)\), where \(F\) is the even-segment optimum above. The two segment-optimal removal orders can be performed independently.
- **Even-\(N\) formula:** The entire sequence is one even segment, so the answer is simply total of largest \(N/2\) values minus total of smallest \(N/2\) values.
- **Heap computation:** Maintain a max-heap for the lower half and min-heap for the upper half, with upper size equal to lower size or one larger. Their sums give \(F\) whenever the processed prefix length is even. Run this on the original order for prefix values and on reversed order to obtain suffix values.
- **Complexity:** Each value is inserted and moved between heaps at most constantly many times, giving \(O(N\log N)\) time and \(O(N)\) memory. Python integers safely handle all scores.
