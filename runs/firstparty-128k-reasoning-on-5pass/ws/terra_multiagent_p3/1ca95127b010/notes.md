- **Invariant:** Label zeros and ones independently in their left-to-right order. A swap `0^X 1^Y -> 1^Y 0^X` moves every involved labeled zero right by exactly `Y` and every involved labeled one left by exactly `X`; the reverse move does the opposite. Hence every labeled zero's position modulo `Y` and every labeled one's position modulo `X` are invariant. Character counts are invariant too.

- **Sufficiency theorem:** For binary words with the same numbers of zeros and ones, equality of the ordered zero-position residues modulo `Y` and ordered one-position residues modulo `X` is sufficient as well as necessary for reachability under `0^X1^Y <-> 1^Y0^X`.

- **Normal-form proof framework:** Orient the relation as the rewrite rule `0^X1^Y -> 1^Y0^X`. It strictly decreases the inversion count, where an inversion is a pair consisting of a zero occurring before a one, by exactly `XY`; therefore rewriting terminates. Two redexes cannot overlap: a shifted occurrence beginning inside the initial `0^X` would require a zero at a position occupied by a one in the first occurrence, while an occurrence beginning at or after that one cannot begin with zero. Thus all simultaneously applicable rewrites are disjoint and commute. By termination and local confluence, every word has a unique oriented normal form.

- **Residue normal-form lemma:** If two oriented normal words have equal ordered zero residues modulo `Y` and equal ordered one residues modulo `X`, then they are identical. A useful proof is the first-disagreement argument. After a common prefix, suppose one normal word has the next zero while the other has the next one. The matching zero occurs later in the latter word by a positive multiple of `Y`, so the latter begins with at least `Y` consecutive ones. Likewise, the matching one occurs later in the former word by a positive multiple of `X`, so the former begins with at least `X` consecutive zeros. If the former has at least `Y` ones after this zero run, it contains a reducible `0^X1^Y`; if the latter has at least `X` zeros after its one run, it contains the corresponding discrepancy after exchanging roles. Otherwise both short runs force the same next disagreement one run later. Repeating this produces an alternating staircase of short runs. Since the words are finite, it must end at a boundary; at that endpoint equal total counts and the same residue congruences force the missing run lengths to be multiples of `X` and `Y`, contradicting that they were short. Hence a first disagreement is impossible.

- **Conclusion from normal forms:** Given two words with equal residue data, repeatedly apply the oriented rewrites to obtain normal forms. The residue data remains unchanged throughout. The residue normal-form lemma makes the two normal forms equal. Reversing the rewrites used for the target and following the rewrites used for the source gives a valid sequence of bidirectional operations from source to target. This includes frozen and boundary cases: if no operation fits, each word is already normal, so matching residue data forces literal equality.

- **Algorithm:** Compare zero counts, then scan each string in occurrence order. Store/check `index % Y` for each zero and `index % X` for each one. Equal lists imply `Yes`; otherwise `No`.

- **Complexity:** `O(N)` time and `O(N)` memory.
