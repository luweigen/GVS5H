For a target building i, only earlier buildings can block it, and checking the top of i is sufficient: if the segment from (0,h) to the top of i is above every earlier top, i is visible; if it is at or below any earlier top, every lower point on i is also blocked. This gives a threshold for each pair j<i: h must be greater than t_{j,i}=(X_i H_j-X_j H_i)/(X_i-X_j), the y-intercept of the line through the tops of j and i. Therefore all buildings are visible exactly when h exceeds the maximum of these intercepts over all pairs, and the requested answer is that maximum if it is nonnegative, otherwise -1. For points with increasing x, the maximum y-intercept of a line through any two points is attained by consecutive points, because for any three points the intercept of the outer pair lies between the intercepts of the two adjacent pairs. Hence an O(N) scan over consecutive buildings, using exact integer fraction comparison, solves the problem.
