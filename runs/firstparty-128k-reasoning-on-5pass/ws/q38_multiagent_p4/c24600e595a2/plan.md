Model each flip as a delta of +C_i or -C_i, and note the operation cost is the current total weighted sum. In an optimal sequence no coordinate can have a 0->1 flip before a later 1->0 flip, because removing that + flip and the next - flip of the same coordinate keeps the sequence valid and lowers every cost in between; hence only A_i=B_i=1 indices may be flipped twice (off then on), and all other indices at most once. For a fixed set of such borrowed indices, pairwise exchange gives all negative deltas (required 1->0 and borrowed off) in decreasing C, then all positive deltas (required 0->1 and borrowed on) in increasing C. An exchange argument shows that if a larger common-1 weight is not borrowed while a smaller one is, swapping them cannot increase the cost, so the optimal set is a prefix of the largest common-1 weights. Compute the cost for every prefix length k using weighted prefix sums of the negative and positive multisets, maintained with Fenwick trees as k grows, and take the minimum.
