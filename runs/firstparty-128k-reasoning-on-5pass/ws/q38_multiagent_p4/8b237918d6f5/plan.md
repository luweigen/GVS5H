The answer is the largest d for which vertices can be labeled 0..d with label(1)=0, label(N)=d, every edge u->v satisfying label(v) <= label(u)+1, and at most K edges going from i to i+1. Such a labeling is necessary and sufficient, because the forward edges can be chosen as the weight-1 edges and every 1-to-N path must contain at least d forward edges. For a fixed d, introduce binary variables z_{v,i} meaning label(v) >= i and build a layered graph with nodes (v,i) for i=1..d. Enforce monotonicity, the fixed labels of 1 and N, and the edge constraints with infinite-capacity arcs, while unit-capacity arcs from (v,i+1) to (u,i+1) for each original edge u->v count forward edges. Thus d is feasible iff the minimum s-t cut has capacity at most K, and feasibility is monotone in d so we can binary search d in [0, N-1].
