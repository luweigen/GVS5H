Model the repeated majority operation as a complete ternary tree whose leaves are the characters of A and whose internal nodes are the majority of their three children. For each node, compute two DP values: the minimum number of leaf flips in its subtree needed to make that node equal to 0 and to make it equal to 1. A leaf has cost 0 for its current bit and cost 1 for the opposite bit. For an internal node and a target bit v, at least two of its three children must be v; this can be computed by trying the three choices of which two children are forced to v, or equivalently by adding the two smallest penalties for forcing children to v. Process the tree bottom-up level by level, reducing the array length by a factor of 3 each time, and the answer is the root cost for the bit opposite the original root value, which is also max(dp0, dp1) at the root.
