- **Model:** The t-th unit of product i has marginal cost P_i(2t-1), and buying k units costs P_i k^2. The optimal purchase for any total count is the cheapest prefix of the merged marginal-cost multiset.
- **Threshold aggregates:** For a threshold x, product i contributes c=(x//P_i+1)//2 units, with total cost P_i c^2. S(x)=sum P_i c^2 is monotone in x.
- **Boundary method:** Binary search the smallest x with S(x)>M. Then all marginal costs <x have total cost S(x-1)<=M, and the remaining budget buys floor((M-S(x-1))/x) units of cost x. This is safe because S(x)>M implies the remaining budget is less than the total cost of all units with cost exactly x, so enough such units exist.
- **Upper bound:** After filtering P_i>M and sorting, p0=min P. Let k=isqrt(M//p0); hi=p0*(2k+1) is the (k+1)-th marginal cost of the cheapest product, so S(hi)>M. If the prefix sum of sorted P exceeds M at p, then S(p)>M, so hi can be tightened to min(hi,p).
- **Filtering:** Products with P_i>M can never be bought. If none remain, answer is 0. Removing them is safe even if they would create a full-set boundary above M; the cheapest-product bound still gives a valid filtered boundary and the final answer is unchanged.
- **Implementation:** Sort P, group duplicates with p<=hi into (p,cnt). The predicate over(x) iterates groups while p<=x, accumulates p*c*c*cnt, and returns True as soon as the total exceeds M. Binary search uses lo=0 and the valid hi. Finally compute exact count and cost at x-1 and print count+(M-cost)//x.
- **Complexity:** O(N log U), where U is at most about 9e13 under the constraints and often smaller after prefix tightening. Grouping duplicates and early cost capping keep the Python implementation fast.
- **Edge cases:** M smaller than all P gives 0; x=1 is handled by y=0; if S(1)>M, the answer is M when the relevant marginal costs are all 1; the 10^100 stock limit is irrelevant for M<=1e18.
