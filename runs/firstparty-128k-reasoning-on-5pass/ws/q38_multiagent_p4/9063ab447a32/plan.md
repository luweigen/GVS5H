Treat the t-th unit of product i as having marginal cost P_i(2t-1), so each product gives an increasing sequence of unit costs. The optimal purchase for any total count is to take the cheapest marginal units, so the answer is the largest count whose cheapest marginal costs sum to at most M. For a threshold x, let c_i(x)=floor((x//P_i+1)/2) be the number of marginal costs of product i not exceeding x; then G(x)=sum c_i and F(x)=sum P_i c_i^2 are the count and cost of all marginal costs <=x. Binary search the smallest x with F(x)>M, using sorted P and early stopping/capping to keep each check O(N). At that boundary, all costs <x are taken, and the remaining budget buys floor((M-F(x-1))/x) additional units of cost x. This yields the maximum total units in O(N log M) time.
