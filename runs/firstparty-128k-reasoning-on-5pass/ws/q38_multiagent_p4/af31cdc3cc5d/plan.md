A deletion sequence is a non-crossing matching of the removed pairs; if N is odd, one element remains unmatched. The unmatched element must have odd 1-based index, and no pair can cross it, so for each odd u the problem splits into an even prefix and an even suffix. For any even-length segment, the best non-crossing score equals the unconstrained best pairing: sort the segment and pair smallest with largest, giving sum(upper half)-sum(lower half); this can be proved by inductively removing an adjacent lower/upper boundary pair. Thus compute P[t] for every even prefix length t and S[t] for every even-length suffix, where each is the upper-half minus lower-half sum of that segment. Maintain the sum of the smallest half under insertions with an order-statistic structure (Fenwick tree over compressed values or two heaps), giving O(N log N). Answer is P[N] for even N, and max over 0-based even u of P[u]+S[u+1] for odd N.
