- **Model:** Label pieces in initial order. Pieces never cross, so a final configuration is a partition of the M initial pieces into K contiguous blocks placed at the 1-positions of B.
- **Gap matching:** Let a be initial 1-positions and b final 1-positions. Initial gaps g[t]=a[t+1]-a[t], B gaps L[j]=b[j+1]-b[j]. Each positive B gap must be matched to a later initial gap with g[t]>=L[j]; unmatched initial gaps are collapsed to 0. For a fixed parity l this is a subsequence match, and the earliest feasible gap is safe by exchange.
- **Parity:** Let C=b[0]-a[0], S=(a[-1]-a[0])-(b[-1]-b[0]). If n operations are used, l=(n-C) mod 2. A matched gap with g==L has zero reduction, so it is never touched and translates by ±1 every operation; its displacement b[j]-a[t] must have parity n. This is equivalent to (a[t]&1)==((a[0]+b[j]-b[0])&1)^l. If g>L no parity restriction is needed.
- **Count:** For feasible l, r=l^(S&1). The endpoint/span lower bounds give n=max(C+l, S+r-C), which is the same as the abs formula in the task. Take the minimum over l=0,1; if no l matches all B gaps, answer -1.
- **Necessary checks:** M<K impossible. K==1 answer max(b[0]-a[0], a[-1]-b[0]). For K>1, S<0 impossible.
- **Implementation:** Use 0-index positions; all differences and the parity equalities are invariant under a global shift. Extract ones with list comprehensions over bytes. Two greedy passes per case, total linear in sum N. Fast readline and joined output.
- **Edge cases:** Consecutive ones, boundary gaps, exact equal gaps, negative C, and M=K are handled by the same formulas; candidate is always nonnegative when feasible.
