Use a digit DP to compute F(n), the count of beautiful numbers from 1 to n, and return F(r) - F(l-1). Treat any number containing a zero as automatically beautiful because its digit product is 0. For numbers with no zero, track the digit sum and the capped prime exponents (2,3,5,7) of the digit product, since the sum is at most 81 and only these primes can divide a nonzero digit product. At the end, a no-zero number is beautiful exactly when the final sum's prime factorization is covered by the tracked exponents. The DP state is small (position, tight, started/zero flags, sum, capped exponents), so it easily handles n < 10^9.
