- **Validation harness:** Added `brute_force` and `run_validation` in the same file; the harness is guarded by `__main__` so the `Solution` class remains importable.
- **Brute force reference:** Enumerates every non-empty mask for small n, computes alternating sum with a running sign and exact product, and keeps the largest product <= limit. This naturally handles zeros and rejects the empty subsequence.
- **Random coverage:** 200 deterministic cases with n<=10, values 0..12, limit 1..40, and k within +/-12*n; 50 out-of-range k cases expecting -1.
- **Explicit edge cases:** provided examples; single element k=0 to ensure empty is not accepted; all zeros small and length 150; ones-only k=0, k=1, impossible k; negative k; limit smaller than positive values; zero fallback when a positive prefix exceeds limit but appending zero gives product 0; k outside 12*n; repeated 12 with product cap.
- **Large expected values:** [12]*150 with limit 5000 gives 144 for k=0 (length 2), 1728 for k=12 (length 3), and -1 for k=-12. [1]*150 gives 1 for k=0 or 1 and -1 for k=-1. [0]*150 gives 0 for k=0 and -1 for k=1.
- **DP invariants checked:** positive dp_even/dp_odd store no-zero subsequences by exact product and length parity; zero dp stores subsequences containing at least one zero; all dp stores every non-empty subsequence and is only used when a zero is appended.
- **Transition checks:** even length appends +x (left shift), odd length appends -x (right shift); x=1 snapshots each product to avoid reusing the same 1; x>1 iterates source products descending; x=0 uses old all/zero snapshots and adds singleton zero.
- **Zero fallback order:** positive products are scanned from limit down to 1 before considering product 0, so any valid positive product beats zero.
- **Pitfalls covered:** no empty state, no reuse of current element, zeros excluded from positive product states, zero-containing subsequences can still append later positives, product cap safe because positive products never decrease, and all dp is not product-capped so zero can rescue oversized positive prefixes.
