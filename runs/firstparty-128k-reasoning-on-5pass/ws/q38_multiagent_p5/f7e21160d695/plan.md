Observe that f(x, y) is the minimum bottleneck path value, i.e. the smallest edge-weight threshold at which x and y become connected. This is exactly the merge time of their components in Kruskal's algorithm on the graph. Represent each A occurrence as +1 charge and each B occurrence as -1 charge, and maintain the net charge of each DSU component. When an edge of weight w merges two components with opposite signs, match min(abs(charges)) pairs at cost w and replace the component charge by their sum; same-sign merges only add charges. This greedy is optimal because any pair that can be connected at weight w should be matched as early as possible, and all future matches would cost at least w. The result is O(M log M + N α(N)) time and O(N + M) memory, with a 64-bit integer answer.
