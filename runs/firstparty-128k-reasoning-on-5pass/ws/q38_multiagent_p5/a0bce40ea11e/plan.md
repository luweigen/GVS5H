Work over F_p and expand (B^p)_{i,j} as a sum over walks of p edges, treating each zero entry as an independent variable. For p > 2, summing over F_p^* kills a monomial unless every zero variable is used 0 or p-1 times, so surviving walks are either all-nonzero walks (exactly A^p) or walks with one zero edge repeated p-1 times and one nonzero edge. The one-nonzero-edge corrections are local: a zero loop contributes its nonzero incoming and outgoing edges, and when p = 3 a non-loop zero edge contributes its reverse nonzero edge. Every surviving monomial receives the same factor (-1)^K, where K is the number of zeros. For p = 2 there is only one possible B, namely the all-ones matrix, so the answer is N mod 2 in every cell. Thus compute A^p by fast matrix exponentiation, add the local corrections, multiply by (-1)^K modulo p, and output.
