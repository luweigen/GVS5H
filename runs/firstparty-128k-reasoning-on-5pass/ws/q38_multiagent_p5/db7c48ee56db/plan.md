We can exploit the guarantee \(\binom{N}{K} \le 10^6\), which implies \(\min(K, N-K) \le 11\). If \(K\) is the smaller side, enumerate all \(K\)-subsets directly and maximize their XOR. If \(N-K\) is smaller, enumerate all unchosen subsets of size \(N-K\) and use \(\text{total\_xor} \oplus \text{xor(unchosen)}\) as the XOR of the chosen complement. This keeps the number of enumerated combinations at most \(10^6\) and the per-combination work at most 11 XORs. Handle the edge case \(K=N\) by returning the XOR of all elements. The result is the maximum over all valid choices.
