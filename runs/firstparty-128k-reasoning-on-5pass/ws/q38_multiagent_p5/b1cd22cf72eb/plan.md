Model the final upper tooth lengths as \(a_i\). For a target common height \(H\), each \(a_i\) must lie in the interval \([\max(0, H-D_i), \min(H, U_i)]\), and adjacent upper lengths must differ by at most \(X\). For a fixed \(H\), feasibility can be checked by propagating the reachable interval of \(a_i\) from left to right, intersecting it with the allowed interval and the \(X\)-neighborhood of the previous reachable interval. Feasibility is monotone in \(H\): if \(H\) is feasible, then \(H-1\) is also feasible by clamping upper lengths to at most \(H-1\). Therefore, binary search the maximum feasible \(H\). The minimum cost is the total initial tooth length minus \(N \times H\). Use 64-bit integers and an \(O(N \log \max S)\) algorithm.
