The total cost is the sum over positions of C_i times the number of operations after which A_i is 1, so bits contribute independently except through the operation order. It is never useful to flip a bit more than once, because every extra flip increases the total number of operations and cannot reduce any bit's minimum number of 1-states. Thus the optimal sequence has exactly m = |A xor B| operations: first perform all 1-to-0 flips, then all 0-to-1 flips. For 1-to-0 bits, earlier flips reduce their future cost, so sort them by decreasing C; for 0-to-1 bits, later flips reduce their cost, so sort them by decreasing C and assign the smallest 1-count to the largest C. Add m times the total C of unchanged 1 bits, plus C_i*(rank) for 1-to-0 bits (0-based rank) and C_i*(rank+1) for 0-to-1 bits (0-based rank in decreasing C). This yields an O(N log N) solution with 64-bit arithmetic.
