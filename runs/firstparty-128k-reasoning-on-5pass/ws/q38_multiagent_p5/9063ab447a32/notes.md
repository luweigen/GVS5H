
## ideation
The key observation is that a quadratic purchase cost can be decomposed into increasing marginal costs: the t-th unit of product i adds (2t-1)P_i to the total cost. Because each product’s marginal costs are increasing, the cheapest way to buy any fixed number of units is to take the globally cheapest marginal units across all products. The core difficulty is that this multiset is the union of N infinite arithmetic progressions, the answer can be on the order of 10^11, and M can be as large as 10^18, so per-unit simulation is impossible. We need bulk counting and summing over these progressions, plus exact integer handling of ties and leftover budget. The enormous stock limit is irrelevant because the maximum possible answer is far smaller than 10^100.

The supplied plan is essentially a threshold-inversion method. For a candidate marginal-cost limit X, each product contributes exactly the number of odd multiples of P_i not exceeding X, and the cost of those units is the square of that count times P_i. This gives an O(N) monotone predicate. Binary searching the largest feasible X is natural. The subtle part is the final adjustment: after the largest feasible X, the smallest next marginal cost C must be X+1 (otherwise X+1 would also be feasible), and maximality also implies the remaining budget cannot afford all units of cost C. Therefore the leftover budget divided by C gives a valid number of additional units. Key pitfalls are choosing a safe but not too large upper bound, capping sums during the search to avoid huge integers, handling ties at the next marginal cost, and using the correct next-cost formula after c units. A continuous relaxation gives an upper bound by Cauchy, but it is not always tight, so it cannot replace exact counting.
