Any type-2 operations leave only the intersection of their intervals unset, so an optimal solution uses at most two type-2 operations, and a first/last reduction shows that if the goal is achievable at all, some solution has cost at most 3. Thus we check costs 1, 2, and 3 in increasing order and stop at the first feasible construction. Cost 1 is an interval [1,N]; cost 2 is exactly one of: two type-1 intervals whose union is [1,N], two type-2 intervals that are disjoint, or a type-1 interval containing a type-2 interval. If no cost 1 or 2 exists, then no two intervals are disjoint and no interval contains another, so after sorting by L the R endpoints are strictly increasing. When M>=3 in this case, the first three sorted intervals I1,I2,I3 give a valid cost-3 solution (type 2 on I2, type 1 on I1 and I3); otherwise the answer is -1, and the whole algorithm is O(M log M).
