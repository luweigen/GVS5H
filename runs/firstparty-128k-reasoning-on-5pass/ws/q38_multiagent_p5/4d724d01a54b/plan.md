Treat every adjacent swap as charged to the larger of the two swapped values. For a fixed value v, its rank among values at most v starts at v-m_v, where m_v is the number of smaller values initially to its right, and must end at v. Any swap between v and a smaller value changes this rank by one and costs at least the current rank, so the total cost charged to v is at least the sum of ranks v-m_v through v-1. This bound is attainable by processing values from N down to 1 and moving each value right to its final position, so the minimum cost is exactly sum_v sum_{k=v-m_v}^{v-1} k. Compute m_v for all v in O(N log N) with a Fenwick tree scanning the permutation from right to left. Then output the closed-form sum m_v*(2v-m_v-1)//2 using 64-bit arithmetic.
