- **Walk expansion:** The \((s,t)\) entry of \(B^p\) is the sum over length-\(p\) directed walks from \(s\) to \(t\), with each walk contributing the product of its selected entries.
- **Finite-field summation:** For every originally-zero matrix entry treated as an independent variable \(x\in\mathbb F_p^*\), \(\sum_{x\ne0}x^e\) equals \(-1\pmod p\) if \(p-1\mid e\), including \(e=0\), and equals \(0\) otherwise. Thus every surviving walk gets a common factor \((-1)^K\), where \(K\) is the number of original zeros.
- **Fixed matrix:** Let \(C\) equal \(A\) with all original zero positions retained as zero. Let \(D\) be diagonal with \(D_{ii}=1\) exactly when \(A_{ii}=0\).
- **Case \(p>3\):** A surviving walk either uses no zero edge, contributing \(C^p\), or uses one zero variable exactly \(p-1\) times and one fixed edge once. Since \(p-1\ge4\), repeated copies of a non-loop edge cannot be arranged with only one interrupting edge, so the repeated zero edge must be a diagonal loop. The two possible arrangements contribute \(CD\) and \(DC\). Therefore the answer is \((-1)^K(C^p+CD+DC)\).
- **Case \(p=3\):** Besides \(C^3+CD+DC\), an off-diagonal zero edge \(i\to j\) may appear twice in the pattern \(i\to j\), \(j\to i\), \(i\to j\). This contributes \(C_{j,i}\) to output entry \((i,j)\). Let \(E_{ij}=C_{ji}\) when \(i\ne j\) and \(A_{ij}=0\), otherwise zero. The answer is \((-1)^K(C^3+CD+DC+E)\).
- **Case \(p=2\):** Every zero has only replacement value \(1\), and every originally nonzero value is also \(1\). Thus \(B\) is the all-ones matrix and \(B^2\) has every entry equal to \(N\bmod2\).
- **Complexity:** Matrix exponentiation computes \(C^p\) in \(O(N^3\log p)\) time and \(O(N^2)\) memory. The exceptional additions cost \(O(N^2)\).
- **Validation:** The formulas cover scalar cases, zero-free matrices, diagonal-zero loops, and the \(p=3\) reverse-edge exceptional pattern. Sample 1 matches after including both \(DC\) and the off-diagonal exceptional term.
