- **Gap transformation:** Let the four selected consecutive coordinates be `a < b < c < d`, with gaps `(p, q, r) = (b-a, c-b, d-c)`. After reflection around `(a+d)/2`, the sorted interior coordinates become `a+d-c < a+d-b`, so the new gaps are `(r, q, p)`. Thus one operation swaps gap indices `i` and `i+2`.
- **Reachability:** Gap indices of different parity never mix. Within each parity class, swaps of positions differing by two are adjacent swaps in that class, so every permutation of odd-indexed gaps and every permutation of even-indexed gaps is reachable independently.
- **Objective:** If `g_j` is the one-indexed gap between positions `j` and `j+1`, then the coordinate sum is `N*X_1 + sum((N-j)*g_j for j=1..N-1)`. The leftmost coordinate stays fixed.
- **Optimization:** Coefficients `N-j` decrease as `j` moves right. To minimize the weighted sum within either parity class, assign smaller gaps to earlier positions with larger coefficients, and larger gaps to later positions. Therefore sort each parity group's gaps in ascending order and place them at its positions from left to right.
- **Complexity:** Constructing gaps and evaluating the answer is `O(N)`. Sorting the two parity groups is `O(N log N)`. Python integers safely handle all values.
- **Validation:** For sample 1, the gaps are `(4,2,3)`, and sorting odd gaps gives `(3,4)`, yielding answer `21`. For sample 2, sorted odd gaps are `(1,2,4)` and sorted even gaps are `(4,5)`, yielding `41`.
