- **Validation result:** No counterexample was identified. The implementation’s five frequency cases partition every valid subsequence according to the number of additional copies of the middle value.
- **Brute-force oracle:** For validation on small arrays, enumerate all index combinations `(a,b,i,d,e)` with `a < b < i < d < e`, count the five selected values, and accept exactly when `nums[i]` has frequency strictly greater than every other value’s frequency. Compare this count to the optimized result.
- **Provided samples:** The implementation gives `6` for `[1,1,1,1,1,1]`, `4` for `[1,2,2,3,3,4]`, and `0` for `[0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8]`.
- **Case partition:** With middle value `x`, there may be four, three, two, or one additional selected `x`. Four and three are automatically valid. With two extra `x`, `x` has frequency three and is also automatically a unique mode. With one extra `x`, the other three selected values must all be non-`x` and pairwise distinct; otherwise another value occurs twice and ties `x`.
- **One-extra formulas:** Let non-`x` left/right frequencies be `Lv`, `Rv`. The aggregate `cross = sum(Lv*Rv)`, `l2r = sum(Lv^2*Rv)`, and `lr2 = sum(Lv*Rv^2)` correctly remove invalid cross-side collisions when selecting three pairwise-distinct non-`x` values.
- **Sweep updates:** Removing the current middle item from the right and later inserting it into the left correctly updates pair counts and all three cross aggregates. In particular, removing a right frequency from `r` to `r-1` changes `sum(L*R^2)` by `L*(2r-1)`.
- **Complexity:** `O(n)` time and `O(d)` memory, where `d` is the number of distinct values.
