Model the repeated majority operation as a complete ternary tree whose leaves are the characters of `A`. For every node, compute the minimum number of leaf flips needed to make that node evaluate to `0` and to `1`. At a leaf, the cost is `0` for its current bit and `1` for the opposite bit. At each internal node, to force a target value, choose the cheapest way to make at least two of its three children have that target value; the answer is the cost for the root to become opposite its original evaluated value.
