- **Canonical form:** Remove any adjacent `0->1` followed by `1->0` on the same coordinate: deleting both flips preserves surrounding states and removes positive costs. By exchanging adjacent flips on distinct coordinates, a `0->1` followed by `1->0` is always worse than the reverse order, since the former pays an additional positive contribution from both involved weights. Thus all downward flips occur before all upward flips.
- **Per-coordinate behavior:** In canonical form, an initially/finally `0` coordinate is never flipped; a mandatory `1->0` coordinate flips down once; a mandatory `0->1` coordinate flips up once; an initially/finally `1` coordinate either stays on, or flips down once in the first phase and up once in the second phase. Therefore temporary disabling unchanged-one positions is necessary and sufficient to consider.
- **Ordering inside phases:** Among downward flips, exchanging two removals shows the larger weight must be removed first. Among upward flips, exchanging two additions shows the smaller weight must be added first.
- **Subset structure:** For any fixed number `k` of temporarily disabled unchanged-one positions, the optimal selected positions are the `k` largest weights. An exchange replacing a selected smaller unchanged-one weight by an unselected larger one cannot increase either phase cost: the larger weight is removed no later in descending removal order and restored no earlier in ascending addition order, while all rank shifts favor the replacement. Hence only prefixes after sorting stable-one weights descending need examination.
- **Cost formula:** If a downward phase has `m` weights with total `W` and `Q = sum(weight * rank)` in descending order, its cost is `m*S - ((m+1)*W-Q)`, where `S` is the original active-weight sum. If the upward phase has analogous ascending-order `Q`, its cost is `m*(S-W_down) + ((m+1)*W-Q)`.
- **Data structure:** Add stable-one weights from largest to smallest. Fenwick trees maintain counts and weight sums by compressed weight. On insertion, update the ranked weighted sum in `O(log N)` by accounting for the inserted rank and the ranks shifted after it. Evaluate every prefix, yielding `O(N log N)` time and `O(N)` memory.
