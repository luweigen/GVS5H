Construct \(A,M\) so that the multiplicative order of \(A\) modulo \(M\) is exactly \(N\). A simple universal construction is to use a prime divisor \(p\) of \(N\), write \(N=p^k d\) with \(\gcd(d,p)=1\), and choose a modulus involving \(p^{k+1}\) together with a factor whose order contributes \(d\). Workers should derive a construction guaranteed below \(10^{18}\), including the special case \(N=1\), and verify it efficiently for up to \(10^4\) tests. The implementation should factor each \(N\) in \(O(\sqrt N)\) or better overall practical time.
