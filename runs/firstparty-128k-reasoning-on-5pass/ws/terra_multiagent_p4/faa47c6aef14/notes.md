- **Normal-form counterexample:** It is false that every feasible instance has an optimum firing every non-\(X\) box at most once. Let \(N=3,X=3\), red balls at box 1, blue balls at box 2, \(P=(2,3,1)\), and \(Q=(3,1,2)\). Red must travel \(1\to2\to3\), while blue must travel \(2\to1\to3\). The forced sequence is to fire 1, then 2, then 1 again. Thus box 1 needs two firings and the optimum is 3.
- **Color-path feasibility:** Red balls always remain on their \(P\)-cycle, and blue balls always remain on their \(Q\)-cycle. Therefore every initially red-containing box must be in the \(P\)-cycle of \(X\), and every initially blue-containing box must be in the \(Q\)-cycle of \(X\). Otherwise the answer is \(-1\).
- **Required tasks:** For one color, all initial balls on the relevant permutation cycle merge while proceeding toward \(X\). If the farthest initial ball is at distance \(d\) before \(X\), exactly the \(d\) non-\(X\) boxes on that path must forward that color, in their forward path order. This is a single chain of color-tasks. A color never returns to a box before reaching \(X\), so it needs at most one forwarding task per box.
- **Dependency formulation:** There are a red chain and a blue chain. At a box present in both chains, its red and blue tasks may be performed by one operation exactly when those two chain positions can be synchronized. A set of synchronizations is feasible iff their order is consistent in both chains; otherwise it would create a directed precedence cycle. Hence feasible synchronizations are precisely common subsequences of the two required box sequences.
- **Minimum formula:** If red and blue required sequences have lengths \(r,b\), and their LCS length is \(L\), the minimum number of operations is \(r+b-L\). Each unpaired task requires one firing; each LCS pair shares one firing. Since boxes occur at most once in each sequence, LCS is computed as LIS after mapping red boxes to their positions in the blue sequence.
- **Complexity:** Constructing cycles and required paths is \(O(N)\). The LIS computation is \(O(N\log N)\), with \(O(N)\) memory.
