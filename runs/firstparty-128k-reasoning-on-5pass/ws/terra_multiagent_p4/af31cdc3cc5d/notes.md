- **Key correction:** The global rank formula `sum(k largest) - sum(k smallest)` is valid for even N, but not for odd N. For example, `[100, 50, 1]` permits only one adjacent removal, worth at most 50 rather than 99.
- **Even-length characterization:** For any even-length sequence, the optimum is the sum of its largest half minus the sum of its smallest half. Orient every removed pair from its larger member to its smaller member. Any solution has exactly `m` positive and `m` negative endpoints, yielding the rank-based upper bound. Label the largest half positive and smallest half negative. In any balanced positive/negative label sequence, an adjacent opposite-labeled pair exists; remove one repeatedly. Every removed pair has score positive minus negative, attaining the bound.
- **Odd-length decomposition:** Exactly one original element survives. No removal can cross that surviving element, so all elements to its left and right are removed independently. Both side lengths must be even, hence the survivor must have an odd 1-based index. The answer is the maximum over valid survivor positions of `F(left) + F(right)`, where `F` is the even-length characterization.
- **Data structure:** Maintain the lower half in a max-heap and upper half in a min-heap, along with their sums. After each insertion, rebalance so lower contains exactly half of all elements. Then `F = sum(upper) - sum(lower)`.
- **Complexity:** Compute all even prefix scores and even suffix scores using two heap passes. Time is `O(N log N)` and memory is `O(N)`.
