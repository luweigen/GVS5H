- **Boundary mapping:** Each boundary point is mapped to a clockwise perimeter coordinate in `[0, 4 * side)`: bottom uses `x`, right uses `side + y`, top uses `3 * side - x`, and left uses `4 * side - y`. The conditional order maps corners consistently.
- **Distance threshold:** For `k >= 4`, the answer cannot exceed `side`. Among four selected boundary points, some circular perimeter gap is at most `side`; for such a close pair, the Manhattan distance is also at most `side`.
- **Opposite-side pairs:** Opposite-side Manhattan distance is always at least `side`. Therefore, for all binary-searched thresholds `d <= side`, opposite-side pairs automatically satisfy the threshold. Exhaustive enumeration of small integer boundary configurations and all valid subsets found no counterexample to the perimeter-gap criterion.
- **Perimeter criterion:** For thresholds `d <= side`, two boundary points have Manhattan distance at least `d` exactly when both circular perimeter separations are at least `d`. Thus it is sufficient to require every consecutive selected perimeter gap, including the wraparound gap, to be at least `d`.
- **Greedy check:** For a fixed first point, repeatedly choosing the earliest point at least `d` farther around the perimeter minimizes every later selected position and maximizes the final closing gap. Hence failure of this greedy sequence implies failure for that starting point.
- **Circular handling:** The sorted perimeter coordinates are duplicated with one perimeter length added. A start at index `s` may use only indices below `s + n`, preventing reuse of the starting point after a complete revolution.
- **Binary search:** Feasibility is monotone in `d`, so binary search over `[0, side]` obtains the maximum valid minimum distance.
- **Complexity:** Each feasibility check costs `O(n log n + nk)`, with `O(n)` auxiliary memory. The total is `O((n log n + nk) log side)`.
