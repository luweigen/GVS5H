- **Cost characterization:** Let \(M\) be the maximum possible minimum building height over all grid paths joining the two queried blocks. The highest floor at which the entire journey can be made horizontally is \(\min(Y,Z,M)\). Descending from \(Y\) to that floor and ascending to \(Z\) costs \(Y+Z-2\min(Y,Z,M)\).
- **Lower bound:** In any valid journey, let \(L\) be the lowest floor reached. Every stair movement contributes to total vertical variation, so the stair count is at least \((Y-L)+(Z-L)=Y+Z-2L\). Horizontal movement at floor \(L\) connects the relevant blocks through buildings of height at least \(L\), hence \(L\le M\), proving the formula.
- **Maximum bottleneck preprocessing:** Give every adjacent grid edge weight \(\min(F_u,F_v)\). A maximum spanning tree preserves the maximum bottleneck value between every pair of vertices.
- **Kruskal reconstruction tree:** Process edges in descending weight. Every successful DSU merge creates an internal node weighted by that edge weight and having the two merged component roots as children. The LCA of two leaves has weight equal to their maximum bottleneck value.
- **LCA implementation:** The reconstruction tree has at most \(2HW-1\) nodes. Binary lifting answers each LCA query in \(O(\log(HW))\), using compact `array('i')` storage.
- **Complexity:** Edge construction and sorting take \(O(HW\log(HW))\). LCA preprocessing takes \(O(HW\log(HW))\) time and memory. Each query takes \(O(\log(HW))\) time.
