- **Reformulation:** For every selected pair, assign the pair to a coordinate attaining its price. Each cake in that pair contributes its value in that coordinate. Cakes assigned to the same coordinate can be paired arbitrarily, so each coordinate group must have even size.
- **Baseline assignment:** Assign every cake to one of its maximum coordinates. Any other assignment is represented by directed moves from its baseline coordinate to its assigned coordinate, with non-positive individual loss.
- **Parity structure:** The moved-coordinate graph must have odd degree exactly at coordinates whose baseline selected count is odd. Since there are only three coordinates, all relevant acyclic patterns reduce to zero moves, one edge, two edges with a common source, two edges with a common destination, or a length-two chain.
- **Sequence optimization:** Baseline groups are sorted by descending baseline value. A regular sequence selects an even or odd prefix. A one-move sequence selects a prefix together with one reassigned item, which may lie inside or outside the prefix. A two-move sequence similarly handles both moved items inside, one inside and one outside, or both outside while enforcing distinctness.
- **Combining groups:** Each sequence supplies its best value for every allowed size. Differences between consecutive sizes are marginal gains. For a fixed total number of selected cakes, the required number of two-cake extensions is obtained by taking the largest available marginals.
- **Complexity:** Each test case performs a constant number of linear scans and sorts, giving O(N log N) time and O(N) memory. The total N over all test cases is at most 10^5.
- **Edge cases:** Empty baseline groups, equal cake values, zero values, K=1, selecting all cakes, and optimal reassigned cakes outside the initial prefix are covered by the sequence boundary checks and suffix maxima.
