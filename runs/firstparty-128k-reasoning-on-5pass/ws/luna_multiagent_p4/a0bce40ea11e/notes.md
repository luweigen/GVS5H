- **Walk expansion:** Each entry of \(B^p\) is a sum over directed walks of length \(p\). A walk contributes the product of its traversed matrix entries.
- **Summing a zero variable:** For a zero position assigned \(x\in\mathbb F_p^\times\), \(\sum_x x^e\) is \(0\) unless \(p-1\mid e\), and is \(-1\) otherwise. Therefore every surviving walk contributes a common factor \((-1)^K\), while its fixed nonzero edge product remains unchanged.
- **Surviving multiplicities for odd \(p\):** Since the walk length is \(p\), each zero edge used by a surviving walk must occur exactly \(p-1\) times. Two distinct zero edges cannot both occur, because \(2(p-1)>p\). Thus surviving walks either use no zero edge or use one zero edge exactly \(p-1\) times.
- **Baseline term:** Walks using no zero entries are exactly the walks counted by \(M^p\), where \(M=A\) with its zero entries retained as zero.
- **Exceptional walks for \(p>3\):** Repeating a directed non-loop edge \(p-1\ge4\) times is impossible with only one other edge available. Hence the repeated zero edge must be a diagonal loop \(u\to u\). The remaining fixed edge must be incident to \(u\), giving the two contributions \(DA\) and \(AD\), where \(D_{uu}=1\) iff \(A_{uu}=0\).
- **Exceptional walks for \(p=3\):** The repeated zero edge occurs twice. Besides the diagonal-loop cases above, a non-loop zero edge \(u\to v\) may appear as \(u\to v\), followed by the fixed reverse edge \(v\to u\), followed by \(u\to v\). This contributes \(A_{vu}\) to position \((u,v)\) whenever \(A_{uv}=0\) and \(A_{vu}\ne0\).
- **No overlap issue:** A zero diagonal entry cannot also be a fixed nonzero diagonal edge, so the two \(DA/AD\) placements never create an invalid repeated-edge overlap. The \(p=3\) reverse-edge walks are distinct from the diagonal cases.
- **Complexity:** Matrix exponentiation costs \(O(N^3\log p)\) time and \(O(N^2)\) memory.
