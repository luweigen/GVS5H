- **Counting by middle index:** Fix index `i` as the third selected position. Exactly two selected indices must come from the prefix and two from the suffix, preserving subsequence order.
- **Center frequency cases:** If the middle value is selected `k` additional times, only `k = 1, 2, 3, 4` can be valid. With `k = 1`, all three non-center values must be pairwise distinct. With `k = 2`, the remaining two values may be equal. With `k >= 3`, all remaining values are unrestricted except for being non-center.
- **Two additional copies:** Let `lx/rx` be counts of the center value on the left/right and `nl/nr` counts of non-center values. The three distributions of the two center copies are `(2,0)`, `(0,2)`, and `(1,1)`, producing `C(lx,2)C(nr,2)`, `C(rx,2)C(nl,2)`, and `lx*rx*nl*nr`.
- **Three or four additional copies:** For three copies, distribute the center copies as `(2,1)` or `(1,2)` and select the final non-center value from the opposite side. For four copies, select two center indices from each side.
- **Exactly one additional copy:** If the extra center copy is on the left, select one non-center left value and two distinct non-center right values. For a left value with frequency `a` and right frequency `b`, the number of valid right pairs is `distinct_right_pairs - b*(nr-b)`. The symmetric formula handles an extra center copy on the right.
- **Complexity:** The frequency maps are updated incrementally. Iterating through the maps for the one-copy case gives `O(n^2)` total time and `O(n)` auxiliary space.
- **Correctness detail:** Frequencies are counted by indices, while equal values across left and right are explicitly excluded from the distinct-pair case, preventing ties with the middle value.
