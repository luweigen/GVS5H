Use the minimax-path property: for every pair of vertices, \(f(x,y)\) equals the maximum edge weight on their path in any minimum spanning tree. Process the MST edges in increasing weight, where each union creates a component containing subsets of \(A\)- and \(B\)-occurrences; at that weight, newly connected \(A\)-\(B\) pairs become available. Derive the minimum-cost perfect matching by assigning as many currently unmatched \(A\) and \(B\) occurrences as possible within each merged component, charging the union edge weight for each newly matched pair.
