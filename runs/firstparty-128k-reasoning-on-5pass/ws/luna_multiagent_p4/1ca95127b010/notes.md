- **Operation interpretation:** Each operation is exactly the reversible rewrite \(0^X1^Y \leftrightarrow 1^Y0^X\). It exchanges a block of \(X\) zeros with a neighboring block of \(Y\) ones.
- **Invariant for ones:** Number the ones from left to right. During either operation, the selected consecutive group of \(Y\) ones moves horizontally by exactly \(X\) positions, without changing their order. Therefore, the position modulo \(X\) of every numbered one is invariant.
- **Invariant for zeros:** Similarly, the selected group of \(X\) zeros moves by exactly \(Y\) positions and zero order is preserved. Hence, for every numbered zero, its position modulo \(Y\) is invariant.
- **Reachability criterion:** Two strings are equivalent exactly when their left-to-right sequences of one-position residues modulo \(X\) are equal and their left-to-right sequences of zero-position residues modulo \(Y\) are equal. Sufficiency follows by repeatedly exchanging an adjacent \(0^X1^Y\) or \(1^Y0^X\) pair to move mismatched blocks toward a canonical arrangement; the residue sequences are precisely the labels preserved by these exchanges.
- **Algorithm:** Store the residue sequences for `S`, then scan `T` and compare each character's residue against the next residue of the same character. This also checks that both strings contain equal numbers of zeros and ones.
- **Complexity:** \(O(N)\) time and \(O(N)\) auxiliary memory. The method also handles \(X=Y\), \(X=1\), \(Y=1\), and \(X+Y>N\) without special cases.
